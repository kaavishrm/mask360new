# Arete Riverscape: 5-year flood-inclusive home insurance paid by Arete

Prepared: 2026-11-03. Research only. No insurer or broker contacted. Not legal or insurance advice; a licensed broker must confirm every point before the offer is announced.

## Bottom line

1. **Yes, the IRDAI standard home policy covers flood.** Bharat Griha Raksha (BGR) lists "Storm, Cyclone, Typhoon, Tempest, Hurricane, Tornado, Tsunami, Flood and Inundation" as insured event 5, with no exclusions written against it in the IRDAI model wording. VERIFIED on the IRDAI model wording PDF.
2. **Multi-year is built in.** BGR can be bought for 1 year or more, up to 10 years. A 5-year single-premium policy is standard. Building sum insured auto-escalates 10% a year at no extra premium (cap 100% of starting SI). VERIFIED (IRDAI wording).
3. **The real risk is underwriting, not the product.** Riverscape flooded in July 2026. An insurer may (a) load the rate, (b) ask for the STFI deletion option on contents, (c) inspect plinth levels and drainage, or (d) decline. Get indications from 3 or more insurers before Arete commits to the offer publicly.
4. **River bank erosion is NOT covered.** The subsidence/landslide peril excludes loss "caused by coastal or river erosion". For a riverside site this matters: flood water damage is covered, the river eating the bank under a villa is not. VERIFIED (IRDAI wording). Ask the broker about any add-on.

## 1. What Bharat Griha Raksha covers (IRDAI model wording)

Source: IRDAI, Annexure I, Bharat Griha Raksha policy wording (64 pages). https://irdai.gov.in/documents/37343/0/Annexure-I-Bharat+Griha+Raksha.pdf/f6742d71-8194-333f-1409-2c8c652a0964?t=1633438223524

- **Product status:** IRDAI standard product for dwellings, in force from 2021-04-01, replacing the old SFSP fire policy for homes. Every non-life insurer selling home cover offers a version (filed under its own UIN). No evidence found of withdrawal or replacement as of 2026-11-03 (search only; IRDAI circular list not checked directly).
- **Insured events (Column A):** 1 Fire. 2 Explosion/implosion. 3 Lightning. 4 Earthquake, volcanic eruption, other convulsions of nature. **5 Storm, cyclone, typhoon, tempest, hurricane, tornado, tsunami, flood and inundation (the STFI group).** 6 Subsidence, landslide, rockslide (excludes coastal or river erosion, settlement of new structures, made-up ground, defective design, construction or excavation). 7 Bush/forest fire. 8 Impact damage. 9 Missile testing. 10 Riot, strike, malicious damage. 11 Terrorism (per terrorism clause, with its own excess). 12 Bursting/overflowing of water tanks and pipes. 13 Sprinkler leakage. 14 Theft within 7 days of an insured event.
- **Building cover:** the structure plus fittings and fixtures, and additional structures (garages, outhouses, compound walls, fences, gates, retaining walls, internal roads, parking). Sum insured = carpet area x declared cost of construction rate at start date. Not market value, not land.
- **Claim basis for building:** cost to repair to substantially the same condition; total loss pays full sum insured. Plus up to 5% of claim for architect/surveyor fees and up to 2% for debris removal.
- **Contents:** in-built cover for general contents of 20% of building sum insured, capped at Rs 10 lakh, when building cover is bought. Higher contents need a declared sum and extra premium. Valuables (jewellery, art) only as an add-on.
- **No underinsurance (average) penalty** on the building cover. The wording states underinsurance does not apply, provided the declared cost of construction rate was accepted by the insurer. (Commentators differ on how complete this waiver is in insurer versions. Ask.)
- **Excess/deductible:** none found in the base wording except under the terrorism clause. VERIFIED by text search of the wording.
- **Loss of rent / alternative accommodation:** optional cover inside the product.
- **Waiting period for flood:** one Business Standard article mentions a 15-day waiting period for flood claims. NOT found in the IRDAI BGR wording. Treat as UNVERIFIED; ask the broker.
- **STFI deletion option:** Universal Sompo's prospectus describes an STFI deletion clause that excludes STFI losses to contents. Arete must make sure no such clause is applied, or the flood promise breaks for contents. https://www.universalsompo.com/assets/file/bharat-griha-raksha-plus/Prospectus-bharat-griha-raksha-plus.pdf

## 2. Long-term options

- **BGR:** 1 to 10 years. "You can buy the policy for one year or more than one year but the policy duration cannot exceed 10 years." (IRDAI wording, also repeated in New India, Tata AIG and Universal Sompo prospectuses.) VERIFIED.
- **Escalation:** for policies longer than 1 year, building SI rises 10% per annum on each anniversary without extra premium, up to 100% of starting SI. Over 5 years a Rs 1.2 Cr SI becomes Rs 1.68 Cr cover in year 5 (simple 10% of original per year). VERIFIED.
- **Total-loss cash settlement on a long-term policy:** insurer cancels the remainder and refunds unexpired-year premium after grossing up any long-term discount. VERIFIED.
- **HDFC ERGO Bharat Griha Raksha Plus, Long Term** (UIN IRDAN146RPPR0070V01202425): marketing brochure states maximum policy period 30 years and contents tenure fixed at 5 years. Prospectus lists online, employee and loyalty discounts of 5% each. https://www.hdfcergo.com/docs/default-source/downloads/prospectus/home/bgr-lt-p.pdf
- **Long-term discount rates:** NOT published in any prospectus reached (New India, Tata AIG, Universal Sompo, HDFC ERGO). Insurers reference "long term discount, if any" without numbers.

## 5. Can Arete buy it in the buyer's name, or as a group policy?

- **Who can buy (IRDAI wording):** "You can purchase the Home Building Cover if You own or are a tenant of the Home Building and You are liable for insurance." So the insured must have an insurable interest: the owner. Nothing in the wording stops a third party (Arete) from paying the premium for the owner.
- **Practical structure (most likely to work, to be confirmed by broker):** Arete pays a 5-year single premium for a BGR policy issued in the buyer's name, starting at possession or sale-deed registration (when the buyer becomes owner). If the buyer has a home loan, the policy carries the "Agreed Bank Clause" naming the lender (provided for in the wording).
- **Before possession:** the villa is Arete's asset. It should be under Arete's own project cover (contractor's all risk / builder's risk, or a fire and perils policy). A buyer-named BGR typically starts only after completion and transfer.
- **Group policy:** no IRDAI guideline for a non-employer group dwelling policy was found. IRDAI group framework distinguishes employer-employee and non-employer-employee groups, and commentators note "group" is not defined for general insurance. A master policy in Arete's name covering buyer-owned villas raises insurable-interest questions. UNVERIFIED either way; ask the broker whether an insurer will issue (a) one multi-location policy with each owner as insured and Arete as premium payer, or (b) 20 individual policies under one quote. Option (b) is the safe default.
- **Alternative structures to price:** (i) Arete reimburses the buyer's premium against a receipt (simplest, buyer chooses insurer), (ii) credit note on the sale price equal to 5 years of premium. Both shift the underwriting risk to the buyer and weaken the marketing promise. The tax and GST treatment of a developer-paid policy needs Arete's CA.
- **Marketing compliance:** if the offer is advertised, the commitment should sit in the agreement for sale. RERA treats advertised promises as binding. Insurance offers in ads may need to avoid naming an insurer without its consent.

## 6. Questions to put to a broker (IRDAI-licensed, with a corporate desk)

1. Will you place a 5-year BGR (or BGR Plus Long Term) for 20 villas at Riverscape, Tukwada, Vapi, given the July 2026 flood? Which insurers will quote?
2. What rate per mille per year, and what long-term discount for 5 years single premium? Any flood loading?
3. Will any insurer apply STFI deletion, a flood sub-limit, a flood excess, or a waiting period? Get it in writing: no STFI deletion on building or contents.
4. Will the insurer survey the site? What do they need: flood history, plinth height above HFL (highest flood level), drainage, embankment or retaining wall, distance to the Kolak river bank?
5. River erosion is excluded under subsidence. Is any add-on or endorsement available?
6. Can the policy be issued in the buyer's name with Arete as payer? Can it start on possession date, which may be months after booking? Can the premium be locked at booking?
7. Agreed Bank Clause for buyers with loans: confirm process.
8. Cost of construction rate per sq m the insurer will accept for Riverscape villas (drives the sum insured). Is Rs 1.2 to 1.5 Cr per villa supportable on carpet area x rate?
9. Is the in-built 20% contents cover (cap Rs 10 lakh) included in the building premium, or priced separately?
10. Terrorism cover: include or exclude? Loss of rent / alternative accommodation: price it (a flooded family needs a place to stay).
11. Claims service: nearest surveyor panel and branch to Vapi, average flood claim settlement time in Valsad district in 2026.
12. Transferability: what happens if the buyer resells within 5 years?
13. Cancellation and refund rules if a sale cancels before the policy starts.
14. Brokerage: who pays, and is it disclosed?

## 7. Cost estimate for 20 villas: ESTIMATE ONLY

**Assumptions (all estimates, not quotes):**
- 20 villas. Building sum insured Rs 1.2 Cr to Rs 1.5 Cr each (cost of construction basis).
- 5-year term, single premium. No long-term discount assumed (conservative). Free 10% annual escalation applies.
- In-built contents (20%, cap Rs 10 lakh) assumed included in building premium. Not verified.
- GST at 18% on premium.
- Terrorism and loss-of-rent excluded.
- Rate scenarios per mille per year (Rs per Rs 1,000 SI): Low 0.15, Base 0.26 (Royal Sundaram chart figure seen in search, unverified), High 0.40, Stress 1.00 (hypothetical flood loading after the July 2026 event).

Working (Base, Rs 1.2 Cr): Rs 1,20,00,000 x 0.26 / 1,000 = Rs 3,120 per year. x 5 = Rs 15,600. + 18% GST = Rs 18,408.

**Read-across for the pitch:** even at the stress rate, the worst case per villa is Rs 88,500 for 5 years, about 0.3% of a Rs 3 Cr villa. All 20 villas at the stress rate cost under Rs 18 lakh, about 6% of the price of one villa. The offer is cheap relative to its signalling value. The binding constraint is whether an insurer will write it at all, not the cost.
