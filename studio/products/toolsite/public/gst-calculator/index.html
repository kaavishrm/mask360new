The two calculations

Adding GST to a price is straightforward: multiply by the rate. Removing GST from an inclusive price trips people up because you divide, not subtract.

Add GST: GST = Price × Rate ÷ 100 Remove GST: Base = Inclusive price ÷ (1 + Rate ÷ 100)

Example: a bill of ₹11,800 inclusive of 18% GST has a taxable value of ₹10,000 and GST of ₹1,800. Subtracting 18% of ₹11,800 would give the wrong answer.

CGST, SGST and IGST

When buyer and seller are in the same state, the GST is split equally between the Centre (CGST) and the state (SGST). An 18% supply becomes 9% CGST plus 9% SGST. When the supply crosses a state border, the whole amount is charged as IGST. The total tax is the same either way; only who collects it changes.

GST rates in India

The main slabs are 5%, 12%, 18% and 28%, with 0.25% for rough diamonds and 3% for gold and jewellery. Essentials such as unbranded food grains are nil-rated. Restaurant services are usually 5% without input credit. Most services and manufactured goods sit at 18%. Rates are set by the GST Council and change from time to time, so check the current schedule for anything borderline.

Questions people ask

How do I remove 18% GST from an amount?

Divide the inclusive amount by 1.18. For ₹11,800 the base is ₹10,000 and the GST is ₹1,800.

When is IGST charged instead of CGST and SGST?

IGST applies when the place of supply is in a different state from the supplier, and on imports. Within a state, the tax is split equally into CGST and SGST.

Is the GST calculation different for services?

No. The arithmetic is the same for goods and services. Only the applicable rate differs.
