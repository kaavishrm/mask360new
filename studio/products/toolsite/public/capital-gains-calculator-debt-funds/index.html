How debt and other non-equity funds are taxed in FY 2026-27

Mutual fund units that are not equity-oriented fall into two groups, and the date you bought them matters as much as how long you held them. A specified mutual fund is one that invests more than 65% of its money in debt and money market instruments, or a fund of funds that puts 65% or more into such funds: liquid, overnight, money market, corporate bond, gilt, banking and PSU, credit risk and most target maturity funds. Units of these bought on or after 1 April 2023 are, by Section 76 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (the old Section 50AA), short term whatever the holding period, so the gain is added to your income and taxed at your slab. Units of the same funds bought before 1 April 2023 escape that rule: held for more than 24 months they are long term and taxed at 12.5% without indexation under Section 197, and held for 24 months or less they are short term at slab rates.

Gain = (redemption NAV minus purchase NAV) × units, less exit load Specified fund, bought on or after 1 April 2023: slab rate, always Otherwise: slab rate if held 24 months or less, 12.5% if held longer

Funds outside the 65% debt rule

Gold funds and gold ETFs, international and overseas feeder funds, funds of funds holding under 65% in debt, and hybrid funds with between 35% and 65% in Indian equity are not specified funds and are not equity-oriented either. For them the only test is time: more than 24 months is long term at 12.5%, and anything shorter is short term at slab rates, regardless of when they were bought. Fund-house tax reckoners for 2026-27 list these categories the same way, and a scheme’s own factsheet states its asset mix, which is averaged over the year for this purpose.

Why the 24 months and no indexation

Before 23 July 2024 these units needed 36 months to be long term and the long-term rate was 20% with indexation. Budget 2024 cut the holding period to 24 months and the rate to 12.5%, and removed indexation for everything except the transitional choice on land and buildings. A debt fund unit bought in 2019 and sold today therefore pays 12.5% on the whole nominal gain. For many investors in the 30% bracket that is still well below slab, which is why older debt fund holdings remain attractive to keep rather than churn.

Short-term gains at slab

Because a short-term gain is ordinary income, the tax it causes depends on everything else in your return: the new regime rebate that makes income up to ₹12 lakh tax free, the marginal relief just above it, and surcharge above ₹50 lakh. The calculator therefore computes the tax on your other income with and without the gain and shows the difference. Someone with ₹12 lakh of salary and a ₹7,000 gain pays ₹7,280, which is the gain plus cess, because marginal relief caps the tax at the income over ₹12 lakh; someone with ₹5 lakh of salary and the same gain pays nothing.

Losses and set-off

A short-term loss can be set off against any capital gain in the year. A long-term loss can be set off only against long-term gains, including those on shares and property. Both carry forward for eight years if the return is filed by the due date. Systematic withdrawal plans and partial redemptions use first in, first out, so the oldest units go first, and for a holding straddling 1 April 2023 that means the pre-cut-off units with their better treatment are consumed before the newer ones.

Not covered: market linked debentures and unlisted bonds (also short term under Section 76), listed bonds and debentures (12 months, 12.5%), dividends from the IDCW option (slab rate, TDS above ₹10,000), non-resident rates and treaty relief, and surcharge. Questions people ask

I started a debt fund SIP in 2021 and still invest. How are the units taxed?

Instalment by instalment. Units bought before 1 April 2023 are long term after 24 months at 12.5%; units bought from that date are short term at slab rates however long you hold them. Redemptions go out first in, first out, so the 2021 and 2022 units leave first.

Is a gold ETF a specified fund?

No. It holds gold, not debt, so it is outside the 65% debt test. Units held for more than 24 months are long term at 12.5% whenever they were bought; 24 months or less is short term at slab.

Do I get indexation on units bought in 2020?

Not for a sale in FY 2026-27. Indexation on fund units ended for transfers on or after 23 July 2024. The 2020 units are long term at 12.5% on the nominal gain.

Does the ₹1.25 lakh exemption apply?

No. That exemption is only for listed shares and equity-oriented funds under Section 198. Debt and other non-equity funds get no fixed exemption, only the unused basic exemption limit for residents.

What about an arbitrage or balanced advantage fund?

If it keeps 65% or more in Indian equity it is equity-oriented and belongs in the shares calculator: 20% short term and 12.5% long term after 12 months. If it holds between 35% and 65% in equity, use this calculator with the second fund type.
