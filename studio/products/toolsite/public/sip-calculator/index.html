How SIP returns are calculated

Each monthly instalment compounds for the months remaining until the end of the period. The standard formula for a level SIP is:

FV = A × ((1 + i)^n − 1) ÷ i × (1 + i) A = monthly amount, i = annual return ÷ 12, n = total months

This calculator runs the month-by-month version of the same arithmetic so that step-ups can be applied at each anniversary.

Why step-up matters

A ₹10,000 SIP for 15 years at 12% grows to roughly ₹50 lakh. Raising it by 10% every year, in line with a salary hike, takes the same plan to about ₹90 lakh while the extra outgo is spread over the years when you can afford it. Most fund houses let you set the step-up at the time of starting the SIP.

What return should you assume?

Long-run returns of diversified Indian equity funds have historically been in the 11% to 14% range, but any single ten-year window can be far better or worse. Use 10% to 12% for planning and treat anything higher as optimistic. Debt funds and hybrid funds sit lower, around 6% to 9%.

Tax on SIP gains

Each SIP instalment is a separate purchase with its own holding period. Equity fund units held longer than a year attract long-term capital gains tax above the annual exemption; units sold within a year are taxed at the short-term rate. Debt funds are taxed at your slab rate regardless of holding period for investments made after April 2023.

Questions people ask

Is the return shown guaranteed?

No. The calculator applies the return you type in, evenly, every month. Market-linked funds do not behave that way. The number is a planning estimate, not a promise.

When does the step-up apply?

At the start of each new year of the SIP. A 10% step-up on ₹10,000 makes the second year ₹11,000 a month, the third ₹12,100, and so on.

SIP or lumpsum, which is better?

If you already have the money, investing it at once has the higher expected return because it is invested longer. SIP suits people investing from monthly income and smooths out the entry price. Use the two modes above to compare the same total amount.
