How property gains are taxed in FY 2026-27

A house, flat, plot or commercial building is a long-term capital asset once it has been held for more than 24 months; sold sooner, the gain is short term and simply joins your other income at slab rates. Long-term gains are taxed under Section 197 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, which replaced Section 112 of the 1961 Act on 1 April 2026, at 12.5% without indexation, plus 4% cess. That flat rate came in with Budget 2024 on 23 July 2024, and it came with a transitional choice: a resident individual or Hindu undivided family selling land or a building acquired before 23 July 2024 may instead have the tax worked out at 20% on the gain after indexation, and pays whichever is lower. The Act words this as ignoring the excess of the 12.5% tax over the 20% indexed tax, which is what the calculator does. Anyone who bought on or after that date, and every non-resident, company or firm, has only the 12.5% rate.

Indexed cost = cost × CII of the year of sale (384 for 2026-27) ÷ CII of the year of purchase (or 100 for 2001-02 if earlier) Tax = lower of 12.5% × (gain without indexation) and 20% × (gain with indexation), plus 4% cess

The index

The Cost Inflation Index runs from 100 in 2001-02 to 384 in 2026-27, notified by the CBDT on 15 July 2026. A flat bought for ₹40 lakh in June 2015 (index 254) and sold in October 2026 has an indexed cost of ₹40,00,000 × 384 ÷ 254, about ₹60.5 lakh. Sold for ₹90 lakh, the gain is ₹50 lakh without indexation, taxed at 12.5% for ₹6,25,000, or ₹29.5 lakh with indexation, taxed at 20% for ₹5,90,551. The second is lower, so that is the tax, plus cess. For a property that has roughly doubled in a decade the two come out close; for one bought long ago and held through years of high inflation, indexation usually wins, and for recent purchases that rose fast, the flat rate wins. The cost of improvement is indexed from the year the work was done. Property bought before 1 April 2001 may take its fair market value on that date as the cost, capped at the stamp duty value then.

Stamp duty value

If the stamp duty value (the circle rate value the registrar uses) is higher than the price you actually received, the law takes the stamp duty value as the sale consideration, unless it is within 110% of the price. Enter it and the calculator applies the rule. A seller who believes the circle rate overstates the property can ask the assessing officer to refer it for valuation.

Reinvestment relief

Three exemptions can wipe out the tax on a long-term gain. Section 82 (the old Section 54) exempts the gain on a residential house to the extent you buy another residential house in India within one year before or two years after the sale, or build one within three years; cost above ₹10 crore is ignored, and a gain of up to ₹2 crore may once in a lifetime be put into two houses. Section 86 (the old 54F) does the same for any other long-term asset, including land, but proportionately: the exempt gain is the gain multiplied by the cost of the new house and divided by the net sale consideration, and the seller must not own more than one other house. Section 85 (the old 54EC) exempts gain from land or a building invested within six months in notified five-year bonds, up to ₹50 lakh in the year and the next together. The calculator applies the house exemption first and then the bonds, and uses both against the indexed gain too when comparing the two rates.

Short-term sales

Sell within 24 months and none of this applies: the gain is added to your income and taxed at your slab, with the rebate, surcharge and cess that your total income attracts. The calculator shows the extra tax the gain causes across the year, so a seller with ₹12 lakh of salary who makes a ₹50 lakh short-term gain sees the tax on ₹62 lakh less the tax on ₹12 lakh.

Not covered: TDS of 1% on purchases above ₹50 lakh and the higher rates for non-resident sellers, joint ownership splits, inherited property (the previous owner’s holding period and cost carry over), agricultural land outside municipal limits, and surcharge above ₹50 lakh of income. Figures were verified on 24 November 2026 against reproductions of Sections 2(101), 72, 76, 78, 82, 85, 86 and 197 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 and the CBDT index notification.

Questions people ask

Can I still choose indexation?

Only if you are a resident individual or HUF, the asset is land or a building, and you bought it before 23 July 2024. Then the tax is the lower of 12.5% without indexation and 20% with it. For purchases on or after that date, and for all other sellers, the rate is 12.5% without indexation.

Does indexation also reduce a loss or the exemption I can claim?

No. The Act only ignores the excess tax; the gain itself, and any loss, is computed without indexation. The calculator applies the reinvestment exemptions to the indexed gain as well so that the comparison is like for like.

I inherited the flat. What purchase date and cost do I use?

The date and cost at which the person you inherited from acquired it, and the indexation runs from their year of purchase (or 2001-02 if earlier). Enter those figures rather than the date of inheritance.

What if I have not bought the new house by the time I file?

Deposit the unused gain in a Capital Gains Account Scheme account at a bank before the return due date and withdraw it to buy or build within the time allowed. Money left unused after three years is taxed as a gain of that year.

Is the 1% TDS the buyer deducted my tax?

No. It is an advance against your liability, credited when you file. The tax shown here is the full amount; the TDS reduces what you still have to pay.
