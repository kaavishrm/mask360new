Security Deposit Return Deadline Calculator: the date your landlord must return it, by state, with the statute

Choose the state, enter when the lease ended and when you handed back the keys, and the calculator works out the deadline from the rule in that state’s statute. Each answer names the section and the date it was checked against the legislature’s text.

How deposit deadlines work

Nearly every state with a deposit statute gives the landlord a fixed number of days to do two things: return the deposit, and explain in writing anything kept back. The number ranges from 14 days in New York and 14 business days in Arizona to 30 in most states, one calendar month in Colorado, and 45 in Indiana, Maryland and Virginia. Tennessee is the exception in this list: its statute sets no number of days for the refund, only a notice rule and a sixty-day window after the notice. What differs more than the number is the event that starts the clock. Some states count from the day the tenant vacates, some from the day the rental agreement ends, and many from the later of the two, so a tenant who stays a week past the lease end or leaves a week early moves the deadline. A forwarding address is a condition in some states and merely practical in others. The calculator applies the rule as the statute states it and names the section, so you can read it yourself.

Deadline = the event the statute names (vacating, termination, or the later of termination and possession) + the statute’s days, business days or month

Two-sided deadlines

In a few states the tenant has a deadline too. Michigan requires the forwarding address in writing within four days of moving out and a detailed written response within seven days of the landlord’s notice of damages, and a tenant who skips either step loses part of the statute’s protection. Maryland gives a right to attend the move-out inspection only to a tenant who sent certified-mail notice at least fifteen days before moving. Arizona starts the fourteen business days only once the tenant has demanded the deposit, so demand it in writing on the day you hand back the keys. More states are added only after the same check; the aim is all fifty, in order of renter population. A state that is not in the list is not covered yet, and a rule from a blog should not be relied on in its place.

What to do before the deadline

Give a forwarding address in writing, with proof of delivery, as soon as you know it; in Ohio it is a condition of recovering damages, in Florida the lease disclosure requires it, and everywhere it removes the landlord’s easiest excuse. Photograph the unit on the day you hand back the keys. Keep the lease, the move-in checklist and the key-return receipt together. If the deadline passes, write a short letter citing the section shown above and asking for the deposit within a stated number of days; most disputes end there, and the rest go to small claims court, where the statute’s penalty provisions apply.

What this calculator does not cover

City ordinances that add rules on top of state law, such as Chicago’s and several California cities’, interest on deposits where a state or city requires it (Maryland, Massachusetts and New Jersey among them), commercial leases, longer periods a lease may set where a statute allows it (Colorado, up to sixty days), and the separate question of what a landlord may lawfully deduct. It does not adjust for weekends and holidays except where the statute itself counts business days, because the other statutes mostly do not say, and a court may or may not extend the date.

Questions people ask

Does the deadline start when I move out or when the lease ends?

It depends on the state and the calculator applies each state’s wording. California, Georgia, New York and Texas count from vacating or surrender. Florida and New Jersey count from termination of the lease. Ohio, North Carolina, Illinois, Virginia, Indiana, Colorado, Washington and Arizona count from the later of termination and delivery of possession (Arizona adds your demand), Massachusetts, Michigan, Maryland and Missouri from the end of the tenancy or occupancy, and Pennsylvania from whichever comes first.

Why does Tennessee show no deadline?

Because the statute does not set one. Tennessee Code 66-28-301 requires the landlord to send notice of the refund due to your last known address and lets the landlord keep the deposit if you do not respond within sixty days of that notice. It does cap when damage can be charged: it must have been discovered within thirty days of your vacating, or within seven days of a new tenant moving in if that is sooner, and the calculator shows that date.

What if I never gave a forwarding address?

The landlord still owes the deposit, but in some states your remedies shrink. In Ohio a tenant who gave no address in writing cannot recover the statutory damages or fees. In Michigan a tenant who gives no address within four days loses the right to the notice of damages. In Indiana and Texas the landlord’s duty does not arise until the address is given. In North Carolina the landlord holds the balance for at least six months for you to collect, and in Virginia it eventually goes to the state as unclaimed property. Give the address in writing in every state.

The landlord sent a list of deductions but no money. Is that compliance?

Only if the deductions use up the whole deposit and the list arrived in time. In New York the statement and any balance must arrive together within fourteen days. In Florida a landlord who wants to keep anything must send the claim notice within 30 days, and you then have 15 days to object.

Can I withhold the last month’s rent instead?

Most states say no and some, such as Texas, make it a separate offence with its own penalty. Pay the rent, hand back the keys, give your address, and claim the deposit under the statute.
