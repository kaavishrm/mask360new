## The tea

The Pant Project, the Mumbai menswear brand that brothers Dhruv and Udit Toshniwal started in 2020 to sell custom-made trousers online, has closed a Series B of INR 109 crore, about USD 11.5 million. Mirae Asset Venture Investments (India) leads. Sorin Investments, which led the USD 4.25 million Series A in June 2024 after four bootstrapped years, comes back in, alongside family offices and angels including Anisha Saraf, B&K Securities, MGA Ventures and B2V Ventures. Alteria Capital adds venture debt of an undisclosed size. IndigoEdge banked the deal. Total funding now tops USD 15.75 million, per [Inc42](https://inc42.com/?p=576745) and [Entrepreneur India](https://india.entrepreneur.com/?p=100607), both on 19 October.

The money is for rooms. The brand runs 25 exclusive stores and sells through its own site, Myntra and Amazon. The stated target is more than 150 stores in two to three years, plus a wider range: jeans, athleisure and casualwear on top of 300-plus bottomwear SKUs, per [Inc42](https://inc42.com/?p=576745) and [Indian Retailer](https://indianretailer.com/news/pant-project-raises-rs-109-cr-expand-150-stores).

Dhruv Toshniwal, founder and CEO, to Inc42: "That foundation gave us the data, the product knowledge and the brand equity to now go deep offline." Puneet Kumar, managing director at Mirae Asset Venture Fund, to [Entrepreneur India](https://india.entrepreneur.com/?p=100607): "As premium menswear becomes one of India's most compelling consumer opportunities, The Pant Project is well positioned to lead it." Udit Toshniwal, founder and creative director, in the same piece: "Indian men deserve better-fitting bottomwear."

## The receipts

[Open magazine](https://openthemagazine.com/business/the-pant-project-wants-150-stores-will-the-numbers-fit) did the arithmetic the release did not. Going from 25 stores to 150 means 125 net additions, which over two to three years is one opening every six to nine days. In a March interview Dhruv Toshniwal put a store at 700 to 800 sq ft and INR 30 to 40 lakh to build, which prices 125 of them at INR 37.5 to 50 crore, a third to half of the round, before stock, staff or marketing.

The revenue line, as the company disclosed in September: INR 80 crore in FY26, with INR 160 crore projected for FY27. Sorin's Mandar Dandekar says revenue is up more than fivefold since the Series A. The founder has said every existing store is profitable and that company-level EBITDA breakeven sits near INR 150 to 160 crore of revenue. Offline bills average about INR 6,000 against INR 3,000 to 4,000 online, per [Open](https://openthemagazine.com/business/the-pant-project-wants-150-stores-will-the-numbers-fit) and [Inc42](https://inc42.com/?p=576745).

So the FY27 projection and the breakeven number are the same number. Land 160 and the model closes. Miss it and 125 new stores are a cost line with a lease.

## The read

INR 109 crore buys the stores. It does not buy the INR 160 crore. The first 25 stores opened over roughly two years. The next 125 open at a pace of one a week, many of them in cities and malls the brand has not tested. Offline bills running 1.5 to 2 times online bills is the thesis, and for a fit-led product it is a good one: nobody buys a third pair of custom trousers from a brand whose first pair did not fit. The test is whether store 90 bills like store 9. The number to watch in FY27 is not the ribbon count. It is whether revenue lands at 160 with every store still in the black, which is the claim the founder made in March and the one Mirae Asset just paid for.
