## The tea

The Advertising Standards Council of India has published a report called A Pill for Thought: Review of Health and Wellness Claims in Nutraceutical Advertising. It is a suo motu sweep of nutraceutical ads published between July and September 2026, and it came out on 28 October, per [Indian Television](https://indiantelevision.com/mam/60-of-61-nutraceutical-ads-breach-asci-code-report-finds/) and [Mediainfoline](https://www.mediainfoline.com/advertising/ascis-a-pill-for-thought-report-flags-gaps-in-evidence-backing-nutraceutical-advertising-claims). The headline number is the whole story. 61 advertisements evaluated. One still in process. All 60 completed cases found in violation of the ASCI Code.

The claims the report quotes read like a list of everything the category has been told not to say. "Lose 6 kgs in 60 days." "Your thigh fat will be gone in 12 weeks." "Your skin, but 7 years younger." "300% stronger skin defence against UV damage." A product that would "cleanse" smokers' lungs, another to "reverse PCOD", and ads addressing asthma, COPD, blood pressure, cholesterol and Type 2 diabetes, per Indian Television. Glutathione ads tied sun protection and de-tanning to visuals of darker skin turning lighter, which the report says risks reinforcing a preference for lighter skin. Comparative words like "faster" and "more effective" turned up with no benchmark, and before-and-after visuals did the work the evidence could not.

The advertisers' response is the second number. 29 did not contest and modified or withdrew. 31 contested the objections and, per ASCI, none produced adequate substantiation, per [MediaBrief](https://mediabrief.com/asci-flags-60-nutraceutical-ads-for-misleading-claims/). Manisha Kapoor, CEO and Secretary General of ASCI, draws the line the report is built on: "Research on an individual ingredient cannot automatically be used to substantiate the efficacy of a finished product." Her other point, via [MxMIndia](https://www.mxmindia.com/advertising/asci-report-flags-evidence-gaps-in-nutraceutical-advertising-claims) on 30 October, is the one brand managers should pin above the desk: substantiation should come before the advertisement, not after a complaint.

Then the money. Grand View Research values India's nutraceuticals market at USD 38.77 billion in 2025, a figure the trade reports repeat. ASCI is a self-regulatory body and cannot fine. The Central Consumer Protection Authority can order a misleading ad discontinued and impose penalties with ceilings of INR 10 lakh, rising to INR 50 lakh for subsequent contraventions, per [Open](https://openthemagazine.com/branding-marketing-and-advertising/misleading-health-ads-the-watchdog-barks-who-bites) on 29 October. Open also notes the report does not say whether any of the 60 cases led to a referral, an investigation or a refund. Nor does any trade report name a single advertiser.

## The receipts

The 61, 60, 29 and 31 figures are in Indian Television, Mediainfoline, MediaBrief and MxMIndia, all citing the ASCI report. The quoted claims are in Indian Television and Mediainfoline. Kapoor's quotes are in all four. The CCPA penalty ceilings and the point about referrals are in Open. [Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/10/223-asci-flags-60-misleading-nutraceutical-ads/), [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/india-news/nutraceutical-ads-lack-scientific-proof-for-health-claims-asci-report-126102801440_1.html) and [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/advertising-news/weight-loss-promises-anti-ageing-claims-under-asci-scanner-in-nutraceutical-ads-159589.html) carried the study the same week.

## The read

A 100 percent failure rate is not a few bad actors. It is how the category advertises. When 31 companies choose to argue the point and none of them can show product-specific evidence, the problem is not a rogue copywriter, it is a brief that starts with the promise and goes looking for a study afterwards.

ASCI has done the useful part and published the pattern. It has not published the names, and it cannot publish a fine. That gap, between a finding and a consequence, is where next quarter's claims will be written. Until a CCPA penalty lands on a nutraceutical brand in public, "6 kgs in 60 days" is a cost of doing business, and so far the cost is zero.
