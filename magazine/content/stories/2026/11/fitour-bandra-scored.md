This is a scorecard of Fitour as it has been presented in public: Aspect Hospitality's launch release, The Nod's review, Esquire India's hotlist entry, and the venue's own published prices. Mask360 has not eaten there. The lines below score the concept, the room, the menu and the price position, not a plate.

## The idea

Fitour opened on 4 October in the basement of House of Akina on Turner Road, Bandra West, [per Restaurant India](https://www.restaurantindia.in/news/aspect-hospitality-opens-fitour-in-bandra-with-14-seat-indian-dining-format.n17383): 14 seats, one seating a service, a tasting menu that chef Ashwin Singh builds as 18 "verses" around Indian ingredients, memory and place, with soundscape, lighting and fragrance shifting between chapters. The name fuses fitoor, passion, with tour; Singh picked it at seven after a Vikas Khanna cookbook, [per The Nod's review of 14 October](https://thenodmag.com/content/in-mumbai-s-newest-xs-eatery-last-summer-s-mangoes-get-the-spotlight). Courses land three or four at a time with no introduction and you guess before he tells. Pani puri hidden under marigold petals with karela, amla and mint chocolate. Litti chokha shaped like a Jim Jam biscuit with a mustard filling. A central wall of pickle jars for a grandmother from Rajasthan who taught him to pickle as a boy in Meerut. The idea is a memory palace with a kitchen in it, and every piece of coverage says the device holds. Idea: 8.5.

## The craft

Singh cooked at Gaggan in Bangkok and Masque in Mumbai. The Nod's Sheree Gomes Gupta reports a kitchen that makes its own garam, chaat and meat masalas and buys in only salt, turmeric, red chilli and coriander; nothing frozen; dishes that take two to three days; a tomato sol kadi made from fermented tomato water clarified overnight; a pork course of three cuts, each cooked 12 to 14 hours; jackfruit cooked five times for the vegetarian rice course. Her pick of the night is the shirvalya and mango mahura rasam with rice noodles, peas and seafood. Head mixologist Shrineet Sonsurkar, ex Naar and Masque, runs the pairings under instruction not to upstage the food. Designer Mahek Kapoor's room has handmade terracotta tiles, niches lined with woven fibre and four arches of hand-drawn landscapes behind rippled glass. The one flat note in the write-up is a Mangalore bun whose sea snails the reviewer says she would have hesitated over had she known. Craft: 8.5.

## The fit

Bandra already has Papa's, Hunger Inc's 12-seat chef's counter above Veronica's, and Fitour arrives as the second serious Indian tasting counter in the same postcode. The site is the old Privé, rebuilt four times, once after a monsoon damaged the floor and once because the ceiling was too low, per The Nod. Thursday to Sunday, one sitting at 7:30pm, service done around 10:30. Aspect Hospitality's Hitesh Keswani signed off the design from ChatGPT renders Singh sent him. Fourteen seats four nights a week is a narrow door, and an unmarked basement door with no signage and no walk-ins, as [Esquire India](https://www.esquireindia.co.in/food-and-drinks/what-to-eat/esquire-india-october-hotlist-new-restaurants-and-bars-across-india-fitour-nām-chor-bizarre-and-more) and [The Nod's roundup](https://thenodmag.com/content/india-new-restaurants-and-bars-october-2026) both note, means the neighbourhood finds it by word of mouth or not at all. Fit: 7.5.

## The courage

INR 8,245 per person plus taxes before a drink, and the pairings are not shy: cocktails INR 5,495, international wine INR 6,495, Indian wine INR 5,145, per Restaurant India. No walk-ins. Dishes explained only after you have eaten them, which is the opposite of the Papa's model and a bet on the diner's patience. A menu that flips every three months, with last summer's raw mangoes bottled in April to carry into the January menu. Sea snails in a bun with no warning. A chef who says, in the release, that he wanted an experience that extends beyond the food itself, and then prices it like one. Courage: 9.
