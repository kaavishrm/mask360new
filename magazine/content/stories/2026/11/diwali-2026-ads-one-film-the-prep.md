## The thesis

For a decade the Diwali ad was the homecoming. A train, a doorbell, a parent pretending not to cry. [Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/topical-spot/memorable-diwali-campaigns-redefined-the-festive-narrative-10564668) clocked the shift last year: brands were moving the story back from the day itself to the rituals before it. In 2026 the shift is complete, and we have sat through enough festive breaks to say so with a straight face. The three most-discussed festive films of October are all set in the week before Diwali, all built on chores, and all end with the product making the chore bearable. The homecoming has been replaced by the prep montage, and the prep montage is now exactly as interchangeable as the thing it replaced. [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/festive-season-news/who-owns-diwali-the-brands-the-culture-or-the-consumer-159612.html) has already asked who owns Diwali when every brand reaches for the same symbols. This year the symbol is a cleaning cloth.

## One: Asian Paints sang it

Taiyyari Shuru, Tyohaar Shuru, by Ogilvy, reported by [Indiantelevision](https://indiantelevision.com/mam/asian-paints-unveils-diwali-campaign-celebrating-the-joy-of-home-makeovers/) on 19 October, is a musical. Cleaning, decorating and painting become a song, and cousins, relatives and friends join in as the house gets its makeover. Amit Syngle, Asian Paints' CEO and MD, frames Diwali as an emotion that builds well before the festival. Sukesh Nayak, Ogilvy India's Group Chief Creative Officer, says the chaos of preparation has its own rhythm. Both are right, and the film is handsome. The [Ads of the World listing](https://www.adsoftheworld.com/campaigns/taiyyari-shuru-tyohaar-shuru) sums up the closing message: celebrate the little preparations that bring homes closer, one wall at a time. The wall is the product. The prep is the plot. Hold that structure in your head, because you are about to see it twice more.

## Two: Spotify cast it

[MediaInfoline](https://www.mediainfoline.com/advertising/spotifys-diwali-campaign-turns-festive-chores-into-family-fun) reported Spotify's three one-minute films with the cast of TVF's Gullak on 30 October. The Mishra family makes laddoos to Taambdi Chaamdi, does the rangoli to Chaiya Chaiya and untangles the lights to Badan Pe Sitare. In each film the family bickers through the chore until someone plays music, and the chore turns into a good time. The tagline is Diwali ki taiyaari fun banao, Spotify chalao, a festive edit of the brand's running line. Shipra Srivastava, Spotify India's Head of Marketing, says the Mishras felt like a natural fit because they are a family viewers recognise. True, and the casting is the best decision in any of the three. Now look at the structure. Chore, friction, product, relief. It is the Asian Paints film with a playlist where the paint tin was.

## Three: Nutella audited it

Reported by [MediaInfoline](https://www.mediainfoline.com/advertising/nutella-and-ranveer-singh-come-together-to-add-more-deliciousness-to-diwali) on 22 October. Ranveer Singh gets the house ready, straightens a cushion, adjusts a light, stops to smell a bowl of mogra, then checks the laid table and notices the Nutella is missing. He puts the jar at the head of the table and the guests chorus that Diwali tastes better with Nutella. Zoher Kapuswala, Ferrero India's Marketing Head, says the actor's energy makes the jar feel like an effortless part of the celebration. It is a one-joke film and the joke is a product check. It is also, beat for beat, the same arc: the prep, the small friction, the product closing the gap. Three brands, three categories, one storyboard. We checked the dates twice, hoping one of them had gone first by a year. All three are October.

## The exceptions

The fix is not theoretical, and the same agency shipped it in the same month. Ogilvy India's Cadbury Celebrations film, reported by [MediaInfoline](https://www.mediainfoline.com/advertising/this-diwali-cadbury-celebrations-nudges-you-to-celebrate-the-sweetness-behind-every-nok-jhok) on 12 October, leaves the house entirely and goes next door: parking fights, shared walls, pets, late parties, compound cricket, and a box that turns the tiff into thoda sa pyaar. A ritual nobody had used, a real conflict, the product as the gesture that ends it. Supertails went further. [Medianews4u](https://www.medianews4u.com/supertails-puts-humans-under-the-table-to-show-how-pets-experience-diwali/) reported No Bang Diwali on 23 October: a family does mithai, makeup and a card game under a table, and the on-screen line, per [Open](https://openthemagazine.com/branding-marketing-and-advertising/supertails-diwali-ad-finds-the-family-under-the-table), asks why the pets should celebrate like that if you would not. One film changed the room, the other changed the point of view. Both are more memorable than any montage because they contain an argument, and an argument is the one thing you cannot swap a logo into.

## The read

The prep is this year's homecoming: warm, true and interchangeable. Swap the logos across the Asian Paints, Spotify and Nutella films and nothing breaks, which is the test a festive film should fail. The craft is not the problem. Ogilvy and Spotify's team made handsome, well-cast work, and we would say so to their faces. The problem is the brief, which has quietly become "show the preparations, let the product lighten them", and three marketing teams approved it without checking what the other two were shooting. The fix is in the exceptions. Pick a Diwali ritual nobody owns yet, give it a conflict the audience recognises, and let the product end the conflict rather than decorate it. Cadbury found the neighbours. Supertails found the table. Next year, find the thing we have not been shown, before the planner writes "the magic is in the preparation" on slide three again. We will know. We always know.
