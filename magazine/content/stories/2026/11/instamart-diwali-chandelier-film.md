## The tea

Instamart's Diwali film opens on a couple coming home from a late party. She wants to call it a night. He is wearing a crystal chandelier as an outfit and running on low battery. That is the film. Deadpan performances, restrained camera, one gag, and a close on an Instamart delivery with the line "Diwali decor and more, only on Instamart", per [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/brand-marketing/instamart-diwali-campaign-features-man-wearing-chandelier-to-promote-home-decor-113768.htm) on 31 October.

The visible decision is on the credit line. [afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/news/advertising/instamart-gives-diwali-d%C3%A9cor-a-quirky-twist-in-new-ad-12770456) reports the film was "conceptualised and executed by the in-house team". No agency, no director, no production house is named there, in Storyboard18, or in [Adgully](https://www.adgully.com/post/23292/instamarts-latest-diwali-film-turns-festive-dcor-into-literal-outfits), which ran it on 3 November. For a Swiggy brand putting out a festive film in the most crowded ad week of the year, that is a choice, and it is a choice about cost and speed as much as craft.

Mayur Hola, VP Brand at Swiggy, is on the record with the strategy, and it is refreshingly short. Most Diwali films show lit-up homes and family moments, he says, so Instamart kept the decor at the centre and let the humour do the rest. The category in question is wide. Storyboard18 lists six lanes in the festive assortment: linen and furnishings, home decor, cleaning supplies, electronics, home appliances, and beauty and personal care hampers. Instamart launched in August 2020 and now runs in more than 131 cities. The film sells the basket, not a product.

The context is money. Quick commerce is the loudest new advertiser of this festive season. Datum estimates quick-commerce advertising could reach about INR 2,200 crore in the 2026 festive period, up around 50 percent year on year, per a [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/brand-marketing/festive-demand-drives-ctv-ad-prices-up-as-audience-reach-doubles-ws-l-112363.htm) report of 12 October on festive CTV rates, which also notes video and OEM home-page placements rising up to 30 percent. When every rival in the category is buying the same inventory, the creative has to do the differentiating, because the media plan cannot.

## The receipts

Hola's line, via afaqs: "A man wearing a chandelier is silly, but it makes people look at the category." And the follow-through: "Diwali shopping is never just one thing, so we're letting people know that decor and the rest of the festive list are all on Instamart. Just maybe not as an outfit."

The in-house credit is in afaqs and Adgully. The 131 cities figure and the six category lanes are in Storyboard18. The INR 2,200 crore Datum estimate is in Storyboard18's 12 October report. [Impact](https://www.impactonnet.com/more-from-impact/instamart-uses-a-chandelier-clad-man-to-sell-its-diwali-dcor-assortment-17380.html) and [MediaBrief](https://mediabrief.com/instamarts-diwali-film-turns-a-chandelier-into-an-outfit/) carried the film the same week.

## The read

Instamart bought one joke and committed to it. No agency, no diya, one tired man in a chandelier. There is a version of this film that over-explains the gag with a voiceover and a product montage, and the brand did not make that version, which is the part worth noticing. The in-house credit will read as a cost line to some agencies, and it is. It is also a speed line. A quick-commerce brand that can go from brief to film without a pitch is a brand that can react to a festive week while it is still happening.

The catch is the one every single-gag film carries. The chandelier does its work once. If the short cuts land the delivery and the line in the first few seconds, this is the cheapest-looking smart film of the season. If they do not, it is a very good meme with an app attached.
