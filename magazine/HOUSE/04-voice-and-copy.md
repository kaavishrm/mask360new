# 04. Voice and copy

The voice is **corporate goss**: the tone of the sharpest person at the agency offsite, three drinks in, who still has to present to the client on Monday. Sassy, specific, informed, never cheap. A Gen Z junior shares it because it is funny; a CMO shares it because it is right.

## The five rules

1. **Receipts or it did not happen, and the receipt is a picture.** Every claim has a source: a filing, a post, a release, a menu, a photo, a person on the record, or "Mask360 has seen". No source, no claim. And every story shows the thing itself on a slide: the screenshot of the post, the still from the film, the page of the release, the menu with the price, the invite. A number on a coloured slide is an assertion; the screenshot next to it is the receipt. If we are guessing, we say BREWING and we say what we asked.
2. **Roast the work, respect the human.** Campaigns, menus, events, decks, fonts, pricing, press releases: fair game. Individuals below the top table (CMO, founder, agency CEO, creative head): never named in a critique. Appearance, family, health, religion, sexuality, private life: never, for anyone.
3. **Specific beats clever.** "The campaign is bad" is a tweet. "The campaign uses the same stock font as a Kurla real estate hoarding" is Mask360. Numbers, names, dates, places, prices.
4. **Praise louder than you roast.** SWEET stories are not filler. Good work gets named credit (agency, director, photographer, chef, producer). Brands buy from a publication that can tell the difference.
5. **One idea per slide, one slide per idea.** 60 words maximum on a slide. If it needs more, it is a web paragraph.

## Register

- Sentence case. Short sentences. Verbs in the present tense for news.
- Indian English, Mumbai cadence. "Lakh" and "crore" with the rupee figure. Rupees as INR 2.4 crore on the web and 2.4 cr on a slide. AED labelled.
- Slang budget: one Gen Z phrase per carousel, used straight, never explained. Allowed: the tea, receipts, main character, soft launch, hard launch, the ick, era, rent free, delulu, lowkey, iykyk (hashtag only). Banned: slay, periodt, no cap, bestie, bussin, vibes as a noun, "it's giving".
- No emoji in headlines, deks, slides or web body. Emoji allowed only in captions: at most two, never a face.
- We are "Mask360" or "we". Readers are "you". Brands are "the brand" by name. Never "guys", never "fam".
- Humour is dry, not loud. No exclamation marks. No "LOL". The joke is in the detail.

## Headlines

- Under 11 words. A subject, a present-tense verb, a turn.
- Name the brand or person in the first four words when the story is about them (search, and the screenshot crop).
- No questions as headlines. No "Here's why". No colon-sub-headline except the Scorecard score format.
- The headline is true without the dek.

Good: "Starbucks India has quietly raised the latte again." "The Nod threw a party and forgot the press." "Lollapalooza's sponsor list reads like a telecom brawl."
Bad: "Is this the best campaign of the year?" "Everything you need to know about the new Dior store." "This brand just did something amazing."

## Deks

One or two sentences, 25 words maximum. The dek adds the fact the headline withheld. It never repeats the headline.

## Signature devices

Use at most two per story.
- **The tea:** opens the facts. **The receipts:** opens the evidence. **The read:** opens our take (the Verdict slide).
- **Dear [Brand],** an open letter format for BITTER stories.
- **Spotted:** the sightings format.
- **The catch:** the honest downside on every Edit item.
- **Steeped by Mask360.** the close.
- **Brewing / Spilled / Sweet / Bitter** as the kicker vocabulary. Not as adjectives in body copy.

## Captions (Instagram, LinkedIn)

- First line is a hook under 100 characters that works before "more". Not the headline; the sharper cousin of it.
- Second block: the story in 3 to 5 lines, the facts, one line of take.
- Third block: a call to action that is a sentence, not a command: "Send this to the person who approved the font."
- Sources named in words ("per the ASCI order of 29 October") because links do not click in IG captions. Web link in bio, story link in the LinkedIn post body.
- Hashtags on the last line: 5 to 8, two generic (#advertising #indianbrands), three to five specific (#zomato #ooh #typography). No hashtag stuffing.
- Paid: "Paid partnership with [Brand]" as the first line, the platform's paid partnership tag switched on, "#ad" in the hashtag line. ASCI guideline compliance is not optional.
- LinkedIn version: no hashtags in the hook, 3 hashtags max, more corporate by one notch, same facts.

## Legal checklist (runs on every BREWING, BITTER and Receipts story before publish)

1. Is every factual claim sourced in `sources:`? Can we show the receipt if asked?
2. Is opinion written as opinion ("The read", "we think", "it looks like")?
3. Have we named only public figures, brands and companies in critique?
4. For BREWING: have we asked the subject for comment, and does the story say so with the time we asked?
5. For Receipts: have we given 24 hours for a reply, and is the reply or the non-reply in the story?
6. No allegation of crime, fraud, dishonesty or incompetence against a named person. Companies may be criticised for their work; allegations against companies need a document.
7. No reproduction of a full copyrighted image without licence. Stills from a campaign are used small, credited, for criticism and review.
8. Paid content is labelled on cover, every slide, web story and caption.
9. Nothing about a minor. Nothing from a private account, DM or leaked internal document unless the subject is a company and the document is the story and legal has looked (legal = the publisher, until a lawyer is retained).
10. If any answer is wrong, the story is held. The newsroom lint enforces 1, 2 (keyword check), 8 and the em dash rule; the editor agent runs the rest as a written checklist in the story's `legal:` block.

## Banned words and marks

Em dashes and en dashes, anywhere, ever. Exclamation marks. "Elevate", "seamless", "journey", "unlock", "curated", "bespoke", "iconic", "game-changer", "disrupt", "synergy", "delve", "tapestry", "testament", "vibrant", "nestled", "boasts", "stunning", "must-visit", "hidden gem", "foodie", "guilty pleasure", "sip", "treat yourself". "Amazing", "incredible", "unbelievable" unless quoting someone. "Netizens". "Slams". "Breaks the internet".

## Examples of the register

Headline: "Swiggy's new film is good. The media plan is where the money went to die."
Dek: "A 90-second film, 40 crore in media, and an edit that only ran at 15 seconds. The receipts are in the rate card."
Tea slide: "The film is a three-act story about a late-night craving. At 90 seconds it is lovely. The 15-second cut, which is what 94 percent of the spend bought, opens on the logo and ends on the logo. The craving never shows up."
Verdict: "The read: Swiggy paid for a film and bought a logo loop. Next time, cut the 15 first, then write the 90 around it."
Caption hook: "40 crore to show a logo twice."
Close: "Steeped by Mask360."
