## 1. How attention works in a feed

### 1.1 The first second and the thumb stop

A thumb stop is the moment a scrolling viewer stops on a post; the thumb-stop ratio (also called hook rate) is 3 second views divided by impressions, the share of people served a video who stayed past the first three seconds. Everything else in this file depends on that number, because an unwatched film has no retention, no message and no brand effect.

The evidence for how short the window is:
- Facebook IQ, "Capturing attention in feed" (2016): people spent an average of 1.7 seconds with a piece of content on mobile feed against 2.5 seconds on desktop. The figure dates from 2016; the house treats 1.7 seconds as the upper bound of the decision window.
- The same Facebook IQ work found that 47 percent of the value of a video campaign (measured as ad recall, awareness and purchase intent lift) was delivered in the first 3 seconds and 74 percent in the first 10 seconds. The reason for front-loading the brand: most viewers never see second 11.
- YouTube counts a thumbnail impression only when the thumbnail is shown for more than 1 second with at least 50 percent of it visible (YouTube Help, answer 9314486, read 29 October 2026). The platform itself defines one second as the minimum unit of attention.

What decides the stop, in order of weight (house reading of the evidence and of ten years of Meta and TikTok creative guidance):
1. Motion already in progress at frame one. A static first frame that then starts moving loses the people who decided on the static frame. The eye's peripheral vision is tuned to motion onset, so the first 3 to 5 frames must move.
2. A human face or hand at a readable scale. Faces are detected pre-attentively; a hand touching a product reads as touch, which luxury needs.
3. Contrast with the feed around it. The feed in October 2026 is saturated, fast and captioned in white boxes; a still, dark, quiet frame with one line of type is a pattern interrupt by default. This is why restraint works on social: it is the rarest signal in the feed.
4. A readable promise in text, inside 4 words (section 2.7).
5. Sound, for the minority listening (section 1.7).

### 1.2 The 3 second view and what it measures

Each platform counts a view differently, and the differences decide what a report means.

| Platform | Metric | Definition | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Meta (Facebook, Instagram ads) | 3-second video plays | Plays of at least 3 seconds, or nearly the full length for videos under 3 seconds | Meta Ads Manager metric; help page not readable in this check |
| Meta | ThruPlay | Plays to completion, or for at least 15 seconds | As above |
| Instagram organic | Views | Instagram moved its Insights to "views" as the primary count across formats in 2024 to 2025, counting repeat plays | Reported; Instagram Help was unreadable |
| Instagram organic | Skip rate | Share of views where the viewer swiped away inside the first 3 seconds | Reported as added to Reels Insights; verify in the account's Insights |
| YouTube Shorts | Views | Since 31 March 2025, every start or replay of a Short, with no minimum watch time | Verified, YouTube Help answer 10059070 |
| YouTube Shorts | Engaged views | The previous metric, kept in Analytics; YouTube Partner Programme eligibility and Shorts revenue sharing are based on engaged Shorts views | Verified, as above |
| YouTube long form | Views | A deliberate play, counted by YouTube's own validation; impressions as defined in 1.1 | Verified for impressions |
| TikTok | Video views | Counted on play start, including replays | TikTok Analytics |

Reading rule: a Shorts view since March 2025 is a start, so Shorts view counts are inflated relative to 2024 and relative to engaged views. Compare Shorts against Shorts using engaged views and average percentage viewed; never compare a Shorts view count with an Instagram ThruPlay.

### 1.3 Retention curves and where drop offs happen

A retention curve (audience retention graph) plots the share of starting viewers still watching at each moment of a video. YouTube defines the parts the house reads on every curve (YouTube Help, answer 9314415, read 29 October 2026):
- Intro: the percentage of the audience still watching after the first 30 seconds.
- Top moments: segments where almost no one dropped off.
- Spikes: moments rewatched or shared; the curve rises above the line before it.
- Dips: moments skipped or abandoned.
- Typical retention: the curve compared with the channel's 10 latest videos of similar length, which is the only fair benchmark.

The shape every short-form curve takes, and what each part means:

| Zone | Where | Typical cause of loss | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| The cliff | 0 to 3 s | No reason to stay: static open, logo first, slow fade in, an establishing shot of a building | Start mid-motion on the strongest image; cut the first 12 to 25 frames of every edit as a reflex |
| The first slope | 3 to 10 s | The hook promised something the next shots do not deliver | Deliver the first piece of the promise by second 5 |
| The plateau | 10 s to 80 percent | Gradual leak; a dip at every repeated beat | Rehook (1.4); remove any shot that repeats information |
| The exit ramp | Last 20 percent | Viewers sense the ending (music resolving, end card, logo build) and leave | Deliver the payoff before the end card; on Reels, loop the ending into the start (1.5) |
| Spikes | Anywhere | A reveal, a line worth rereading, a detail worth pausing for | Repeat the device; it is the account's signature |

Absolute and relative retention: absolute retention is the share of this video's viewers still watching; relative retention (YouTube) compares that with other videos of similar length across YouTube. A curve above 100 percent at a point means rewatching, which is the most valuable signal a film can earn.

### 1.4 Rehooks every 5 to 8 seconds

A rehook is a new reason to keep watching placed inside the film: a reveal, a change of scale, a new location, a line, a sound. The 5 to 8 second interval is a practitioner heuristic from creator editing, with no platform publication behind it. The mechanism behind it is real: attention habituates to a constant stimulus within seconds, and a change in the stimulus restores it (the orienting response, described by Evgeny Sokolov in 1963).

The luxury version of a rehook is new information. A cheap rehook is noise: a zoom punch, a whoosh, a flash frame, a text bubble that says "wait for it". A luxury rehook is a cut from the wide of a Jaipur courtyard to the hand-cut jaali in close-up, a second line of copy, a silence (15-lighting-and-sound.md, section 14.4), or the moment the model turns to camera. Rule: in a 30 second Reel, plan rehooks at about 6, 12, 18 and 24 seconds, and make each one a different device.

### 1.5 Loops and the invisible loop

A loop is a film whose last frame cuts back to its first so cleanly that the replay reads as a continuation. Replays count as views on every platform listed in 1.2 and raise average percentage viewed above 100 percent, which every recommender reads as satisfaction. The invisible loop is the craft of hiding that join.

Craft rules:
- Length 7 to 12 seconds (16-editing-colour-grading-and-delivery.md, section 3.5). Longer films rarely loop because viewers notice the restart.
- Match composition, motion vector and exposure between the last and first frame: a pour that ends where it began, a door that closes onto the same door opening, a camera move that ends on the frame it started on.
- Audio: cut the music on a bar that resolves into the bar at the start; crossfade ambience over the join in the edit by placing the head of the ambience track under the tail.
- Text: if a line sits on the last card, it must also make sense as the first thing read, or the loop exposes itself.
- No end card on a loop. The mark sits in the profile and the caption.

### 1.6 Pattern interrupts and curiosity gaps

A pattern interrupt is a deliberate break in an established rhythm that resets attention (16-editing-colour-grading-and-delivery.md, section 3.7, places one at the turn of a 30 second film). On a feed, the interrupt is relative to the feed itself, which is why stillness, silence and black work in 2026.

A curiosity gap is the gap between what a viewer knows and what they want to know; George Loewenstein's information gap theory ("The psychology of curiosity", Psychological Bulletin, 1994) shows that curiosity rises when people are made aware of a specific missing piece and that it is satisfied by closing it. Two craft consequences:
- The gap must be specific. "What happens next" is weak; "the one room at this palace that has never been photographed" is strong.
- The gap must close inside the film. Clickbait opens a gap and closes it elsewhere; viewers learn the account lies and its sends and saves fall. ASCI's creator resources include a note on clickbait (see Sources).

### 1.7 Sound on and sound off

Most feed video is watched muted, and the share depends on surface and context:
- Digiday reported in May 2016, from publisher data, that up to 85 percent of Facebook video was watched without sound.
- Verizon Media and Publicis Media's 2019 survey of US consumers, as reported at the time, found 69 percent watched video with sound off in public places and 25 percent in private places, and that viewers were more likely to finish a video when captions were available.
- TikTok is the exception by design: TikTok's own commissioned research (TikTok Marketing Science, 2021) reported that 88 percent of its users said sound was essential to the experience. TikTok opens with sound on; Instagram feed and Facebook feed autoplay muted, Reels and Stories play with sound when the device is not silenced.

House consequence: captions and on-screen type are structural. Every social cut is designed twice, first as a silent film that is fully understood without sound, then as a sound film (15-lighting-and-sound.md, section 14.1). The test is a muted viewing on a phone at arm's length before the sound pass.

### 1.8 The hook taxonomy

A hook is whatever earns the first 3 seconds. Nine families, each with its luxury-legitimate version and its cheap version:

| Hook | What it is | Luxury version | Cheap version | Best on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Visual | An image that has not been seen before | A hand-blown glass bottle filling with light; an aerial of a desert camp with one lantern lit | Drone reveal of a building, stock sunset | Every platform |
| Text | A line that opens a specific gap | "Jaipur / chose you back" (mask360-copywriting, Mode 2) | "You won't believe this" | Reels, TikTok, Shorts |
| Sound | A real sound at frame one | A clasp closing, a pour, heels on marble (15-lighting-and-sound.md, section 14.2) | Trending audio, riser and hit | TikTok, Reels with sound |
| Motion | Movement already happening | A silk sleeve mid-swing, a door mid-open | Zoom punch, shake, speed ramp | Reels, Stories |
| Question | A question the viewer cannot answer yet | "What does a 400 year old room smell like?" | "Did you know...?" and a rhetorical question answered in the next line (banned by mask360-copywriting) | YouTube, LinkedIn |
| Claim | A specific, checkable statement | "Every stone in this necklace was cut in Jaipur" | "The best hotel in Dubai" (unsubstantiated superlatives also breach ASCI and UAE advertising rules) | LinkedIn, X, Shorts |
| In medias res | Start in the middle of the action | The chef already plating; the model already walking | Opening on a reaction face | Reels, TikTok |
| Reveal | Withhold, then show | A slow pull from a detail to the full suite | "Wait till the end" text | Reels, Shorts, Stories |
| Contrast | Two states set against each other | Before and after of a restoration; the raw stone and the finished ring | Split screens of "us vs them" | Carousels, Reels |

Rule: one hook per film. Two hooks in the first second compete and both lose.

### 1.9 What the 2026 algorithms reward

Each recommender predicts a set of viewer actions and ranks by a weighted sum of those predictions. What each platform says it predicts, from its own pages:

| Platform | What it predicts or weights | Source and date | Craft consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Instagram Reels | Likelihood to reshare, watch to the end, like, and go to the audio page; signals in rough order: the viewer's activity, history with the poster, information about the reel, information about the poster | Adam Mosseri, "Instagram ranking explained", 31 May 2023 | Completion and sends are the targets; the audio page signal favours original audio |
| Instagram Feed | Likelihood to spend a few seconds on a post, comment, like, share and tap the profile photo | As above | Dwell on carousels; profile taps reward a grid worth visiting |
| Instagram, all recommendations | Accounts that mainly post unoriginal reels, photos or carousels are removed from recommendations; borders, watermarks, subtitles or a caption credit do not make a post original; original posts were 75 percent of recommendations in the US; eligibility returns when most recent posts over a rolling 30 days are original | "Rewarding original creators", 30 April 2026 | Brand accounts must post their own footage; reposted creator content goes through Collab or a credited reshare |
| Instagram, viewer control | "Your Algorithm" lets viewers see and edit the topics shaping their recommendations; launched for Reels in the US on 10 December 2025, extended to Explore on 15 April 2026 and to Feed, Reels and Explore as one system, globally in English, on 10 June 2026 | about.instagram.com, reels-algorithm-control | Topic clarity matters: an account that posts one subject is easier to match to a stated interest |
| TikTok For You | Interactions (likes, shares, follows, comments, content created), video information (captions, sounds, hashtags), device and account settings at lower weight; finishing a longer video is a strong signal; follower count and past high-performing videos are not direct factors | TikTok newsroom, "How TikTok recommends videos #ForYou", 2020, read 29 October 2026 | A new account can reach the For You feed on its first post; completion of longer films outweighs likes |
| YouTube | Impressions and click-through decide whether a video is tried; watch time decides whether it keeps being shown; the A/B test picks the title or thumbnail that earns the highest watch time | YouTube Help answers 9314486 and 13861714 | Thumbnails that overpromise lose the test |
| X | For You score is a weighted sum of predicted probabilities of actions: favourite, reply, repost, quote, share, clicks (post, profile, link, photo, video), dwell, follow, and negative actions (not interested, mute, block, report) with negative weights | github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm, entries dated 13 August to 26 October 2026 | Replies and dwell beat likes; one report costs more than many likes earn |
| X, historical | The 2023 open-sourced heavy ranker weighted a like 0.5, a repost 1.0, a reply 13.5, a profile click 12.0, a reply the author engaged with 75.0, negative feedback -74.0, a report -369.0 | github.com/twitter/the-algorithm-ml, README dated 5 April 2023 | Superseded; useful as the clearest public statement of how much conversation outweighs approval |
| LinkedIn | LinkedIn does not publish its ranking weights; its engineering blog has described dwell time as a feed signal | LinkedIn engineering, reported | Document posts and native video earn dwell; posts with external links are widely observed to reach fewer people |
| Pinterest | Saves and outbound clicks from search and related pins | Pinterest business help, reported | Keywords in titles and descriptions; a pin earns traffic for months |

The cross-platform signal set for 2026, in the order the house optimises: completion (and replays), sends and shares, saves, comments with substance, profile visits and follows from non-followers. Likes are the weakest positive signal on every platform that publishes weights. House position: optimise a film for completion and sends, because those two are rewarded everywhere, and treat each platform's quirks (Instagram's audio page, X's replies, Pinterest's saves) as secondary.

## 2. Text on screen craft

On-screen type is the film's script for the muted majority. It follows the house type layer (design-taste, section 7, and design-taste, references/type.md) and the caption rules of 16-editing-colour-grading-and-delivery.md, section 11; this section adds the feed-specific values.

### 2.1 Size

Minimum readable size on a phone: a font size (em height) of about 3 to 4 percent of frame height. On a 1080 x 1920 frame that is 58 to 77 px; on the 1440 x 2560 frame Meta recommends for Reels and Stories, 77 to 102 px. The reason: a phone shows a 1920 px tall frame at about 1,000 device-independent pixels of height, so a 58 px font renders at about 30 px on screen, which matches the comfortable body size for reading at arm's length. File 16 sets the English caption line at 56 to 72 px with a floor of 48 px; the 3 to 4 percent rule and file 16 agree, and the floor never moves.

| Element | Font size on 1080 x 1920 | Share of height | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook line (one to four words) | 96 to 140 px | 5 to 7 percent | Read in under 0.5 s, from a scroll, by peripheral vision |
| Card copy (one thought) | 64 to 88 px | 3.3 to 4.6 percent | Comfortable reading at arm's length |
| Spoken-word captions | 56 to 72 px | 2.9 to 3.8 percent | File 16, section 11.2 |
| Legal line, credit, location | 40 to 48 px | 2.1 to 2.5 percent | The minimum for anything that must be legible; never below 40 |
| Mark (m360 or client) | Cap height about 40 to 60 px | 2 to 3 percent | Present and quiet |

Scale contrast still governs (design-taste, section 2): one large line against small supporting type, nothing medium.

### 2.2 Weight

Medium to bold weights survive recompression; light weights do not. Instagram, TikTok and WhatsApp all re-encode uploads, and the macroblocking (the square artefacts of a compressed video frame) erodes thin strokes first. House values: Space Grotesk 500 for captions and card copy, 700 for a hook line, never 300 on moving video below 80 px. The reason for 500 over 700 on running captions: a bold caption reads as shouting across a whole film, and the house voice does not shout.

### 2.3 Position within safe zones

A safe zone is the part of the frame that no platform interface element covers. Values in pixels on a 1080 x 1920 canvas, from 26-platform-and-format-specs-2026.md, section 5.6, where the status of each is recorded:

| Placement | Top | Bottom | Left | Right | Source status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Instagram Reels | 269 (14 percent) | 672 (35 percent) | 65 (6 percent) | 65 (6 percent) | Verified on Meta's ads guide, 29 October 2026 |
| Instagram and Facebook Stories | 269 | 672 | 65 | 65 | Verified, same percentages |
| Same, at 1440 x 2560 | 358 | 896 | 86 | 86 | Derived from the percentages |
| TikTok | about 290 | about 480 | about 60 | about 200 | Working default from TikTok's downloadable overlays and third-party guides |
| YouTube Shorts | about 120 | about 384 | 40 | about 130 | Third-party |
| Pinterest idea ads | 270 | 440 | 65 | 195 | Verified on Pinterest's product specs |
| Snapchat | 150 | 150 to 250 | 0 | 0 | Third-party or unverified |
| House universal vertical box | 290 | 672 | 65 | 200 | The union of the above: an 815 x 958 px box |

Placement rules inside the box:
- Hook line in the upper third of the box (about 300 to 620 px from the top), because the eye enters a vertical frame high and the lower band competes with the caption and audio chrome.
- Spoken captions with their bottom edge no lower than about 1,200 px (16-editing-colour-grading-and-delivery.md, section 11.3).
- Arabic cuts: check the frame on a handset set to Arabic, because right-to-left interfaces can mirror the action column; the universal box already keeps 200 px clear on the right and the house keeps 200 px clear on the left for Arabic versions.
- The grid crop: the profile grid shows the centre 3:4 of a Reel (1080 x 1440, so 240 px lost top and bottom), so a cover's title sits inside the band from 240 to 1,680 px.

### 2.4 Duration per word

Average silent reading speed for adult English non-fiction is about 238 words a minute, or about 0.25 seconds a word (Marc Brysbaert, "How many words do we read per minute?", Journal of Memory and Language, 2019). On a moving image the viewer is also reading the picture, so the house allows 0.3 to 0.4 seconds a word, with a floor of 1.5 seconds for any card, plus 0.3 seconds for the eye to find the text after a cut. File 16's supers rule (1 second plus about 0.3 seconds a word) gives the same values in the 3 to 6 word range.

| Words on the card | Hold | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 to 3 | 1.5 s | The floor; a one-word card shorter than this flickers |
| 4 | 1.5 to 1.9 s | The split screen line (2.7) |
| 6 | 2.1 to 2.7 s | |
| 8 | 2.7 to 3.5 s | |
| 12 | 3.9 to 5.1 s | Too long for a card; split into two |
| Hindi or Arabic line | Add 20 to 30 percent | Devanagari conjuncts and Arabic ligatures carry more per glyph; file 16, section 11.4, uses 15 to 17 characters a second for both |

Test: play the cut once at full speed with someone who has not seen the copy. If they cannot repeat the card, the hold is short or the card is long.

### 2.5 Contrast devices

Type over moving footage needs a contrast plan, because the background changes under it. WCAG 2.2 success criterion 1.4.3 sets 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text; the house applies 4.5:1 to every card, measured against the brightest frame the text sits over, because a card that passes on the average frame fails on the brightest.

The devices, in order of preference:
1. Choose the shot. Place copy over a dark or plain area composed for it on set (6.2). This is the only device with no visual cost.
2. A gradient band: a 20 to 30 percent darkening of the lower or upper third, feathered over 300 px or more (file 16, section 11.2). Invisible when done well.
3. A blur plate: a soft local blur behind the text, 20 to 40 px radius. Use once per film; glass and blur are peaking in 2026 (design-taste, section 6).
4. A solid backing plate in a brand colour, edges matching the house radius. Reads as editorial when the plate is the design; reads as the in-app default when it is a white rounded box.
5. A drop shadow: only a soft, low-opacity shadow (15 to 25 percent, 4 to 8 px offset, 10 to 20 px blur) to lift white text off a busy highlight. Hard black shadows and outlines are cheap tells (design-taste, section 3).

### 2.6 One thought per card

A card carries one thought, and the next card starts a new one. The reason is mechanical: a viewer reads a card in one fixation sequence, and a second clause forces a re-read that the hold time does not allow, so the second thought is lost and the first is weakened. mask360-copywriting sets reel overlays at 1 to 5 words per card and asks that copy never describe what is on screen. The craft corollary: if the card needs a comma, it is two cards.

### 2.7 The house split screen copy format and the 4 word rule

Split screen copy is the house format for agency showcase and campaign Reels: the line is divided into two halves that land as one thought. mask360-copywriting sets the rules: maximum 4 words per side; left side is tension or statement, right side is completion or subversion; together they land as one thought; weight and placement do the emotional work. Its examples include "PALACES / DON'T ASK TWICE" and "SOME DOORS / ONLY OPEN ONCE" (Mode 2) and "WE MADE / JAIPUR FEEL IT" (Mode 1).

The craft layer beneath those rules:
- Geometry. Two halves of equal font size on a shared baseline, either side by side across a central gutter of at least 40 px or stacked across the frame's horizontal centre. The halves must sit inside the universal box; the box is 815 px wide, so each side gets about 385 px after the gutter, which is why the limit is 4 words.
- Timing. The left half enters on a cut; the right half enters 8 to 15 frames later at 25 fps (0.3 to 0.6 s), on the next cut or on a sound event. The whole line then holds for at least 1.5 s. The delay is the reveal: it makes the second half land as completion.
- Case. The copywriting examples are set in capitals; on screen the house sets them in Space Grotesk 600 to 700 with tracking at or slightly above zero, because caps tracked wide read as the every-label tell (design-taste, section 3).
- Picture under the split. One continuous shot under both halves, or one shot per half if the edit itself is split, and no third image during the line.

### 2.8 Caption styles: karaoke, pop and static

| Style | What it is | Reads as | House use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Karaoke | Word-by-word highlighting as words are spoken, often in a colour or with a box | The creator-tool default of 2022 to 2026; reads automated | Never on brand or agency work (file 16, section 11.2) |
| Pop | Words appearing one or two at a time, often bouncing or scaling | Energetic, mass, performance; reads as an ad | Never on luxury; acceptable on a client's performance variant only if testing proves it, and the luxury master stays clean |
| Static phrase | A full phrase appears, holds, cuts with the next phrase | Editorial, subtitled film; reads as cinema | The house default for spoken captions |
| Typographic card | Designed type as a frame of its own or over picture, timed to the edit | Title design; reads as authored | Hooks, split screen lines, chapter cards |

Kaavish's personal channel uses one static overlay for the whole reel (kaavish-personal-ig); that is a deliberate exception and stays there.

### 2.9 Fonts for overlays

The house face is Space Grotesk, 300 to 700 (design-taste, section 7), with Departure Mono as the pixel alternative; client work uses the client's own typeface, licensed for video and social use (check the font licence: many desktop licences exclude video titles or require a separate broadcast or app licence). Render every card in the edit (Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve, After Effects or the Edits app with a saved brand preset) and upload with the type burned in. The in-app text tools of Instagram and TikTok offer a small set of system styles (the white text in a rounded colour box, the outlined classic, the neon) that every account uses; they are the single most recognisable tell that a post was made in the app in five minutes. For Devanagari and Arabic companions, see 03-typography.md; test Arabic shaping in the exact editor version (file 16, section 11.2 records the tools that break Arabic ligatures).

## 3. Formats by platform as of October 2026

Exact numbers with their status codes live in 26-platform-and-format-specs-2026.md, section 2 (organic) and section 3 (paid). This section is the craft of each format; the consolidated table is in section 10.2.

Regional fact that shapes every plan: TikTok has been blocked in India since 29 June 2020, when the Government of India banned 59 apps under Section 69A of the Information Technology Act, 2000. For India the short-form stack is Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, WhatsApp and, for specific audiences, Snapchat and LinkedIn. In the UAE and Saudi Arabia, TikTok and Snapchat are both central. Platform reach in India, from the Government of India's figures released on 25 February 2021 with the intermediary rules: WhatsApp 530 million users, YouTube 448 million, Facebook 410 million, Instagram 210 million, Twitter 17.5 million. These are the last official figures and are dated; the order has held, and Instagram has grown fastest since.

### 3.1 Instagram Reels

- Length: 3 seconds to 3 minutes in the Reels camera and editor, with Instagram suggesting 3 to 15 seconds for transitions and one-liners, 15 to 60 seconds for tutorials and 60 seconds to 3 minutes for storytelling (Instagram, "How to make Instagram Reels: complete 2026 guide", read 29 October 2026). Longer uploads are reported on many accounts (file 26, section 2.1). House lengths: 7 to 12 seconds for a loop, 15 to 30 seconds for a campaign cutdown, 45 to 90 seconds for a story with a person in it.
- Canvas: 9:16; export 1080 x 1920 for organic, 1440 x 2560 for ads (file 26). Instagram's own export advice for the Edits app is 2K, HDR and 60 fps (creators.instagram.com, best camera settings, read 29 October 2026); the house exports at the frame rate it shot (24 or 25 fps for film material) because a 25 to 60 fps conversion duplicates frames and judders on pans, and uses 50 or 60 fps only for material shot at it.
- Cover frame: chosen from the reel or uploaded; the cover appears on the profile grid (Instagram's 2026 guide). Instagram itself notes that a strong opening line of text in the first frame can work as well as a thumbnail. The grid shows the central 3:4 (1080 x 1440), so the cover is composed for that crop (6.8).
- Trial reels: launched December 2024 (reported; the announcement page could not be read). A trial reel is shown first to non-followers only, so a new format can be tested without the followers seeing it, and its performance is visible before deciding whether to share it to followers. House use: test hook variants and new series formats as trial reels before committing a client's main grid.
- Collabs: a Collab post is co-authored by two or more accounts, appears on each profile and shares likes, comments and views (Instagram's 2026 guide). The house uses Collab for every client and creator partnership, because it pools reach, it credits the original creator (which the April 2026 originality rules reward) and it keeps the paid-partnership label visible.
- Originality: since 30 April 2026 Instagram removes accounts that mainly post unoriginal reels, photos or carousels from recommendations (section 1.9). A client account that reposts creator content with a credit line is now at risk; the fix is Collab, the remix feature, or reshares to Stories.
- Edits: Instagram's editing app, launched 22 April 2025; it gained an assistant on 30 September 2026 and a carousel editor on 7 October 2026 (iOS first), with saved brand presets for fonts, colours, audio and text styles (edits.instagram.com, read 29 October 2026). It is acceptable for social cutdowns when the brand preset carries the house type; the master edit stays in Premiere Pro or Resolve.
- Instagram for TV: Instagram's television app runs on Amazon Fire TV, Google TV and Samsung smart TVs from 2020 models onward in the US, with tests of horizontal video and casting (about.instagram.com, read 29 October 2026). A hotel or property film may now be watched on a 65 inch screen; the master stays 4K.

### 3.2 Stories

- Length: a single story clip can be up to 60 seconds; longer videos are split into consecutive clips (Instagram, "How to use Instagram Stories: complete 2026 guide"). The 15 second segment of the early Stories years no longer applies to organic Stories; Meta's ads guide still notes that Stories ad clips under 16 seconds play whole (file 26).
- Life: 24 hours, then the archive; Highlights keep Stories on the profile as round covers under the bio until removed.
- Interactive stickers (Instagram's 2026 guide): link (to an outside URL, with editable button text), poll (two options, live results), quiz (four choices, one correct), questions (private replies, shareable anonymously), countdown (viewers subscribe to a reminder), Add Yours (a chain prompt), music, emoji slider, location, mentions, GIFs. Layout holds up to 6 photos in one frame.
- Close Friends: a list-only audience. House use: a hotel's past guests, a jeweller's private clients, a launch's press list; the luxury use of Stories is intimacy.
- Craft: Stories are the one surface where low production reads as correct. Phone-shot, real-time, natural light, one line of type in the house face. The link sticker is the only clickable link on Instagram outside the bio and ads; design the frame around it (lower-middle, inside the universal box) and write the button text as the action.
- Disclosure: a paid Story has no caption, so ASCI requires the disclosure label superimposed on the picture or video; for videos of 15 seconds or less it must stay for at least 3 seconds (ASCI guidelines, section 5.8).

### 3.3 Carousels

A carousel is a single post of several swipeable images or videos; Instagram allows up to 20 items (announced August 2024; 10 through the publishing API most schedulers use), and every slide takes the ratio of the first (file 26). Three formats work for premium brands:

| Format | What it is | Slide count | Example use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Editorial carousel | A photo story in sequence, like a magazine spread: establishing, detail, person, detail, close | 6 to 10 | A hotel suite, a collection, a restaurant service |
| Typographic carousel | Type as the image: one statement per slide, set large in the house face on a flat ground | 5 to 8 | Agency point of view, a founder's argument, a manifesto |
| Swipe narrative | Each slide continues the image or the sentence of the one before; a panorama split across slides, a sentence finished on the next slide | 3 to 10 | A landscape, a process, a reveal |

Craft rules:
- Slide one is the hook and the grid image; it carries the whole post if no one swipes. It must work alone at 3:4 on the grid.
- Slide two is the second hook. Instagram is widely observed to show a carousel again, opening on slide two, to people who did not swipe the first time; Instagram has not published the mechanism. Treat slide two as a second cover.
- Count: 6 to 10 is the house range. Below 5, a carousel is a set of photos; above 10, completion falls and the last slide, which carries the reframe or the ask, goes unseen.
- Ratio: 4:5 at 1080 x 1350 (or 1440 x 1800) remains the safe export; 3:4 at 1080 x 1440 fills the grid tile without crop but its feed behaviour is reported as inconsistent (file 26), so test one post first.
- Panoramas: a swipe narrative across slides needs each seam to fall on a quiet part of the image, away from faces and products.
- The dead carousel: the listicle with icons ("5 reasons to visit Jaipur", one icon per slide, a numbered pill, a gradient). It is the most recognisable agency-content format of 2020 to 2025, and it reads as generated (design-taste, section 3: icons in circles, numbers on non-sequences).

### 3.4 The grid as an object

The profile grid is the brand's homepage on Instagram, and a visitor reads it as one composition in under 3 seconds. Since January 2025 the grid shows 3:4 tiles (file 26).

Planning rules:
- Plan in rows of three, nine posts ahead (a 3 x 3 block), in a grid planner (Later, Planoly, Preview, or a Figma frame at 3 x 1080 px wide). Each row must hold as a triptych.
- Rhythm: alternate scale (wide, detail, person), alternate tone (light, dark) and alternate type (image, image, typographic) so no two neighbouring tiles repeat.
- Pinned posts: up to 3 pinned posts at the top of the grid. Pin the brand's thesis: the current campaign film, the signature image, and the one post that explains who the brand is.
- Covers: every Reel's cover is designed for the grid crop, with or without a title. A grid of auto-selected Reel covers is a grid of blurred mid-motion frames.
- The profile as lookbook: the first 9 tiles are what a client, a journalist or a guest sees. Archive or hide posts that break the current lookbook; Instagram's archive keeps them recoverable.

What the reference accounts do on social, as craft (the taste reasons are in design-taste, references/canon.md):

| Account | The social move | What to borrow |
|---|---|---|
| Bottega Veneta | Deleted its Instagram, Facebook and Twitter accounts in January 2021 under Daniel Lee and moved its communication to Issue, a quarterly digital journal, letting fans and press carry the brand; check the house's current account status before citing it as absent | Scarcity as a social strategy; the brand that does not post is discussed more |
| Jacquemus | Product as sculpture and scale play; in 2023 it posted CGI films of giant Le Bambino bags rolling through Paris, a widely copied example of faux out-of-home (FOOH: a CGI film that places an impossible object in a real street); Simon Porte Jacquemus's personal account carries the intimate voice | One surprising idea per post; a founder's account beside the house account |
| Loewe | Craft and art at the centre: artisans, the Loewe Foundation Craft Prize, deadpan celebrity stills; creative direction passed from Jonathan Anderson to Jack McCollough and Lazaro Hernandez in March 2025 | Culture before product; humour without slapstick |
| Aesop | Architecture of its stores, literature, typographic restraint; product shot as still life | Place and words as the brand; very few faces |
| The Row | Sparse product photography, no campaign noise; reported as asking guests not to photograph its February 2024 show | Silence as signal; posting less than anyone else in its tier |
| Rhode | Founder-led (Hailey Bieber), product as an object on a single colour, sold to e.l.f. Beauty in a deal worth up to 1 billion dollars announced in May 2025 | Founder presence plus a ruthlessly consistent product image |
| Aman | Landscape and architecture held long, almost no people, very low cadence | Stillness and space; the grid as a quiet place |

### 3.5 Broadcast channels and DMs

A broadcast channel is a one-to-many chat on Instagram (launched February 2023) where an account posts text, photos, voice notes and polls to followers who join; followers can react and vote but not reply in the thread. DMs (direct messages) are the private channel where most sends happen.

House use:
- A channel is an inner circle. A jeweller announces a private viewing there before the grid; a hotel shares the opening date of a new villa; the agency shares work in progress. Cadence: 2 to 4 messages a month. A channel that posts daily is a spam folder.
- Sends are the 2026 signal (1.9). Design posts to be sent: a specific detail, a place someone wants to show a friend, a line worth forwarding.
- DMs for luxury service: reply within business hours in the brand voice, in the customer's language (English, Hindi, Arabic), with a named person signing off. Automation is for routing only (Meta's messaging tools can label and route); a luxury client never receives a bot's answer to a question about a 2 crore rupee necklace.

### 3.6 TikTok

- Market: blocked in India since June 2020; central in the UAE and Saudi Arabia.
- For You mechanics (TikTok's own explainer, read 29 October 2026): interactions, video information (captions, sounds, hashtags) and device and account settings feed recommendations; finishing a longer video is a strong signal; follower count and past performance are not direct factors. A brand's first video can reach the For You feed; equally, a large following buys nothing.
- Native and polished: TikTok's audience reads native grammar (phone camera, direct address, on-screen text, sound-on, trends) as honest and broadcast polish as an ad. The premium position is native grammar with luxury content: a real artisan speaking to a phone in a Jaipur workshop beats a graded brand film cut down to 9:16.
- Text hooks: TikTok users read on-screen text first; the hook line sits in the upper third, inside the 290 px top margin and clear of the right-hand action column (about 200 px).
- Sound: TikTok opens with sound on; the first sound is part of the hook. Business accounts use the Commercial Music Library or original audio (15-lighting-and-sound.md, section 14.3).
- Photo mode: up to 35 images swiped with music (file 26, third-party); the TikTok equivalent of a carousel.
- TikTok Shop: TikTok's in-app commerce; not available in India, and the UAE did not appear among TikTok Shop markets in the house's last check, so verify on TikTok's seller pages before planning commerce for a Gulf client.
- Brand accounts that work share three traits: a recurring person (an employee, a founder, an artisan), a recurring format, and replies to comments as videos. Luxury houses that do well on TikTok mostly let creators and their own staff carry the voice and keep the house account for campaign films.

### 3.7 YouTube Shorts and long form

Shorts:
- Up to 3 minutes with the multi-segment camera (YouTube Help, answer 10059070, read 29 October 2026); title 100 characters; vertical; maximum 1080p.
- Views count every start or replay since 31 March 2025; engaged views remain the measure for revenue sharing and Partner Programme eligibility (1.2).
- Links in Shorts descriptions and comments have not been clickable since 31 August 2023 (an anti-spam change announced by YouTube); the related video link inside a Short points viewers to a long-form video, which is the house's main use of Shorts for hotels and property: a Short as the trailer, the long-form tour as the destination.

Long form, thumbnails:
- Size: 3840 x 2160 at 16:9 recommended for videos (minimum width 640), 2160 x 3840 for Shorts; JPG or PNG; 2 MB on mobile upload, 50 MB on desktop; a 1280 x 720 file under 2 MB still passes when an older checklist asks for it (file 26, verified on YouTube Help answer 72431).
- The 160 px test: design the thumbnail at full size, then judge it at 160 px wide, about the size of a suggested-video thumbnail in the desktop sidebar. What survives at 160 px: one face or one object, at most 3 words, and strong tonal contrast. What does not survive: a logo, a second face, a sentence, thin type.
- The three elements: face (a human face with a readable expression, eyes toward the subject or the viewer), 3 words or fewer in the house face at bold weight, and contrast (subject against a ground of opposite value). For luxury the face can be a hand, a doorway, a single plate: one subject, one reading.
- Testing: YouTube's A/B test lets creators test up to 3 titles, 3 thumbnails or 3 title and thumbnail pairs; the version with the highest watch time is shown to all viewers, and if none wins clearly the first upload stays (YouTube Help, answer 13861714, read 29 October 2026). Optimising for watch time punishes thumbnails that overpromise.
- Click-through check: YouTube advises reading click-through rate for Home and Suggested in the first 24 hours after publication, on videos with above-average impressions (YouTube Help, answer 12340300).

Long form, titles, end screens and chapters:
- Titles: 100 characters (file 26); put the specific subject in the first 40 to 50 characters, which survive truncation in most surfaces. Sentence case. No capitals for emphasis, no emojis.
- End screens: the video must be at least 25 seconds; the end screen occupies the last 5 to 20 seconds with up to 4 elements (file 26). Leave the last 20 seconds of a 16:9 film composed for it: picture held, nothing important in the areas the elements cover.
- Chapters: the first timestamp must be 00:00, at least three timestamps in ascending order, and each chapter at least 10 seconds (YouTube Help, answer 9884579, read 29 October 2026). Chapters make a 12 minute property tour searchable and skippable, which raises satisfaction more than it costs in watch time.

### 3.8 LinkedIn

- Document posts: a PDF (or PPT, PPTX, DOC, DOCX) shown as a swipeable carousel; 100 MB and 300 pages maximum; square 1:1 or 4:5 pages read largest on mobile (file 26, verified). The document post is LinkedIn's carousel. House range: 6 to 12 pages, one statement per page, the typographic carousel format of 3.3.
- Native video: 3 seconds to 30 minutes; 4:5 or 9:16 for mobile, 16:9 for anything screened; under 30 fps (file 26). LinkedIn has run a vertical video feed since 2024. Captions are essential, because LinkedIn is read at work and mostly muted.
- Post text: 3,000 characters; about 150 show before "see more" (file 26). The first two lines are the hook.
- The founder voice: LinkedIn is where Mask360 sells to brand owners and marketing heads. Practitioners consistently report that posts from a person reach further and earn more trust than posts from a company page, and LinkedIn's Thought Leader Ads (sponsored posts from a member's own profile, launched 2023) are built on that behaviour. Kaavish's posts follow kaavish-personal-ig's voice discipline adapted to text: one idea, first person, a real moment from the work, no hollow agency flex (mask360-copywriting).
- Links: practitioners widely observe lower reach for posts with external links; LinkedIn has not published a penalty. House practice: the link goes in the first comment only when the post's job is traffic.

### 3.9 X

- 280 characters for standard accounts; Premium allows long posts (file 26, third-party). Images 16:9 or 1:1; video up to 2 minutes 20 seconds for standard accounts in the apps.
- Ranking: a weighted sum of predicted actions with heavy weight on replies and dwell and strong negative weight on reports and mutes (1.9).
- Use for Mask360: commentary and press, for an agency or a founder; rarely for a luxury consumer brand in India or the Gulf, where the audience is on Instagram, WhatsApp and Snapchat.

### 3.10 Threads

- 500 characters per post; images up to 1440 px wide; video up to 5 minutes and 1 GB; carousels of 2 to 20 items (Meta's Threads developer documentation, file 26, verified).
- One topic tag per post; the tag works like a channel.
- Shares the Instagram identity. Use for conversation: the founder's opinions, behind the scenes, replies to the industry. A luxury consumer brand's grid images posted to Threads unchanged read as a cross-post.

### 3.11 Pinterest for jewellery and interiors

Pinterest is a visual search engine: people search, save to boards and click through, often months before buying, which suits jewellery (wedding and engagement planning, festive buying) and interiors (homes, hotels, real estate fit-outs).
- Standard pin 2:3 at 1000 x 1500; title 100 characters with the first 40 visible; description 800 characters; video pins 4 seconds to 15 minutes, 6 to 15 seconds recommended for ads (file 26, verified on Pinterest's product specs).
- Keywords do the work hashtags do elsewhere: name the stone, the cut, the metal, the style, the room, the material ("emerald cut solitaire in platinum", "lime plaster bathroom Jaipur").
- Every pin carries a destination link, which makes Pinterest the most direct traffic channel in this file for a jeweller's product pages.
- Half-life: a pin keeps earning saves and clicks for months; a Reel's distribution is mostly over in days. Plan Pinterest as a library, built from every shoot's stills (5.6).
- Audience: Pinterest reported 553 million monthly active users for the fourth quarter of 2024 (company results, reported). India and the Gulf are smaller Pinterest markets than the US and Europe; use it for diaspora and international buyers of Indian jewellery and for interiors clients selling abroad.

### 3.12 WhatsApp channels and status in India and the Gulf

WhatsApp is the operating system of commerce in India and the Gulf: hotel reservations, jewellery appointments and restaurant bookings happen in WhatsApp chats.
- Status: WhatsApp's Stories, 24 hours, 9:16 at 1080 x 1920; video Status runs 60 seconds per clip in current builds, with 90 seconds reported in beta (file 26).
- Channels: one-to-many broadcast in the Updates tab, launched globally in September 2023. Followers cannot see each other and cannot reply in the channel; they react and vote.
- The Updates tab is used by about 1.5 billion people a day globally; on 16 June 2025 WhatsApp announced channel subscriptions (a monthly fee for exclusive updates), Promoted Channels (paid visibility in the directory) and ads in Status (businesses promoting in Status, with a message button), rolling out over several months and not touching personal chats (Meta announcement, read 29 October 2026).
- Craft: WhatsApp recompresses harder than any other platform the agency uses; send every final file to a test handset on iOS and Android before the client sees it (file 26). Keep text 20 percent larger than on Instagram for the same reason.
- The WhatsApp Business Platform (the API used by brands for templated messages) requires the customer's opt-in; a broadcast to a bought list breaches WhatsApp's business policy and gets numbers banned.

### 3.13 Snapchat in the Gulf

- Snapchat is a primary platform for under-35 audiences in Saudi Arabia and a major one in the UAE and Kuwait; Snap's own marketing figures put its reach among 13 to 34 year olds in Saudi Arabia near 90 percent. Confirm the current figure in Snap's audience tools before quoting it to a client.
- Canvas 1080 x 1920; ads 3 to 180 seconds; brand name 25 characters, headline 34 (file 26, verified on Snapchat for Business). Safe zone working default 150 px top and bottom, more for collection ads.
- What works: augmented reality lenses for beauty and fashion try-on, Spotlight (Snapchat's short-video feed) for reach, Snap Map for venue discovery in Dubai and Riyadh, and Ramadan and Eid content, when Gulf usage patterns shift late into the night.
- Craft: Snapchat's grammar is the most informal of the vertical platforms; a polished brand film needs a native first second (a person, a phone-shot moment) to survive the swipe.

## 4. The luxury social problem

### 4.1 Why most luxury social looks cheap

Luxury is built on scarcity, distance and restraint; feeds reward frequency, proximity and noise. Jean-Noël Kapferer and Vincent Bastien's "anti-laws of marketing" (The Luxury Strategy, 2009) put it as a rule: luxury does not sell, it is desired; a channel built to push volume works against that unless it is used against its grain. The five failure modes the house sees in Indian and Gulf luxury accounts:

1. Over-posting. Daily posts to "stay in the algorithm" dilute the grid and train followers to scroll past. Reach per post falls as frequency rises past what the account can make well.
2. Stock and borrowed imagery. Library images of generic interiors, generic models, generic sunsets, and since 2024 generic AI renders (design-taste, section 3).
3. Selling. Prices in the image, "Book now" cards, festive discount graphics, countdown stickers on every Story. Luxury that asks reads as discounted.
4. The in-app look. Default text styles, trending audio the brand cannot license, karaoke captions, reaction faces, green-screen formats.
5. Event photography as content. The launch-night photo wall, the celebrity with the step-and-repeat banner, forty near-identical frames. It records that something happened; it gives no one a reason to watch.

### 4.2 The models that work

| Model | How it works | Reference | Fits Mask360 clients |
|---|---|---|---|
| The exit | Leave or nearly leave social; let press, partners and fans carry the image | Bottega Veneta, January 2021 | Only a house with existing fame |
| The editorial grid | The account runs as a magazine: commissioned photography, a stable art direction, captions as writing | Aesop, Loewe | Heritage hotels, jewellers, fashion |
| The film still | Every post is a frame from a film or composed as one: aspect, grade, grain and stillness of cinema | Aman, The Row | Palace and resort hotels, property |
| The campaign drop | Silence, then a concentrated drop of a campaign (film, stills, Stories, creators) over 7 to 14 days, then silence | Jacquemus launches, fashion houses at show time | Collections, launches, openings |
| The quiet account | Very low cadence, one strong post every week or two, nothing in between but Stories | The Row | Ultra-high-end jewellery, private members' clubs, residences |
| Founder-led | The founder's own account carries opinion and process; the brand account stays clean | Rhode and Hailey Bieber; Simon Porte Jacquemus | Agencies, chef-led restaurants, designer labels, Mask360 itself |

House position: most Mask360 clients run the editorial grid with campaign drops, plus a founder-led layer where a founder exists. The exit is recommended to no current client, because it presumes a fame the brand would be spending.

### 4.3 Stills versus video

| Choose stills when | Choose video when |
|---|---|
| The subject is an object whose value is detail and stillness (a ring, a plate, a room at dawn) | The value is in motion, light change or sound (a pour, a fabric, a waterfall pool, a chef's hands) |
| The post is for the grid's composition or for Pinterest | The post is for reach to non-followers (Reels and Shorts are the recommendation surfaces) |
| The photographer's eye is the asset | A person speaking is the asset |
| The account needs a typographic or editorial carousel | The idea needs time: a reveal, a before and after, a process |

Both, always, from the same shoot (5.6). A still that moves for 3 seconds (a cinemagraph: a still photograph in which one element moves in a loop) is a strong grid tile and a strong Reel cover.

### 4.4 The 70 to 30 visual to text house rule

The house layer sets 70 percent visual to 30 percent text (design-taste, section 7). On social that means: in a carousel of ten slides, at most three are typographic; in a Reel, type occupies the screen for at most 30 percent of the running time, measured in seconds of on-screen copy; and no single frame is more than about 30 percent type by area unless it is a typographic card by design. The reason: the picture carries the luxury signal and the type points at it. When the ratio inverts, the post is a slide deck.

### 4.5 The one post a day myth

The myth: the algorithm punishes accounts that do not post daily. What the platforms state: recommendations are predicted per post and per viewer (1.9); Instagram's April 2026 originality rule judges the share of recent posts that are original, which a daily schedule of reposts fails; TikTok states that past high-performing videos are not a direct factor. More posts produce more total reach because each post is a lottery ticket, and each weak post also trains followers to skip and lowers the grid. Les Binet and Peter Field's IPA work (The Long and the Short of It, 2013) found that brand effects build from emotional, memorable work over long periods; the house reads that as the case against filling a calendar with weak executions.

House position: post as often as the account can make posts that pass the checklist in section 10.1, and no more.

### 4.6 Cadence by brand tier

Working cadences per month on Instagram, set by the house and adjusted per client after 90 days of data:

| Tier | Grid posts (Reels, carousels, stills) | Stories | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Heritage maison or ultra-luxury jeweller | 4 to 8 | Event-led, 0 to 10 | Campaign drops around collections; silence between |
| Palace or resort hotel | 8 to 12 | 15 to 30 | One series per season; guest Stories reshared with permission |
| Premium beauty and fashion | 12 to 20 | 30 to 60 | Product launches as drops; creator content via Collab |
| Fine dining and F&B | 12 to 16 | 30 to 60 | Stories carry the daily specials; the grid carries the room, the chef and the plate |
| Luxury real estate | 6 to 10 | 10 to 20 | Construction milestones as series; every post rights-cleared and compliant with RERA (India) or Dubai Land Department advertising permits |
| Agency (Mask360) | 8 to 12 | 10 to 30 | Work drops after client clearance; Kaavish's personal account on its own rhythm |

Real estate note: in India, the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 requires a registered project's RERA registration number on advertisements, and in Dubai the Dubai Land Department and RERA (Dubai's Real Estate Regulatory Agency) require an advertising permit, issued through the Trakheesi system, for property marketing; both apply to social posts that market a project.

### 4.7 The Mask360 agency account as a case

Two accounts, two jobs:
- The agency account (Mask360). mask360-copywriting, Mode 1: Mask360 is the hero, the property or brand is the proof; copy positions the agency's eye and never explains what the video shows ("WE MADE / JAIPUR FEEL IT", "THIS IS / OUR WORK"). Format: the split screen Reel (2.7), the editorial carousel of a shoot, and the occasional typographic carousel of the agency's point of view. Grid planned as a portfolio: every tile a different client category, every row a triptych. Work appears only after the client has cleared it.
- Kaavish's personal account. kaavish-personal-ig governs it entirely: one continuous take, a parked car or small fixed space, natural light, a lav mic, one static overlay line in the top third, a seven-beat spoken structure, no caption or hashtags unless he asks. Do not load mask360-copywriting for it.

Why both: the agency account shows the work; the founder account carries the opinion and the trust. Brand owners in India and the Gulf buy from a person they have heard think. The two accounts cross-reference each other through Collab posts on launches and through the bio.

## 5. Content systems

### 5.1 Pillars and series

A content pillar is one of three to five subjects an account returns to, chosen because each serves a business goal. A series is a named, recurring format inside a pillar, with a fixed structure the audience learns to recognise.

Example for a Jaipur palace hotel:

| Pillar | Goal | Series | Cadence |
|---|---|---|---|
| The house | Desire for the place | "Rooms at the hour": one room filmed at its best hour of light, 12 seconds, looped | Fortnightly |
| The hands | Craft and authenticity | "Made here": an artisan of the property (block printer, gardener, chef) at work, 30 seconds, sound on | Monthly |
| The table | F&B revenue | "One plate": a single dish from pass to table, 15 seconds | Fortnightly |
| The city | Reasons to travel | Editorial carousel of the city through a guest's day | Monthly |
| The guest | Social proof, rights-cleared | Guest Stories reshared with permission; creator Collabs | Weekly in Stories |

Why series work: a recognisable format lowers the production cost of each post, sets an expectation that viewers return for, and makes testing possible because one variable changes per episode.

### 5.2 Formats as repeatable systems

Each series is documented as a one-page format sheet: duration, aspect, number of shots, shot list by position (opening shot type, middle, end), type positions and sizes, sound rule, caption structure, cover rule, and the one variable that changes per episode. The format sheet is the system; the edit project file with locked type layers and the brand preset in Edits or Premiere is its tooling. A new editor should be able to make episode 12 to the standard of episode 1 from the sheet alone.

### 5.3 Batch production days

A batch day is one shoot day that produces a month or more of content for a series. The economics: a crew day in Mumbai or Dubai costs the same whether it produces one film or thirty assets, so the shot list is written for the month. Rules:
- Shoot every series episode for the month on the same day, by location, to keep light consistent.
- Shoot verticals natively for the vertical series (6.1) and stills between takes.
- Wardrobe and styling changes to make episodes feel like different days.
- A one-hour block for Stories material: unpolished, phone-shot, by the social lead.
- A one-hour block for the founder or chef pieces to camera.

### 5.4 Shoot to post pipeline

| Stage | What happens | Owner | Time |
|---|---|---|---|

### 5.6 Repurposing a hero shoot into 30 days

From one two-day hero shoot for a resort (one hero film, stills, verticals shot natively, behind the scenes), a 30 day plan that never repeats an asset:

| Asset | Count | Source | Placement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hero film 60 to 90 s, 16:9 | 1 | Hero edit | YouTube, website, LinkedIn, Instagram for TV |
| Cutdowns 30 s and 15 s, 9:16 and 4:5 | 4 | Hero edit, reframed or vertical takes | Reels, paid Meta, YouTube Shorts |
| 6 s bumper | 1 | Hero edit | YouTube |
| Loop Reels 7 to 12 s | 6 | Vertical takes | Reels, Shorts, TikTok (UAE) |
| Editorial carousels | 3 | Stills | Instagram, LinkedIn as document posts |
| Single-image grid posts | 4 | Hero stills | Instagram, Threads |
| Behind-the-scenes Reel | 1 | BTS footage | Reels, Stories, agency account via Collab |
| Stories sequences | 8 | Stills, outtakes, BTS, polls | Stories, WhatsApp Status, broadcast channel |
| Pins | 20 to 40 | Stills and verticals | Pinterest, over the following months |
| WhatsApp channel posts | 4 | Stills and Reels | WhatsApp |

The order matters: the hero lands first (day 1 to 3) with the strongest cutdown, the carousels and loops follow to extend it, the BTS and Stories close the month. The second half of the month introduces a series episode so the account does not read as one campaign.

### 5.7 Evergreen versus timely

Evergreen content is content whose value does not decay with date (a room, a craft, a recipe, a place); timely content is tied to a moment (an opening, a festival, a trend). Evergreen posts compound: they keep earning saves, Pinterest traffic and search views on YouTube. Timely posts spike and die. House ratio for luxury accounts: about 70 percent evergreen, 30 percent timely, with every timely post designed to stay on the grid after the moment (no date in the image, the date in the caption).

### 5.8 UGC and creator content direction

UGC (user-generated content) is content made by customers or creators about a brand; creator content is UGC that is commissioned and paid for. Direction rules:
- Brief creators with the same discipline as a shoot (art-direction skill, the shoot brief and the "what we are not doing" list), shortened to one page: the single idea, three reference posts, the tropes to avoid, the mandatory shots (product in hand, the place by name), the forbidden (competitor products, prices, unverified claims), the format and length, and the disclosure.
- Leave the creator's voice intact. The value of creator content is that it reads as the creator's; a script read word for word reads as an ad and loses that value.
- Rights. Agree in writing before production: organic posting on the creator's account; reposting on the brand's accounts (Collab or reshare); paid usage (the brand runs the creator's post or file as an ad); Spark Ads or partnership ads authorisation (running the post from the creator's handle); duration (30, 90 or 365 days are the usual terms); territory (India, GCC, global); exclusivity (no competitor work in the category for a period); raw footage delivery. Paid usage and exclusivity are priced separately from the post. In India, Section 17(b) of the Copyright Act, 1957 makes the person who commissions a photograph or film for valuable consideration its first owner absent an agreement to the contrary; creator contracts commonly reserve ownership to the creator and license usage, so the house always states ownership and licence explicitly.
- Disclosure in India (ASCI guidelines for influencer advertising in digital media, read from ASCI's PDF on 29 October 2026): a material connection (payment, free product, any relationship) must be disclosed upfront and prominently; a label only in the bio, at the end, behind "more" or among hashtags does not count. Permitted labels: Advertisement, Ad, Sponsored, Collaboration, Partnership, Employee, Free gift, Affiliate, Instagram's "Paid Partnership" tag and YouTube's "Includes Paid Promotion" tag, in English or the language of the ad. On picture or video posts without accompanying text (Stories, Snapchat) the label is superimposed: at least 3 seconds for videos of 15 seconds or less; one third of the length for videos over 15 seconds and under 2 minutes; the entire section mentioning the brand for videos of 2 minutes or more. Live streams announce the label at the start and end. Audio discloses at the start, the end and around every break. Virtual influencers must also disclose that they are not human. Advertiser and influencer share responsibility; the advertiser must ask for edits or deletion when a disclosure is missing.
- India, statutory: the Department of Consumer Affairs issued "Endorsements Know-hows!" for influencers in January 2023 under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019, whose Section 21 empowers the Central Consumer Protection Authority to penalise misleading advertisements (up to 10 lakh rupees, up to 50 lakh for repeat offences) and to bar endorsers.
- UAE: the UAE Media Council regulates influencer advertising under Federal Decree-Law No. 55 of 2023 on media regulation; in 2025 the council announced an advertiser permit for individuals who publish paid promotional content. The council's website returned HTTP 503 during this check, so confirm the permit's fee, validity and start date on uaemc.gov.ae before contracting a UAE creator, and ask every UAE creator for the permit number in the contract.
- Saudi Arabia: the General Authority for Media Regulation's Mawthooq licence is required for influencers who advertise (reported); confirm current terms before contracting a Riyadh creator.

### 5.9 Influencer tiers and vetting

| Tier | Followers | Use for luxury | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nano | Under 10,000 | Local credibility, community, events, hyperlocal F&B | Inconsistent quality; brief tightly |
| Micro | 10,000 to 100,000 | The best cost per engaged viewer; niche authority (a Jaipur heritage writer, a Dubai fine-dining critic) | Audience geography |
| Macro | 100,000 to 1 million | Reach with a point of view | Fake followers, brand conflicts |
| Mega and celebrity | Over 1 million | Launch moments, campaigns | Cost, control, exclusivity, reputational risk |

Vetting checklist before any contract: audience geography and language split (a Dubai hotel needs GCC and Indian diaspora viewers, verified in the creator's Insights screenshots; a media kit alone is not evidence); follower growth curve (sudden steps indicate bought followers); comment quality (real sentences from real accounts); past sponsored posts and their disclosure (ASCI compliance history); category conflicts in the last 6 to 12 months; quality of the creator's own photography and editing at full size; previous controversies; and, in the UAE, the advertiser permit.

### 5.10 Community management tone

- Voice: the brand's own, written down with examples of replies (warm, brief, specific, signed by a first name where the brand allows). No emojis in replies (house rule), no exclamation marks, no "Thank you for your kind words!!" stock replies.
- Speed: comments in the first 60 minutes after posting get answered within that hour, because early replies extend the conversation that ranking reads (1.9, X and Instagram comment signals); DMs within the same business day.
- Language: reply in the language of the comment. Hindi in Devanagari or Roman Hinglish as the commenter wrote it; Arabic in Arabic, by a native writer.
- Escalation: complaints move to DM within one reply, with a named person; nothing about a booking, a price or a defect is discussed in public. Legal or safety issues go to the client the same hour.
- Moderation: hide spam and abuse; never delete legitimate criticism; keep a log.

## 6. Craft of the short film for social

### 6.1 Shooting vertical natively versus reframing

The arithmetic (26-platform-and-format-specs-2026.md, section 5.5): a 9:16 crop from a UHD 16:9 master is 1215 x 2160, larger than the 1080 x 1920 delivery; a 9:16 crop from a 1080p master is 608 x 1080 and is upscaled and soft. A 16:9 cut from a vertical master keeps 31.6 percent of the picture.

| Approach | When | Cost | Craft risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shoot UHD or larger landscape, reframe to 9:16 | Hero films with a vertical cutdown; budgets for one camera | Lowest | Vertical framing is a crop: compositions designed for 16:9 lose their geometry, and close-ups become extreme close-ups |
| Shoot vertical natively (camera rotated, or a second body) | Vertical-first series, Reels and TikTok as the main deliverable, pieces to camera | A second setup or a second camera | 16:9 versions become split screens or letterboxed |
| Open gate (recording the sensor's full area) or a large sensor (6K, 8K, 3:2 or 17:9 gate) with both crops marked on the monitor | Campaigns that need hero 16:9 and premium 9:16 | Higher data and post cost | Two framings compromise each other unless the director composes for the overlap |

House rule: for any series whose main deliverable is vertical, shoot vertical. Framing is composition, and a crop of a landscape composition is a different and weaker picture. Mark the 4:5 and 9:16 protect lines (monitor guides showing the area each crop keeps) for every landscape shoot so the reframes are planned.

### 6.2 Holds for text

A text hold is a shot left long enough, with enough quiet space, for a card to be read over it. Plan them on the shot list: for each card, a shot of at least the hold time in 2.4 (1.5 seconds plus), with a composed area of low detail where the type will sit (sky, wall, shadow, water, a defocused background), and slow or no movement in that area. Shooting holds costs seconds on set; fixing a missing hold in the edit costs a gradient, a blur plate or a slowed shot, all visible.

### 6.3 The loop shot

Shoot loops on purpose. Lock the camera or repeat the move precisely (a slider or gimbal with a programmed move, a motion-control head for products), shoot the action through its whole cycle twice, and record clean ambience for 30 seconds for the audio join. Natural loops: a pour that refills, a door, a swing, a fountain, waves, a turntable, a lift between floors, a hand turning a ring. The editor finds the join frame by matching position and motion direction (1.5).

### 6.4 B-roll rhythm

B-roll is supporting footage cut over or between primary action and speech. On social, b-roll carries the rehooks: each b-roll shot is a new piece of information. File 16, section 3.1 sets a social cutdown's average shot length (ASL, the duration of a film divided by its number of shots) at 0.8 to 1.5 seconds. Rhythm rules: vary shot length (1.0, 1.4, 0.8, 2.0 seconds reads alive; 1.2 seconds every time reads mechanical); cut on motion so the eye carries across; let one shot in every five or six run long (3 seconds or more) as a breath.

### 6.5 The cut every 1 to 2 seconds versus the long take as luxury

This is contested, and both positions have evidence.
- Fast cutting: the creator-economy position, argued by short-form editors and growth coaches, is that a new shot every 1 to 2 seconds holds the decaying attention of a feed viewer; James Cutting and colleagues (Psychological Science, 2010) showed Hollywood shot lengths shortening across 1935 to 2005, and social editing has continued the curve.
- The long take: the luxury position, visible in the Aman, The Row and Aesop accounts and in Kaavish's own one-take talking-head format (kaavish-personal-ig), is that stillness is the rarest signal in the feed and that luxury is the confidence to hold. A single continuous 12 second shot of light crossing a room is a pattern interrupt among cut-every-second Reels.

House position: the long take for brand and luxury hospitality accounts, with movement inside the shot (light, people, camera) and one rehook device per 5 to 8 seconds supplied by type, sound or a reveal inside the frame; the fast cut for fashion, events and F&B energy pieces, with rhythm variation (6.4). Test both on the same subject as trial reels when a client doubts it.

### 6.6 Transitions that are not whip pans

The whip pan, the zoom punch and the spin are the transitions of 2018 to 2024 creator content. Transitions that read as authored:
- Match cut on shape: a round plate to a round window, a stone's facet to a building's facade.
- Match cut on motion: a hand moving right in one shot continues right in the next.
- Match cut on light: a candle flame to the sun, a reflection to a pool.
- Object wipe: a figure, a column or a door crossing the lens hides the cut.
- Focus pull: rack to soft, cut, rack from soft into the next shot.
- Sound bridge: the next shot's sound starts 6 to 12 frames before its picture (a J-cut, named for the shape the audio and picture tracks make on the timeline), so the viewer is pulled forward.
- Hard cut on a sound event: the cleanest transition, used most.

### 6.7 Sound design for feeds

15-lighting-and-sound.md, section 14 governs: sound-off first, a real sound in the first second, the licensing limits of trending audio for business accounts, original audio, VO styles, and the silence move. Two feed-specific additions:
- Meta's Reels ads guide recommends original audio or royalty-free music from Meta's Sound Collection over licensed music (read 29 October 2026). For organic and paid alike, the house uses original audio or cleared library music, because a muted Reel loses its distribution and its spend.
- Original audio is also a ranking asset on Instagram: going to the audio page is one of the predicted actions for Reels (1.9). A distinctive original sound (a hotel's own ambience, a jeweller's clasp) can travel as other accounts reuse it.

### 6.8 The first frame as thumbnail and cover frame selection

The first frame does three jobs: it is the autoplay frame in the feed, the default cover, and on Shorts and TikTok often the frame in the grid. Design it as a still: composed, sharp, in motion (1.1), with the subject inside the central 3:4 crop (1080 x 1440) and any title inside the universal box.

Cover selection, when the cover is chosen separately:
- Choose a frame with the subject sharp and centred in the 3:4 crop, eyes open if a face, motion blur absent.
- Or upload a designed cover: a still from the shoot, graded as the film, with a title in the house face at grid-readable size (a cap height of 60 px or more on 1080 wide survives the grid's thumbnail scale).
- Check the cover in the grid preview beside the two neighbouring tiles before posting (3.4).
- On YouTube Shorts, the cover can be chosen in the upload flow; on TikTok, the cover frame is chosen from the video and text can be added in the app (in the house face only if rendered in the edit).

### 6.9 Captions as copy

The post caption (the text under the post, distinct from on-screen captions) follows mask360-copywriting. Its structure for client Instagram captions: line 1 the hook with no preamble; lines 2 to 4 a micro-story or sensory description; line 5 a reframe or quiet punchline; the call to action subtle ("Link in bio." or the location tag). For the agency account: a hook (something Mask360 believes or did), proof, point of view, and a call to action or silence. Hard limits from the same skill: no caption longer than 6 lines for luxury brands, no "we are thrilled to announce", no SaaS calls to action.

Mechanics:
- The first 125 characters show before "more" on most Meta placements; the Reels ad overlay truncates at 44 (Meta's ads guide). Line 1 must stand alone at 44 characters.
- Hashtags: Meta's ads guide still listed 30 per post when read on 28 October 2026 (file 26); Instagram was reported in December 2025 to be capping organic posts at 5 hashtags, unverified in this check. House practice is 0 to 3, specific (place and category), at the end, because hashtags have little discovery value in 2026 and many hashtags read as a growth hack.
- Location tag on every place-based post; it is a search and discovery surface in itself.
- Alt text: write a factual description in Instagram's advanced settings (about 100 characters, file 26); it serves screen readers and gives the platform a text description of the image.
- Disclosure: the paid-partnership tool plus the ASCI label in line 1 for any material connection (5.8).

## 7. AI content on social as of October 2026

### 7.1 Platform labels

| Platform | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Meta (Instagram, Facebook, Threads) | "AI info" label (renamed from "Made with AI" on 1 July 2024), applied when industry signals in the file (C2PA, IPTC metadata) indicate AI or when the uploader discloses; Meta AI's own photorealistic images are marked "Imagined with AI" | 22-ai-era-craft-and-generative-tools.md, section 7.6, verified there |
| YouTube | Creators must disclose realistic content that is AI-generated or meaningfully altered: a real person appearing to say or do something they did not, altered footage of real events or places, realistic scenes that never happened, and music that is the main focus. Photorealistic AI content and AI-generated music may be labelled on the player; other disclosed content in the expanded description; videos on sensitive topics get a more prominent label. Exempt: clearly unrealistic content (animation), minor aesthetic edits (colour, lighting filters, background blur, beauty filters), caption creation, and cloning one's own voice for voice-overs or dubs | YouTube blog, 18 March 2024; YouTube Help answer 14328491; both read 29 October 2026 |
| TikTok | Creators must label AI-generated content that contains realistic images, audio or video (policy announced 19 September 2023), using TikTok's label or another clear disclosure; since 9 May 2024 TikTok auto-labels content carrying C2PA Content Credentials from other platforms | TikTok newsroom, read 29 October 2026; file 22, section 7.6 |

C2PA (the Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity) defines the signed provenance record that platforms read; Content Credentials is its consumer name (file 22, section 7.7). Most platforms strip some metadata on upload, so the visible label in the caption is the one that reliably survives.

### 7.2 Disclosure norms and law

- India: since 20 February 2026, the IT Rules amendment on synthetically generated information (SGI: content artificially created or altered so that it appears real and depicts a person or event in a way likely to be indistinguishable from a real one) requires a prominent visible label and embedded provenance metadata, with exclusions for routine editing such as colour correction (file 22, section 7.6). A hybrid film with a generated sky that depicts a real place as real is SGI, and the house labels it.
- India, ASCI: a virtual influencer must disclose that the audience is not interacting with a real human being (ASCI guidelines, read 29 October 2026); generated guests, results and testimonials are never presented as real.
- EU: Article 50 of the AI Act applies from 2 August 2026 to deepfakes and machine-readable marking (file 22). Any Mask360 campaign targeting EU audiences (a Dubai hotel selling to Germany) is labelled as if the house were the deployer.
- UAE: no AI-specific disclosure rule in advertising regulation was found as of October 2026; the media law and consumer protection rules on misleading content apply (file 22). The house labels anyway.

### 7.3 The tells and how audiences react

The tells of generated video in a feed (file 22, section 5, has the full list and fixes): skin with no pores, hands and jewellery that change between frames, text that almost reads, physics that floats (fabric, water, hair), light with no source, a camera move no rig could make, and the median composition of a model's training data. Audiences in 2025 and 2026 found these tells and named them publicly: Coca-Cola's AI-made "Holidays are coming" films (November 2024) drew wide criticism; the Guess advertisement in the August 2025 issue of Vogue, featuring an AI-generated model, drew a backlash against both the brand and the magazine; Toys R Us's film made with OpenAI's Sora (June 2024) was mocked for its uncanny faces. For luxury the reputational risk is asymmetric: the gain from a generated post is a lower production cost the audience never sees; the loss is a public reading of the brand as fake, which goes to the heart of a price premium built on craft. Platform policy has moved the same way: Instagram's April 2026 originality rules and YouTube's 2025 tightening of its monetisation policy on mass-produced, repetitive content (renamed "inauthentic content", reported July 2025) both reduce the reach of low-effort generated media.

### 7.4 Where AI content works and where it damages a luxury brand

Works (disclosed where the law or the platform requires it):
- Motion from stills: a subtle camera move, a curtain moving, steam over a cup, generated from a real photograph of the real place or product, for a grid tile or a Reel cover. The subject stays real; only motion is generated.
- Previs: animatics and shot planning for a shoot (file 22, section 6), never published.
- Cleanup and extension: sensor dust, cables, an aspect expansion for a 9:16 from a 4:5 still, never on a hallmark, a label, a product detail or a building (file 22).
- Localisation: subtitles, translation drafts checked by native writers, and dubbing a founder's own voice with consent (YouTube exempts cloning one's own voice for dubs from disclosure).
- Abstract and clearly unreal content: typographic motion, abstract textures, illustration styles that no viewer mistakes for a photograph.

Damages:
- Generated people presented as guests, customers, models or artisans.
- Generated products, jewellery, food or rooms presented as the real thing; a generated view from a suite that does not have it.
- Generated "behind the scenes" or "made by hand" content.
- Any generated content on an account whose promise is craft, without a label.

## 8. Ad creative craft and specs

Media strategy, budgets, audiences and bidding belong to the performance-marketer skill. This section is the creative side: what the asset must be for the placement to work. Specs carry their status in 26-platform-and-format-specs-2026.md, section 3.

### 8.1 Meta: Reels, Stories and feed

- Two masters cover almost every placement: 4:5 at 1440 x 1800 for feed and 9:16 at 1440 x 2560 for Reels and Stories (Meta's ads guide, read 29 October 2026). 1:1 at 1440 x 1440 is derived from the 4:5 centre for the placements that still crop square; hold the subject in that central square.
- Placement asset customisation: Ads Manager lets one ad carry a different asset per placement. Use it every time: the 4:5 asset in feed and the native vertical in Reels and Stories. Letting Meta auto-crop one asset to every placement is the most common reason a luxury ad looks wrong in Stories.
- Safe zones for Reels: keep the top 14 percent, bottom 35 percent and 6 percent each side free of text and logos (verified). Primary text on Reels truncates at 44 characters.
- Sound: Meta recommends sound, using original audio or Sound Collection music, and recommends captions; it advises avoiding Reels published before 15 October 2021 as ads (Reels ads guide, read 29 October 2026).
- The 20 percent text rule: until 2020 Meta reduced delivery for images with more than 20 percent text by area; the rule was removed in 2020 and the guide no longer states a ratio, but text-heavy images still tend to earn lower estimated action rates. The house keeps image text to one line and lets primary text carry the copy (file 26, section 3.1).
- The 3 second hook: the first 3 seconds carry most of the value (1.1), so the brand or product appears by second 2, in motion, and the hook line reads by second 1.
- Andromeda: Meta's retrieval system for ads, announced on 2 December 2024, narrows tens of millions of candidate ads to a few thousand per impression; Meta reported a 6 percent recall improvement and an 8 percent ads quality improvement on selected segments, and described the system as built to handle the growing volume of creatives from generative AI tools (Meta engineering blog, read 29 October 2026). The creative consequence, as practitioners read it: the system matches distinct creatives to distinct people, so an account needs several genuinely different concepts (different hooks, people, settings, formats), and ten near-identical variants of one concept count as one.
- Advantage+ creative enhancements: Meta can automatically adjust brightness and contrast, add music, animate images, expand images and vary text. Review each enhancement per ad and switch off any that alters the graded image, adds music outside the brand's library, or rewrites copy, because luxury creative is approved as a finished object.
- Creative fatigue: the decline in performance as an audience sees the same ad repeatedly. The performance-marketer skill sets the house trigger at a frequency above 3.5 (average impressions per person); a refresh means a new hook or a new first frame at minimum, a new concept at best. Working refresh cadence for always-on luxury campaigns: a new concept every 3 to 4 weeks, new hooks on winning concepts every 1 to 2 weeks, adjusted to the frequency trigger.

### 8.7 The creative testing method

The performance-marketer skill sets the order of variables by impact: hook or first frame, then format, then copy angle, then call to action; one variable at a time; at least 7 days before judging; at least 50 link clicks or 1,000 impressions before comparing; kill at twice the target cost per acquisition without conversion.

The metrics, their formulas and house starting thresholds (calibrate to the account's trailing 90 day median once it exists):

| Metric | Formula | What it diagnoses | House starting threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thumb-stop ratio (hook rate) | 3 second video plays / impressions | The first frame and the first second | Above 30 percent (performance-marketer) |
| Hold rate | ThruPlays / 3 second video plays | Whether the body of the film keeps the viewers the hook won | Account median; investigate any variant 20 percent below it |
| Average watch time and percentage viewed | Total watch time / plays | Pacing and rehooks | Above the account median for that length |
| Click-through rate (CTR, link) | Link clicks / impressions | The promise and the ask | Above 1.5 percent general, 0.8 percent acceptable for luxury with tight targeting (performance-marketer) |
| Cost per acquisition (CPA) | Spend / conversions | The whole chain including the landing page | Set per client against margin |
| Frequency | Impressions / reach | Fatigue | Refresh above 3.5 |

Test design for creative: a hook test keeps the body of the film identical and swaps the first 3 seconds (3 to 5 hooks); a first-frame test swaps only the opening frame or cover; a format test runs the same concept as a Reel, a carousel and a still. Read thumb-stop ratio first for hook tests and hold rate first for body edits, then CTR and CPA for the winners. Organic pre-testing: run hook variants as Instagram trial reels (3.1) before spending.

## 9. Analytics that matter

### 9.1 Reach, impressions and views

Reach is the number of unique accounts that saw a post; impressions (or views) count every time it was shown or played, including repeats. Reach measures audience; views measure exposure. Instagram moved its Insights to views as the headline count across formats (reported, 2024 to 2025); YouTube Shorts views count every start and replay since 31 March 2025 (1.2). House rule: report reach and engaged views to clients, because both are robust to the 2025 counting changes, and report views only alongside them.

### 9.2 3 second views and skip rate

3 second views (Meta) and the swipe-away share on Instagram and Shorts measure the hook. Report the thumb-stop ratio per Reel and track it per series: a series whose hook rate falls over episodes has a tired opening.

### 9.3 Average watch time and completion rate

Average watch time is total watch time divided by plays; completion rate is the share of plays that reach the end. For films under 15 seconds, completion above 50 percent and average percentage viewed above 100 percent (replays) are strong; for 30 to 60 second films, compare with the account's own same-length median, because completion falls with length on every platform. YouTube's typical retention comparison against the 10 latest videos of similar length is the model (1.3).

### 9.4 Reading a retention graph

Read in this order: the height at 3 seconds (the hook); the slope from 3 to 10 seconds (the promise); dips (find the shot, the line or the repeat at that timecode); spikes (find what was rewatched and repeat the device); the exit ramp (move the payoff before it). Then compare with the typical curve for the account. Annotate the edit timeline with the dips and spikes and keep the annotations with the format sheet (5.2), so the next episode starts from the lesson.

### 9.5 Saves, shares and sends as the 2026 signals

Sends (shares by direct message) and shares are the strongest positive signals Instagram, TikTok and X publish (1.9); saves signal future intent (a recipe, a hotel, a ring for later) and power Pinterest. Report them per reach: sends per reach and saves per reach, as percentages, because raw counts scale with reach and hide quality. A post with fewer views and twice the sends per reach is the better post.

### 9.6 Profile visits, follows per reach and link clicks

- Profile visits per reach: whether a post makes people want the account; the grid (3.4) converts the visit.
- Follows per reach (follows from a post divided by its reach, or follows per 1,000 accounts reached): the cleanest measure of whether non-followers found the account worth keeping.
- Link clicks: link sticker taps on Stories, bio link clicks, Pinterest outbound clicks, YouTube end-screen clicks; tag every link with UTM parameters (the source, medium and campaign tags read by web analytics) so web analytics attribute the visit.

### 9.7 What to ignore

- Likes: the weakest positive signal on every platform that publishes weights (1.9), and on Instagram hideable from public view. Report them as context only.
- Follower count as a KPI: TikTok states that follower count is not a direct ranking factor; recommendation-led platforms distribute to non-followers. Follower count is a reputation number and an input to creator pricing, and it can be bought.
- Engagement rate by followers: divides interactions by a number that does not determine reach; use rates per reach.
- Views without reach, and impressions without frequency.

## 10. Checklist and specs table

### 10.1 Checklist for a premium social post

Idea and taste
1. The post has one idea, writable in one sentence, and it belongs to a named series or campaign (5.1).
2. Cover the logo: the post could only be this brand (design-taste, section 4).
3. No cheap tells: no in-app text styles, no karaoke captions, no trending audio the account cannot license, no stock, no listicle with icons (design-taste, section 3; sections 2.8 and 3.3 here).
4. Visual to text is 70 to 30 or more visual (4.4).

Attention
5. Frame one is in motion, composed, and readable as a still (1.1, 6.8).
6. One hook, from one family in the taxonomy (1.8), landing inside the first second.
7. A rehook every 5 to 8 seconds, each a different device (1.4).
8. The payoff lands before the end card; loops have no end card (1.3, 1.5).
9. The film is fully understood muted (1.7).

Type
10. Every card is one thought, at most 4 words a side for split screen, in the house face or the client's licensed face (2.6, 2.7, 2.9).
11. Font sizes at or above 3 percent of frame height for copy and never under 48 px on 1920 (2.1).
12. Every card holds at least 1.5 seconds and 0.3 to 0.4 seconds a word (2.4).
13. Every word inside the universal safe box: 290 top, 672 bottom, 65 left, 200 right on 1080 x 1920 (2.3).
14. Contrast at least 4.5:1 against the brightest frame under the text (2.5).

Platform
15. Exported at the platform's recommended size, at the shot frame rate, loudness per file 26, section 5.4.
16. Cover designed for the 3:4 grid crop and checked in a 3 x 3 grid preview (3.4, 6.8).
17. Each placement has its own asset: 4:5 in feed, 9:16 in Reels and Stories (8.1).
18. The final file viewed on an iOS and an Android handset after upload, including WhatsApp where it will be sent (3.12).

Words and law
19. Caption follows mask360-copywriting: hook in the first 44 characters, no more than 6 lines for luxury, quiet call to action (6.9).
20. Disclosure for any material connection, upfront, with the ASCI label in India and the paid-partnership tool; the UAE advertiser permit confirmed for UAE creators (5.8).
21. AI labels applied where any realistic content is generated or meaningfully altered, per platform and per jurisdiction (7.1, 7.2).
22. Rights confirmed for every person, place, track, font and creator asset; client clearance obtained before agency reposting (5.8, 4.7).
23. Alt text and location tag written; hashtags 0 to 3 (6.9).

After posting
24. First 60 minutes of comments answered in the brand voice (5.10).
25. Metrics read at 24 hours and 7 days: thumb-stop ratio, average percentage viewed, sends and saves per reach, follows per reach (9).

## Terms introduced

- 3-second video plays: Meta's count of plays lasting at least 3 seconds, or nearly the full length of a shorter video.
- A/B test (YouTube): YouTube's test of up to 3 titles, thumbnails or pairs, won by the highest watch time.
- Absolute retention: the share of a video's own viewers still watching at each moment.
- Ads in Status: WhatsApp's paid placement for businesses inside Status, announced 16 June 2025.
- Advantage+ creative enhancements: Meta's automatic edits to an ad (brightness, music, animation, expansion, text variation).
- Advertiser permit (UAE): the UAE Media Council's permit for individuals who publish paid promotional content, announced in 2025.
- Aggregator: an account that mainly reposts others' content; excluded from Instagram recommendations since April 2026.
- Andromeda: Meta's ad retrieval system, announced December 2024, that narrows candidate ads per impression.
- Average shot length (ASL): film duration divided by the number of shots.
- Average watch time: total watch time divided by plays.
- Batch day: one shoot day planned to produce a month or more of series content.
- Broadcast channel: Instagram's one-to-many chat where followers react and vote but do not reply in the thread.
- B-roll: supporting footage cut over or between primary action and speech.
- C2PA: the Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity, whose signed manifests record an asset's origin and edits.
- Caption (post): the text under a post, distinct from on-screen captions.
- Cinemagraph: a still photograph in which one element moves in a loop.
- Click-through rate (CTR): clicks divided by impressions.
- Close Friends: an Instagram Stories audience restricted to a chosen list.
- Collab post: an Instagram post co-authored by two or more accounts, shown on each profile with shared likes, comments and views.
- Completion rate: the share of plays that reach the end of a video.
- Content pillar: one of three to five subjects an account returns to, each serving a business goal.
- Cost per acquisition (CPA): spend divided by conversions.
- Cover frame: the image chosen to represent a video on the profile grid and in feeds.
- Creative fatigue: declining ad performance as an audience sees the same creative repeatedly.
- Creator content: user-generated content commissioned and paid for by a brand.
- Curiosity gap: the gap between what a viewer knows and wants to know, which drives attention until closed.
- Dips (YouTube): moments in a retention curve where viewers skipped or stopped watching.
- Document post: a LinkedIn post of a PDF or slide file shown as a swipeable carousel.
- DOOH: digital out of home, screens in public places.
- Edits: Instagram's video and carousel editing app, launched 22 April 2025.
- Engaged views: YouTube's Shorts count of views that meet its engagement threshold, used for revenue sharing and eligibility.
- Evergreen content: content whose value does not decay with date.
- FOOH: faux out-of-home, a CGI film that places an impossible object in a real street.
- Format sheet: the one-page specification of a series format that lets any editor reproduce it.
- Frequency: average impressions per person reached.
- Hold rate: ThruPlays divided by 3-second video plays.
- Hook: whatever earns the first 3 seconds of attention.
- Impressions: the count of times content was shown, including repeats.
- In medias res: opening in the middle of the action.
- Intro (YouTube): the share of viewers still watching after the first 30 seconds.
- Invisible loop: a loop whose join between last and first frame cannot be seen.
- J-cut: an edit in which the next shot's sound starts before its picture.
- Karaoke caption: word-by-word highlighted captions synchronised to speech.
- Loop: a film whose last frame cuts back to its first so the replay reads as continuous.
- Macroblocking: the square artefacts of a compressed video frame.
- Material connection: any relationship between a creator and a brand (payment, free product, employment) that requires disclosure.
- Mawthooq: Saudi Arabia's licence for influencers who advertise.
- Open gate: recording the full area of a camera's sensor so several crops can be taken.
- Orienting response: the automatic redirection of attention to a change in stimulus.
- Pattern interrupt: a deliberate break in an established rhythm that resets attention.
- Photo mode: TikTok's swipeable multi-image post with music.
- Placement asset customisation: Meta's option to give each ad placement its own asset.
- Pop caption: captions whose words appear one or two at a time with motion.
- Promoted Channels: WhatsApp's paid visibility for channels in the directory.
- Protect lines: monitor guides showing the area each planned crop keeps.
- Reach: the number of unique accounts that saw content.
- Rehook: a new reason to keep watching placed inside a film.
- Relative retention: YouTube's comparison of a video's retention with videos of similar length across YouTube.
- RERA: India's Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016, and its authorities; in Dubai, the Real Estate Regulatory Agency.
- Retention curve: a plot of the share of starting viewers still watching at each moment.
- Safe zone: the part of the frame no platform interface element covers.
- Sends: shares of a post by direct message.
- Series: a named, recurring format inside a content pillar.
- SGI: synthetically generated information under India's amended IT Rules, 2026, which must carry a visible label and provenance metadata.
- Skip rate: the share of views in which the viewer swipes away inside the first 3 seconds.
- Spark Ads: TikTok's format for running organic posts as ads, the brand's own or a creator's with authorisation.
- Spikes (YouTube): moments in a retention curve that were rewatched or shared.
- Split screen copy: the house overlay format of two halves, at most 4 words each, that land as one thought.
- Static phrase caption: a full phrase that appears, holds and cuts to the next.
- Swipe narrative: a carousel in which each slide continues the image or sentence of the one before.
- Text hold: a shot left long enough, with quiet space, for a card to be read over it.
- Thought Leader Ads: LinkedIn ads that sponsor a member's own post.
- ThruPlay: Meta's count of plays to completion or of at least 15 seconds.
- Thumb stop: the moment a scrolling viewer stops on a post.
- Thumb-stop ratio (hook rate): 3-second video plays divided by impressions.
- Top moments (YouTube): segments of a video where almost no one dropped off.
- Trakheesi: the Dubai Land Department system that issues property advertising permits.
- Trial reel: an Instagram reel shown first to non-followers only, for testing.
- Typical retention (YouTube): a comparison of a video's retention curve with the channel's 10 latest videos of similar length.
- Typographic carousel: a carousel in which set type is the image.
- UGC: user-generated content, made by customers or creators about a brand.
- Universal safe box: the house union of vertical platform safe zones, 290 top, 672 bottom, 65 left, 200 right on 1080 x 1920.
- UTM parameters: source, medium and campaign tags added to a link so web analytics attribute the visit.
- Views (Shorts): since 31 March 2025, every start or replay of a Short, with no minimum watch time.
- Your Algorithm: Instagram's control that shows and lets viewers edit the topics shaping their recommendations.
