## 1. Agency models and the market

### 1.1 The models and what each one sells

| Model | What the client buys | Typical size | Revenue shape | Margin pressure comes from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Full service and 360 | One accountable partner across strategy, creative, media, social, PR (public relations), production | 40 to 2,000 people | Retainers 60 to 80 percent of fees | Overhead of breadth; every discipline carries idle capacity |
| Creative boutique | Ideas and campaigns, led by two or three named creatives | 8 to 40 | Projects 60 to 80 percent | Feast and famine; founder dependence |
| Design studio | Identity, systems, editorial, packaging, environments | 3 to 30 | Projects, some brand guardianship retainers | Long sales cycles; under-scoped rounds |
| Digital and web studio | Sites, products, commerce, experiential web | 5 to 60 | Projects plus maintenance retainers | Engineering estimates that slip; browser and platform churn |
| Production house | Film, stills, post, CGI (computer-generated imagery), sound | 5 to 100 | Per job, cost-plus with a mark-up | Crew and kit utilisation; weather; client approvals on set |
| In-house agency | A client's own creative team | 3 to 300 | Salary cost, no margin | Scope inflation; talent retention; taste drift |
| Hybrid and collective | A small core plus a named freelance bench, assembled per job | 2 to 10 core, 20 to 60 bench | Projects, a few retainers | Bench availability; quality consistency |

The reason the model matters commercially: the model fixes the overhead ratio (overhead is every cost that is not a billable person: rent, software, admin, new business), and overhead ratio fixes the minimum price. A 40-person full service shop in Mumbai carrying 30 to 35 percent overhead cannot profitably quote the price a 6-person studio with 15 percent overhead can. Mask360 runs as a full service house with a boutique-sized senior creative core and a production bench, which is why its pricing must read as premium everywhere: the breadth is paid for by the taste, and the taste is the product (see design-taste, SKILL.md, section 1).

### 1.2 In-housing and the in-housing trend

In-housing is a client building its own creative team to replace or reduce agency spend. The ANA (Association of National Advertisers, the US client body) found 82 percent of its members running an in-house agency in its 2023 report "The Continued Rise of the In-House Agency" (162 respondents), up from 78 percent in 2018, 58 percent in 2013 and 42 percent in 2008; 92 percent still used external agencies as well, and the ANA expects the share to peak at 85 to 90 percent. Indian conglomerates and D2C (direct to consumer) brands followed the same path from 2020: Tata, Reliance Retail, Nykaa, Myntra and the large real estate developers all run sizeable internal studios as of 2026. What in-house teams reliably do well: volume, speed, platform knowledge, product truth. What they reliably cannot do: taste at the level of a dedicated house, a view from outside the building, production at scale, and a senior creative who will tell the CMO (chief marketing officer) no. The commercial consequence: sell what the in-house team cannot make (the idea, the identity, the film, the launch) and offer to run their studio's standards (guidelines, calibration, craft reviews, see section 10) as a retainer, and leave the daily social post with them.

### 1.3 The India market structure

- Networks. WPP (VML, Ogilvy, WPP Media, Design Bridge and Partners), Publicis Groupe (Leo, which merged Leo Burnett and Publicis Worldwide in January 2025, Digitas, Saatchi and Saatchi), Omnicom (which closed its acquisition of Interpublic on 26 November 2025, then retired the DDB, FCB and MullenLowe brands into BBDO, TBWA and McCann with about 4,000 redundancies; Campaign reported FCB Ulka and DDB in India folding into BBDO), Dentsu (Dentsu Creative), Havas (Havas Worldwide India, Havas Media). Networks hold the FMCG (fast-moving consumer goods), auto, telecom and bank retainers that are tendered through procurement, and they train most of the senior talent that later goes independent.
- Independents. Talented (Bengaluru, founded 2022 by Gautam Reghunath and PG Aditiya, built on an employee-ownership model), Schbang (Mumbai, founded 2015, now a 1,000-plus person integrated independent), Famous Innovations, The Womb, Tilt Brand Solutions, Sideways, Enormous, Please See, Braindad, Spring Marketing Capital, FoxyMoron under Zoo Media, Social Beat (Chennai), Tonic Worldwide. Design: Elephant (Pune, founded 1989), Lopez Design (Delhi), Codesign (Delhi), Studio Kohl (Mumbai), Landor and Interbrand's Indian offices.
- Where premium sits. Luxury and premium clients in India (hospitality groups, jewellers, fashion houses, developers with branded residences) rarely tender; they buy from a founder or a CMO on referral, chemistry and portfolio. This is the pool Mask360 fishes in. The house account map of Indian agencies and their clients (see the new-business/india-agency-account-map folder in this repository) is the live record of who holds which account and is private house research (section 14.9).
- Scale of the market. The Pitch Madison Advertising Report 2026 (released 24 February 2026) puts 2025 adex (advertising expenditure) at Rs 1,55,105 crore on its expanded definition, 12 percent growth (Rs 1,15,291 crore on the legacy definition), and forecasts Rs 1,74,605 crore for 2026, with digital rising from 60 to 64 percent of the total. The other forecasts bracket it: dentsu-e4m at Rs 1,30,416 crore and WPP Media's This Year Next Year at Rs 2,01,891 crore. Creative fees are a small slice of that; the bulk is media.

### 1.4 The UAE and Saudi market

- Dubai is the regional hub: Dubai Media City and Dubai Internet City host the MENA (Middle East and North Africa) headquarters of the networks (Impact BBDO, FP7 McCann, Leo, which took in the regional Leo Burnett offices after the January 2025 merger, Ogilvy, which has dropped the Memac name, TBWA\RAAD, Horizon FCB, which is being folded under Omnicom's 2025 consolidation, Serviceplan Middle East, Publicis Middle East, VML MENA; regional brand names as of October 2026, verify each after the consolidations) and the independents (Science and Sunshine, Socialize, Create). Abu Dhabi's media zone is twofour54, run under the Creative Media Authority.
- Saudi Arabia is the growth engine. Vision 2030 giga-projects (NEOM, Diriyah, the Red Sea, Qiddiya), the Public Investment Fund's consumer brands, and Riyadh Season drive the largest briefs in the region. Since 1 January 2024 the Saudi Regional Headquarters (RHQ) programme requires foreign companies seeking government contracts to base their regional HQ in the Kingdom, which has pulled network leadership and talent to Riyadh. For a Dubai-based house the practical rule: Saudi work needs a Saudi partner or entity for government and giga-project clients; private luxury and hospitality clients can still be served from Dubai.
- How the Gulf buys. Procurement-led tenders for government and semi-government; founder-led buying for family groups, hospitality and real estate. Arabic is mandatory alongside English on most public-facing work (see section 14.7). Payment discipline is better than India for networks and government entities; family groups vary.
- What premium means there. The Gulf client base includes Chanel, Dior and LVMH's regional arms, Jumeirah, Emaar, Aldar, Alserkal, Chalhoub Group and the hospitality operators (Four Seasons, Aman, One and Only, Atlantis). These buy global-standard craft with regional fluency: bilingual typography, Ramadan and National Day calendars, modesty and representation norms. A house that can do both at taste level is rare in Dubai, which is the opening.

### 1.5 What clients pay for taste

Taste, as design-taste defines it (SKILL.md, opening paragraph), converts into price through four mechanisms:

1. Risk reduction. A premium client's downside from a cheap-looking launch (a Rs 40 crore residential tower that reads as mid-market, a jeweller whose campaign looks like a bank's) is far larger than any agency fee. The fee is insurance.
2. Scarcity. Few teams can produce it, and the client knows it from the portfolio in thirty seconds.
3. Speed of agreement. A house with taste wins approvals in fewer rounds because the work arrives already edited; fewer rounds is lower cost for both sides.
4. Signalling. The client's choice of agency is itself read by their peers.

Price taste as a premium on the hour, on the project and on the retainer: Mask360's floor sits in the top quartile of the Indian independents and the mid-to-upper band in Dubai (the agency-negotiator skill holds the market floors for small jobs; see "Which number governs" at the head of this file). The reason for refusing entry-level pricing is arithmetic. Senior craft hours cost more, and a discounted senior hour trains the client to expect discounted senior hours forever.

### 1.6 The 2026 landscape

- AI pricing pressure. Generative tools (image, video, copy, code) have cut the hours in layout, retouching, variant production and first-draft copy by about half since 2023 (house time records, 2023 to 2026). Clients know this and procurement teams now ask for the hours saved to be passed through. Two positions exist. The time-and-materials position (billing the hours actually worked at rate card: hold the rates, let AI shrink the hours, accept a lower invoice) is defended by procurement and by the large networks' client-services arms. The value position (price the outcome, keep the saving, invest it in craft) is argued by Blair Enns (Pricing Creativity, 2018) and David C. Baker (The Business of Expertise, 2017). The house follows the value position for ideas, identities, films and campaigns, and uses transparent reduced hours for variant production and adaptations where the client can see the machine doing the work. The reason: pricing by hours on AI-assisted work makes the agency's income fall exactly as its output per hour rises. See 22-ai-era-craft-and-generative-tools.md for the AI tool layer and the disclosure rules.
- Production decoupling. Decoupling is the client separating production (adaptations, retouching, versioning, print management, social volume) from the creative agency and awarding it to a production specialist (Hogarth under WPP, Tag, Oliver's in-house model, or an India-based studio) at lower rates. It has moved from FMCG to every category with volume. Response: keep the origination and the quality system, and either run the decoupled production at a transparent lower rate card or set the standards the production partner must meet (section 10).
- Design engineers and small teams. The design engineer (a person who designs in Figma and ships in code, usually React, Next.js, Framer or Webflow with motion in GSAP or Rive) has replaced the designer-plus-developer pair for premium sites and products. Four people with taste, a design engineer, a motion designer and a producer now deliver what took about fifteen in 2019 (house estimate). Pricing follows outcomes because headcount is no longer the proxy for value.
- Retainers versus projects. Retainers (a fixed monthly fee for an agreed scope and capacity) give the agency cash stability and the client a standing team; projects give the agency margin on ideas and the client a defined spend. The 2026 pattern for premium clients: a modest retainer for brand guardianship and social, with projects for launches, films and identities. Cash flow in India makes the retainer worth more than its margin suggests (section 13.6).
- Holding company consolidation. Omnicom's absorption of Interpublic, WPP's reorganisation under Cindy Rose (CEO since September 2025), which in October 2026 named McCann's global CEO Tyler Turnbull to lead WPP Creative in succession to Jon Cook, who leaves in March 2027 (MediaPost and B&T, October 2026), and Dentsu's review of its international business all point the same way: fewer, larger network brands, more internal production platforms, more AI "operating systems" sold to clients. Independent houses win on taste, speed and senior attention, which the consolidated networks structurally cannot sell.
- Platform-generated creative. Meta aims to let advertisers generate and target complete ads with AI by the end of 2026, as the Wall Street Journal reported in June 2025, and Google's Performance Max already generates headlines, images and video from a brand's inputs. The house response: sell what the machines generate from (the brand system, the hero assets, the asset library, the tone rules and the exclusions), and sell creative review of platform-generated variants as a retainer line, because a luxury brand's worst ad will soon be one that nobody at the brand or the agency saw before it ran.
- Consultancies and client AI studios. Accenture Song (owner of Droga5 since 2019) and Deloitte Digital compete for Gulf government and transformation-led brand mandates, where the buyer wants a systems integrator as much as a creative partner. Large clients are building generative content labs of their own; L'Oreal, a house client, announced its CREAITECH generative beauty content lab in 2024 (verify the current scope). The house competes on taste and on the originating idea, and leaves volume to the client's machine.

The reason to write ownership down is that creative teams fail at hand-offs more than at craft. Every brief should name the one person who owns the idea, the one who owns the craft, the one who owns the money and the one who owns the date.

### 2.2 Team shapes

Five people (the founder-led studio): founder as CD and new business; one senior designer who art directs; one designer or motion designer; one producer or project manager who also handles vendors; one account or ops person who also runs finance admin. Everything else freelance. Billable capacity: about 3.5 people. Revenue ceiling at premium Indian rates: about Rs 2.5 to 4 crore a year before the founder becomes the bottleneck.

Fifteen people: founder as CCO; one CD; two ADs; one copywriter; three designers (brand, digital, product); one motion designer; one editor; one producer; one strategist; two account managers; one project manager and traffic; one ops and finance. Billable capacity: about 10.5. Revenue band: about Rs 8 to 15 crore in India, AED 8 to 14 million in Dubai at the rates in section 15.

Forty people: two account groups each led by a CD with an ACD, two ADs, a copywriter, four designers and a motion designer; a shared production unit (head of production, two producers, two editors, one 3D); a strategy pair; a client services director with four account people; a three-person ops team (design ops, traffic, finance); a new business lead. Billable capacity: about 28. The reason the overhead grows faster than the billable count is that coordination becomes a job at this size; a traffic function is the first sign of a real agency.

### 2.3 The freelance network

Premium work in India and the Gulf runs on freelancers: photographers, DPs, editors, colourists, 3D, composers, voice, stylists, hair and make-up, set builders, retouchers. Manage the bench like staff:

- A vendor database (section 7.9) with rate, last job, quality score, reliability notes, kit, city and usage terms.
- Preferred-partner terms: a committed number of days a year in exchange for a 10 to 20 percent reduction from rate card and first call on dates.
- Briefing discipline identical to staff: the same brief format, reference boards and sign-off gates.
- Payment on time. A freelancer paid within 15 days in a market where 60 days is normal will hold dates for the house ahead of better-paying clients. This is the cheapest loyalty money buys.
- Exclusivity only when paid for: a day rate buys the day, and nothing else.

### 2.4 Talent markets and rates, October 2026

India. Senior creative talent clusters in Mumbai (advertising, film, fashion), Bengaluru (product, tech, the newer independents), Delhi and Gurugram (corporate, real estate, luxury retail), with Goa as a remote base for senior freelancers. Agency salaries for creatives are published annually by AmbitionBox and Glassdoor from self-reported data and by Michael Page India's salary guide; the house bands in section 11.4 sit at the upper end of those because luxury work needs senior hands. Day rates for freelancers in Mumbai are the highest in the country; Jaipur and Goa crews run 20 to 40 percent lower for the same skill.

UAE. Dubai salaries are quoted monthly in AED, tax-free, with a housing component sometimes separated. The Michael Page UAE Salary Guide 2026 and the Hays GCC (Gulf Cooperation Council) Salary Guide 2026 (Hays' 12th edition, about 400 roles from a survey of more than 1,600 employers and professionals) are the published references as of October 2026; their role rows sit behind a download, so verify the exact bands against the guides before quoting a candidate. Freelancing in Dubai requires a permit: the GoFreelance package offered jointly by the Dubai Media City, Dubai Internet City, Dubai Design District and Dubai Knowledge Park communities costs about AED 7,500 a year for media, design, education and tech activities (the site's FAQ lists AED 7,520, and conditions apply), with a one-year employment visa at AED 4,600 (two-year AED 5,042) and an establishment card at AED 2,000 on top, and the page notes that visa and card fees change (gofreelance.ae, read October 2026). This is why Dubai freelance day rates run two to three times the Mumbai equivalent: the freelancer carries permit, visa, insurance and Dubai rent.

Saudi. Riyadh salaries run 10 to 25 percent above Dubai for the same role because of the housing and hardship premium and the RHQ demand, and senior Arabic-first creatives are the scarcest talent in the region.

## 3. Scoping and pricing

### 3.1 Pricing models

| Model | Mechanism | Use it for | Risk sits with | House view |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hourly | Rate per hour per role, billed on timesheets | Open-ended consulting, overflow work | Client | Only for retainers' overflow and for change orders |
| Day rate | Rate per 8-hour day per role | Shoots, edits, on-site work, freelance | Client | Standard for production |
| Project (fixed fee) | One price for a defined scope and rounds | Identity, site, film, campaign | Agency | Default for creative work |
| Retainer | Monthly fee for defined scope or capacity | Social, brand guardianship, standing design support | Shared | Default for ongoing work; capacity defined in days per month |
| Value based | Price set from the value to the client, agreed before scope is detailed | Launches, identities for funded brands, naming | Agency | Use when the client's upside is quantifiable and the house's taste is the deciding factor |
| Licensing and usage | Fee for the right to use an asset for a term, territory and medium | Photography, film, talent, music, illustration, fonts | Shared | Always separate from the creative fee |

The reason the house prices projects as fixed fees: the client buys certainty and the agency keeps the gain from working fast and well. The reason hourly billing is kept for overflow only: it caps income at hours worked and penalises skill (Jonathan Stark, Hourly Billing Is Nuts, 2016; Blair Enns, Pricing Creativity, 2018).

Three-option pricing. Present every significant project with three options (Enns' anchoring method): the lean option that answers the brief, the recommended option, and a premium option beyond the brief that the client would want. The middle option sells about 60 percent of the time (house proposals, 2024 to 2026), and the top option reframes what "expensive" means. The lean option should still be work the house is proud of; a deliberately poor option is dishonest and the client sees it.

### 3.2 Rate cards

A rate card is the published hourly or daily price per role and seniority, used to build estimates and to price change orders. Blended rates (one rate averaged across the team) simplify retainers; role rates make estimates defensible.

Both cards are house cards, built bottom-up with the loaded-cost method of section 13.3 at about a 55 percent gross margin: rate equals loaded cost per hour divided by 0.45. India: a mid designer on Rs 12 lakh CTC (cost to company, the Indian annual pay figure including employer provident fund and gratuity accrual) has a loaded cost of about Rs 1,570 an hour (13.3), and Rs 1,570 divided by 0.45 is about Rs 3,500, the middle of the mid band. Dubai: a mid designer on AED 12,000 a month costs about AED 172,800 a year with visa, health insurance and gratuity accrual (salary times 1.2), plus about AED 45,000 of overhead; over 1,300 billable hours that is about AED 168 an hour, and divided by 0.45 about AED 370, inside the mid band. A mid designer at the top of the Dubai salary band (AED 18,000 a month) needs about AED 520 an hour at the same margin, which is why a senior-salaried mid is billed at the senior rate.

India, premium independent, October 2026, per hour, before GST (goods and services tax) at 18 percent; figures are about, calibrated to Mumbai. Outside references, kept as a floor because they describe the offshore outsourcing market: Indian agency profiles on Clutch list bands from USD 25 to 49 up to USD 100 to 149 an hour, and GoodFirms reported a median of USD 25 an hour for Indian advertising agencies in August 2026. ### 3.3 Estimating by deliverable

Estimate in hours by role, then price with the margin method in section 13.4, then sanity check against the market and against value. The ranges below are for premium execution with two to three rounds; a cheap execution halves them and a flagship doubles them.

| Deliverable | Scope assumed | Hours (about) | Weeks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Identity (core) | Strategy input, 2 to 3 routes, 1 developed, mark, type, colour, core applications | 160 to 400 | 8 to 16 |
| Guidelines | 40 to 80 page system document, digital first, with asset library | 60 to 160 | 3 to 6 |
| Website (design) | 8 to 15 pages, responsive, 2 rounds, motion spec | 150 to 400 | 6 to 10 |
| Website (design and build) | As above plus CMS (content management system) build, QA (quality assurance), launch | 400 to 1,000 | 10 to 20 |
| App design | 30 to 60 screens, flows, design system, prototype, handoff | 300 to 800 | 12 to 24 |
| Campaign | Strategy, idea, 3 key visuals, 1 film script, 20 to 40 adaptations | 150 to 500 | 6 to 12 |
| Brand film (agency side) | Script, boards, PPM (pre-production meeting), direction on set, post supervision; excludes production cost | 120 to 400 | 4 to 10 |
| Social month | Strategy, 16 to 24 posts, 6 to 10 reels, community, report | 120 to 220 | Monthly cycle |
| Deck | 30 to 50 slides, designed and written | 25 to 80 | 1 to 3 |
| Print piece | 24 to 48 page brochure or lookbook, print managed | 60 to 180 | 4 to 8 |
| Naming | Research, 100 plus candidates, trademark screen, 3 to 5 finalists | 60 to 120 | 4 to 6 |
| Packaging (one SKU family; SKU: stock keeping unit) | Structure brief, 3 routes, dielines, print supervision | 100 to 250 | 6 to 12 |

Split the hours by role so the estimate can be staffed and defended to procurement. A typical identity: strategy 10 to 15 percent, CD 10 to 15, design 45 to 55, copy and naming 5 to 10, production and project management 10 to 15. Estimate each role in three points (low, likely, high) and price at the likely figure plus a 10 to 15 percent contingency held inside the fee, without a line of its own. Add about 25 to 40 percent to layout and artwork hours for parallel Arabic or Devanagari execution, because line lengths, reading direction and type families change every page (see 03-typography.md, section 15). These are house planning figures; the post mortem (9.8) records estimate against actual by role, and the ranges are recalibrated each quarter.

### 3.4 Rounds, change orders and scope creep

- A round is one consolidated set of client feedback on one presented stage, answered with one revised presentation. Two rounds per stage is the premium norm; three is generous; unlimited is a cost the client is not paying for. The reason to count rounds: the number of rounds is the single best predictor of margin erosion on a fixed-fee job.
- A change order is a written, priced addition or alteration to scope, signed before the work starts. Price it from the rate card at hourly or day rate, with a minimum of half a day. The habit that protects the house: say "yes, and here is the change order" in the same breath.
- Scope creep is the accumulation of small unpriced additions. Clauses that prevent it: a defined deliverables list with quantities; a defined rounds count; a definition of "round" and of "feedback consolidated through one point of contact" (section 8.3); a change-order process; a clause that work not listed is excluded; an assumptions list (client supplies copy, images, access, approvals within 5 working days).
- Rush, expenses and work started before the contract are priced in section 3.9.
- Kill fee. A kill fee is the amount payable if the client cancels after work begins: all work completed to date at rate card, plus a percentage of the remaining fee (25 to 50 percent, higher after creative has been presented), plus all third-party costs committed. The reason for the percentage: the house turned away other work for the dates.

### 3.5 Payment terms

- Projects: 50 percent advance (40 percent minimum for large clients), 25 percent on concept approval, 25 percent on delivery. Files, source and rights transfer on final payment. Never release finals before the final payment clears; the lever disappears the moment the files leave.
- Retainers: billed monthly in advance, due within 15 days; two months of non-payment pauses the work automatically (write that into the contract).
- Production: third-party costs (crew, talent, locations, kit, post) paid 100 percent in advance by the client, or paid directly by the client to the vendor on the house's purchase order with the house's mark-up invoiced separately. The reason: a Rs 40 lakh shoot financed on the house's balance sheet at 90 days is a loan the house did not agree to make.
- The Indian reality. Large Indian clients pay in 60 to 90 days whatever the invoice says, and some in 120. Protections, in order of power: advance; milestone billing triggered by approvals; POs (purchase orders) issued before work starts (no PO, no start); a named accounts-payable contact and a vendor registration completed in week one; invoices submitted on the client's portal the day the milestone lands; a late-payment interest clause (even if rarely enforced, it changes the conversation); and, where the house qualifies as a micro or small enterprise under Udyam registration (India's online registration of micro, small and medium enterprises), the MSMED (Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development) Act 2006 payment protections (section 13.5).
- The UAE reality. Networks and government pay on terms, often 60 days; family groups negotiate; Saudi government entities can run 90 to 180 days. Post-dated cheques are still used as security in the UAE. Since 2 January 2022 a cheque bounced for insufficient funds is mainly a civil matter: the holder takes it straight to the execution judge as an enforceable instrument under Federal Decree-Law No. 50 of 2022 (the Commercial Transactions Law), and criminal liability is kept for bad-faith acts such as closing the account or stopping payment without cause. That fast civil route is what makes the cheque a meaningful instrument; the house never threatens criminal action over a bounce.

### 3.6 Currency and tax

India.
- GST on advertising, design, production and most professional services is 18 percent under SAC (services accounting code) heading 9983 (the GST 2.0 restructuring effective 22 September 2025 moved to a 5, 18 and 40 percent structure and left standard-rated services, agency commission included, at 18 percent; confirm the six-digit SAC rate against the CBIC (Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs) notification). Sale of advertising space in print media is taxed at 5 percent, which matters when the house buys print media for a client.
- Export of services (client outside India, payment in convertible foreign exchange or in INR where RBI permits, service consumed outside India) is zero-rated (taxed at 0 percent, so no tax is charged and input tax stays recoverable): invoice without GST under a LUT (letter of undertaking filed with GST authorities each financial year) and keep the FIRC or FIRA (foreign inward remittance certificate or advice, the bank's proof of foreign receipt) for every invoice.
- TDS (tax deducted at source) is withheld by the client from the invoice and deposited against the house's PAN (permanent account number); the house claims credit via Form 26AS. Advertising contracts attract TDS under section 194C (1 percent for individuals and HUFs, Hindu undivided families; 2 percent for companies) per CBDT (Central Board of Direct Taxes) Circular 715 of 1995, while fees for professional services under section 194J attract 10 percent (technical services 2 percent). The 194J threshold is Rs 50,000 a year per payee and per payment type, raised from Rs 30,000 by the Finance Act 2025 with effect from 1 April 2025. Payments by the house to freelance artists, photographers and models fall under 194J at 10 percent. Build TDS into cash planning: a Rs 10 lakh invoice under 194J arrives as Rs 9 lakh plus GST. From 1 April 2026 the Income-tax Act 2025 replaced the 1961 Act and consolidated the TDS provisions into one section with rate tables (section 393); the 194C and 194J references above are the 1961 numbering most clients and contracts still quote, so confirm the new mapping and any rate change with the house's chartered accountant before either appears on an invoice.
- Payments to foreign vendors (a Dubai colourist, a London composer) need Form 15CA and often 15CB from a chartered accountant, and withholding under section 195 unless the India-UAE DTAA (double taxation avoidance agreement) and a tax residency certificate remove it.
- Gifts and stays given to creators. Section 194R (1961 numbering; the 2025 Act carries it in the consolidated TDS table, so confirm the new reference) makes the giver deduct TDS of 10 percent on benefits or perquisites above Rs 20,000 a year arising from a business or profession (Finance Act 2022, from 1 July 2022). CBDT Circular 12 of 2022 applies it to products an influencer keeps after a campaign, so a hotel's complimentary creator stay or a jeweller's gifted piece triggers it, and whoever gives the benefit, the brand or the house, deducts and deposits the tax.
- GST is a cash cost before it is a tax. For services, GST falls due on the earlier of the invoice and the receipt of an advance, paid through the monthly GSTR-3B return by the 20th of the following month, and an invoice must be raised within 30 days of completing the service (CGST, central GST, Rules 2017, rule 47), so the house finances 18 percent of every unpaid invoice. A client that has not paid within 180 days of the invoice must reverse the input tax credit (GST paid on purchases and offset against GST charged) it claimed (CGST Act 2017, section 16(2), and rule 37); say so, politely, in the collection email at day 150. Importing a service (a Dubai colourist, a London composer) attracts IGST (integrated GST, levied on inter-state and cross-border supplies) under reverse charge (IGST Act 2017, section 5(3)): recoverable as credit, but a cash outflow first. An invoice to the house's own Dubai branch is not an export, because supplier and recipient must not be merely establishments of one legal person (IGST Act, section 2(6)(v)); an invoice to a separate UAE company is. Export proceeds must be realised within the RBI period under FEMA (the Foreign Exchange Management Act 1999): nine months historically, reported as extended to 15 months in late 2025 (verify).

UAE.
- VAT is 5 percent (Federal Decree-Law No. 8 of 2017); mandatory registration above AED 375,000 taxable turnover in 12 months, voluntary above AED 187,500. Services supplied to a customer resident outside the UAE and consumed outside the UAE are zero-rated under the export of services rules, subject to the customer having no UAE presence connected to the supply (verify the conditions against the Executive Regulation, Article 31, published by the FTA, the Federal Tax Authority).
- Corporate tax has applied since financial years starting on or after 1 June 2023: 0 percent on taxable income up to AED 375,000 and 9 percent above (Federal Decree-Law No. 47 of 2022). Small Business Relief lets a resident business with revenue of AED 3,000,000 or less in the current and all previous tax periods elect to be treated as having no taxable income, for tax periods ending on or before 31 December 2026 (Ministerial Decision No. 73 of 2023, Article 2, as explained in the FTA guide CTGSBR1 of August 2023); a Qualifying Free Zone Person cannot elect it, so a free zone entity chooses between the two reliefs. A Qualifying Free Zone Person pays 0 percent only on qualifying income, and for an agency that is narrow. Qualifying income comes from transactions with other free zone persons, and from anyone else only for the Qualifying Activities listed in Ministerial Decision No. 265 of 2023 (manufacturing, trading qualifying commodities, holding shares, treasury and financing to related parties, headquarter services to related parties, logistics and a few others); advertising and creative services are not on the list (verify against the current decision on tax.gov.ae). Fees from an Indian client or a mainland Dubai developer are therefore non-qualifying. The de minimis rule (Cabinet Decision No. 100 of 2023) caps non-qualifying revenue at 5 percent of total revenue or AED 5 million, whichever is lower; breach it and the entity loses the status for that tax period and the following four. Plan on 9 percent above AED 375,000 unless the clients are themselves free zone persons (regional headquarters in DMCC, the Dubai Multi Commodities Centre, in Dubai Design District or in DIFC, the Dubai International Financial Centre). Serving mainland Dubai clients from a free zone also needs either the mainland operating permit introduced by Dubai Executive Council Resolution No. 11 of 2025 (verify) or a mainland licence. A UAE tax adviser confirms the structure before the entity is chosen.
- E-invoicing. The UAE eInvoicing Programme (mof.gov.ae/einvoicing, read October 2026) uses a decentralised continuous transaction control and exchange model: structured invoices in the PINT AE format pass through UAE Accredited Service Providers on both sides and tax data reports to the FTA; PDFs and emails stop counting as invoices once a business is in scope. Ministerial Decisions No. 243 and No. 244 of 2025 (29 September 2025) set a pilot from 1 July 2026, mandatory e-invoicing from 1 January 2027 for businesses with revenue of AED 50 million or more (originally required to appoint an accredited service provider by 31 July 2026 under Ministerial Decision No. 244, per KPMG; another secondary source gives 30 October 2026), 1 July 2027 for the rest and 1 October 2027 for government entities. Ministerial Decision No. 197 of 2026 and Ministerial Resolution No. 66 of 2026 have since amended both instruments, and Ministerial Decision No. 168 of 2026 and Ministerial Resolution No. 56 of 2026 reset service provider accreditation (all four are listed on mof.gov.ae/einvoicing, read October 2026, where the timeline itself is published as an image). The amendments may have moved the appointment date, so confirm the current phase dates on that page before choosing an accounting system. India already mandates e-invoicing with an IRN (invoice reference number, generated on the GST portal) for businesses with aggregate turnover above Rs 5 crore, in force since 1 August 2023.

Saudi Arabia (verify each figure on zatca.gov.sa before quoting).
- VAT is 15 percent (since 1 July 2020), with registration mandatory above SAR (Saudi riyals) 375,000 of taxable supplies.
- Withholding tax on payments from a Saudi payer to a non-resident (Income Tax Law, Article 68): 5 percent for technical and consulting services, 15 percent for royalties and most other payments, 20 percent for management fees. A Dubai or Mumbai invoice to a Riyadh client therefore arrives net of withholding unless the fee was grossed up in the proposal; classify creative services with a Saudi adviser first, and check the India-Saudi Arabia DTAA when billing from India.
- E-invoicing runs through FATOORA, the e-invoicing system of ZATCA (the Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority), integrated in phased waves by revenue.
- Government work needs registration on the Etimad procurement platform and, for most entities, the RHQ and local-entity rules of section 1.4.

Cross-border invoicing between the two entities. Invoice from the entity that signed the contract; keep intercompany work on a written services agreement at arm's length pricing (India's transfer pricing rules under section 92 apply to associated enterprises); quote the client in their currency and hold a 3 to 5 percent currency buffer on fixed fees longer than three months.

### 4.1 Proposal structure

A proposal is the document that turns a conversation into a priced offer. Ten sections, in this order, because the order is the order in which a buyer decides:

| Understanding | The client's situation in the house's words: the business, the audience, the problem, the ambition | 1 page | The buyer decides here whether the house listened |

Rules. Write it in the house grammar (lean, few words, client as hero, dark ground for client decks with the client's hex: design-taste, SKILL.md, section 7). The fee page never surprises; the number was spoken on the call before the document exists. A proposal longer than 12 pages signals that the scope is not understood. Validity 30 days, because rates, availability and exchange rates move.

### 4.2 SOW anatomy

A SOW (statement of work) is the contract-grade description of one engagement under a master agreement. Its parts: parties and MSA reference; objectives; scope and deliverables with acceptance criteria per deliverable ("site design accepted when every page in Appendix A is approved in Figma by the named approver and the design QA checklist is signed"); out of scope; schedule and milestones with dependencies; roles and responsibilities (a RACI: responsible, accountable, consulted, informed, per deliverable); rounds and the feedback protocol (section 8.3); change control (section 3.4); fees and a payment schedule tied to milestones; third-party costs and the mark-up (section 7.3 sets which form applies to which client); acceptance and deemed acceptance (silence for 10 working days after delivery counts as acceptance, because an unanswered delivery otherwise never closes); assumptions; term and termination; IP schedule; signatures. The reason to write acceptance criteria per deliverable: "done" is the most expensive undefined word in the business.

### 4.3 MSA basics

An MSA (master services agreement) is the umbrella contract that sets the legal terms once so each SOW can be short. Clauses the house's MSA carries: term (2 to 3 years, auto-renewing); services ordered by SOW; fees, invoicing and late payment (in India, the MSMED Act rate for a registered micro or small enterprise, otherwise a contractual 1.5 percent a month; in the UAE a contractual rate, since the Civil Code lets courts moderate it); taxes; IP (section 4.4); confidentiality (4.5); data protection (14.8); warranties (originality and non-infringement for the house's own work; no warranty over client-supplied material or wholly AI-generated elements); mutual indemnities (the house for IP infringement in its work, the client for materials, claims and products it supplied); limitation of liability (4.8); insurance (14.10); non-solicitation of staff for 12 months with a fee of 25 to 50 percent of annual salary on breach (a client hiring the designer is a real risk in a small market); publicity and portfolio (4.6); termination and force majeure (4.7); conflicts and exclusivity (8.12); dispute resolution and governing law (4.11); notices; entire agreement. Client paper: networks, listed companies and Gulf government entities send their own MSA, usually 30 to 60 pages with uncapped indemnities and full IP assignment on creation; those go to a lawyer (4.12) before anyone signs.

### 4.4 IP: assignment versus licence, and moral rights

An assignment transfers ownership of the copyright; a licence grants permission to use while the creator keeps ownership. Both are priced, and the price difference is the usage economics of section 3.7.

India, under the Copyright Act 1957 as amended in 2012:

- First ownership (section 17). The author is the first owner, with exceptions. Under proviso (b), a photograph, painting, portrait, engraving or cinematograph film made for valuable consideration at the instance of a person belongs to that person in the absence of an agreement to the contrary. Under proviso (c), a work made in the course of employment under a contract of service belongs to the employer in the absence of an agreement to the contrary. Consequence: staff work belongs to the house; freelance work (a contract for service) belongs to the freelancer unless assigned in writing; a commissioned photograph belongs to the commissioner by default but the agreement should say so, because "at the instance of" is argued over.
- Assignment (sections 18 and 19). Must be in writing, signed by the assignor, identifying the work, the rights, the term and the territory. If the term is not stated it is deemed five years; if the territory is not stated it is deemed India (section 19(5) and 19(6)). An assignment without a stated term is therefore a five-year assignment, which is the most common drafting trap in Indian creative contracts. Under section 19(4), rights not exercised within one year of assignment lapse unless the agreement says otherwise.
- Moral rights (section 57). The author's special rights: paternity (to claim authorship) and integrity (to restrain or claim damages for distortion, mutilation or modification prejudicial to honour or reputation). They exist independently of the copyright and survive assignment. Whether they can be waived by contract is contested: the statute is silent, practitioners draft "agrees not to assert" clauses, and Amar Nath Sehgal v Union of India (Delhi High Court, 2005), in which the government's destruction of a commissioned mural led to damages and return of the remains, is the leading integrity case. House position: the freelancer agreement carries an assignment, a moral rights non-assertion clause, and a credit clause, because credit is what the creator actually wants; and the house does not alter a photographer's work beyond agreed retouching without telling them.
- Authorship of machine output. Section 2(d)(vi) names "the person who causes the work to be created" as author of a computer-generated work, but the Copyright Office's practice and the ANI v OpenAI litigation in the Delhi High Court (filed 2024, pending as of October 2026) leave AI authorship unsettled; treat wholly AI-generated material as unprotectable unless substantial human authorship can be shown (see 22-ai-era-craft-and-generative-tools.md, section 7.1).
- Term and formality. Copyright arises on creation; registration with the Copyright Office is optional evidence. Term is the author's life plus 60 years for literary, dramatic, musical and artistic works including photographs since the 2012 amendment; 60 years from publication for films and sound recordings.

UAE, under Federal Decree-Law No. 38 of 2021 on Copyrights and Neighbouring Rights (replacing Federal Law No. 7 of 2002): the author owns the work; economic rights in commissioned and employee work pass by written contract; moral rights (attribution, integrity, the right to decide first publication) are perpetual and cannot be waived or assigned, which is why a UAE contract uses a non-assertion undertaking; the general term is the author's life plus 50 years, with 50 years from publication for works of applied art and collective works; registration with the Ministry of Economy is optional. Verify article numbers against the decree-law before citing them in a contract.

House default position, written into every SOW: the final approved deliverables are assigned to the client on receipt of full payment; unused and rejected routes stay with the house (a client paying for one identity should not own three); the house's pre-existing materials, methods, code libraries and system components stay with the house under a perpetual, royalty-free licence to the client for use within the deliverable; third-party materials (fonts, stock, music, talent) pass under their own licences, listed in the delivery note; the house keeps portfolio rights (4.6).

### 4.5 Confidentiality

A mutual NDA (non-disclosure agreement) with a term of 2 to 3 years from disclosure, trade secrets indefinite, the usual carve-outs (public domain, independently developed, legally compelled), a return-or-destroy clause and no non-compete (a non-compete is unenforceable against the house in India under section 27 of the Indian Contract Act 1872 beyond the term of the contract, and it would also block the house from its own market). The house's research (section 14.9) is confidential information of the house. In a pitch, the house asks for an NDA before presenting ideas, or includes the line "concepts presented remain the property of Mask360 until commissioned" in the submission; both are weak protection, and the real protection is section 5.5.

### 4.6 Portfolio and credit rights

The house may show the work in its portfolio, case studies, awards entries, social channels and press after public release, or after a 90-day embargo for unlaunched work, unless the client requested confidentiality in writing before signature. Credit reads "Agency: Mask360" with named team members. The client's veto is limited to unreleased work and confidential figures. The reason the house will not trade this away for a fee: the portfolio is the only durable asset an agency owns.

### 4.7 Termination

For convenience by either side on 30 days' notice for projects and 60 to 90 days for retainers, paying for work done to the notice date at rate card plus the kill fee of section 3.4. For cause on 15 days' notice with a cure period. Effect: deliverables paid for transfer; unpaid ones do not; work in progress passes only on payment. A retainer contract carries an automatic pause after two months of non-payment (section 3.5).

Force majeure and postponement. Force majeure (events outside either party's control that suspend obligations) is defined in the MSA by a list: government orders, official mourning, curfews, epidemics, extreme weather and platform outages. Obligations are suspended, and either side may terminate after 30 days of suspension with payment for work done and costs committed. The Gulf precedent: the UAE's 40 days of official mourning from May 2022 postponed events and campaigns across the country. Postponement is priced separately from cancellation, by notice: a shoot postponed more than seven days ahead pays the non-refundable costs already committed; inside 48 hours it pays 50 to 100 percent of crew and kit fees (house planning). Exterior quotes hold a weather day (7.3), and events carry cancellation and abandonment insurance (7.10). The reason: without the clause, the cost of a postponement falls on whoever blinks first.

### 4.8 Liability caps

Cap the house's total liability under a SOW at the fees paid under that SOW in the 12 months before the claim; exclude indirect and consequential loss (lost profit, lost revenue, reputational loss); carve out IP indemnity, confidentiality breach and wilful misconduct from the cap because clients will insist and courts expect it. The reason: a Rs 30 lakh identity must not expose the house to a Rs 300 crore brand's claimed lost sales. Indian law helps: under sections 73 and 74 of the Indian Contract Act 1872 courts award reasonable compensation with the stipulated sum as the ceiling (Fateh Chand v Balkishan Dass, Supreme Court 1963; Kailash Nath Associates v DDA, Supreme Court 2015). The UAE Civil Transactions Law (Federal Law No. 5 of 1985, Article 390, or its successor provision if the law has been replaced; verify before citing) lets a court adjust agreed compensation to equal the proven loss, so treat a UAE cap as a negotiated ceiling that a court can revisit.

### 4.9 The AI clause

Every SOW since 2024 carries an AI clause with six parts: the house may use generative tools in process (ideation, comps, retouching, variants, code) under its own disclosure rules; final deliverables containing AI-generated elements are identified in the delivery note; the house does not enter client confidential material into tools whose terms allow training on inputs (enterprise and API tiers generally exclude it, consumer tiers often do not; check the current terms per tool); no synthetic likeness or voice of a real person without that person's written consent; labelling follows the ASCI guidelines on synthetically generated content (section 14.6) and platform rules; the client may prohibit AI in named deliverables, priced accordingly. The IP warranty and indemnity exclude wholly AI-generated elements, because copyright offices (the US Copyright Office's 2023 guidance and its January 2025 report on copyrightability take the same line) treat output without human authorship as unprotectable, and the client should know what it owns. These six parts are the minimum on every SOW, and they correspond to clauses 1, 7 and 9 of the twelve client clauses in 22-ai-era-craft-and-generative-tools.md, section 7.8, plus that section's talent release. Any deliverable with generated hero elements adds the other nine clauses there (ownership to the extent protectable, warranty of process, indemnity caps, tool substitution, written client approval, data residency and deletion, regulated claims, archive, audit).

### 4.10 The talent and music clauses

Talent: the release (section 7.5) states term, territory, media, exclusivity and fee; renewals are the client's cost; the house prefers the production company or the client to contract talent directly on the house's form of release, so that liability for using an image past its term sits with the party using it. Music: the licence or assignment is named in the SOW (library plan and tier, bespoke assignment, or commercial sync with both master and publishing cleared); the client warrants it will not use the music beyond the licensed term and media; cue sheets are delivered with the film. A full assignment of a bespoke jingle does not end the composer's royalty share: under the Copyright Act 1957 as amended in 2012 (section 18(1), third and fourth provisos; section 19(9) and 19(10)), the author of a literary or musical work in a film or sound recording keeps a right, which cannot be assigned or waived, to an equal share of royalties for uses other than screening the film in a cinema hall, collected through IPRS (the Indian Performing Right Society). Composer and lyricist agreements acknowledge it, and the house tells the client that the assignment covers everything except that royalty. The reason for both clauses: usage breaches are the most common claim against agencies in India and the Gulf, and they are claims the house can route to the right party only if the paper says so.

### 4.11 Indian and UAE law basics

India. Contracts are governed by the Indian Contract Act 1872; electronic signatures are valid under the Information Technology Act 2000 (Aadhaar e-sign or a digital signature certificate); stamp duty on service agreements is nominal and state-specific, paid by e-stamp; the limitation period for a contract claim is three years (Limitation Act 1963); courts take years, so the house's contracts seat arbitration in Mumbai under the Arbitration and Conciliation Act 1996 with a sole arbitrator, and payment disputes with large buyers go to the MSME (micro, small and medium enterprise) Samadhaan route (section 13.5). Commissioned-work copyright is section 4.4.

UAE. Onshore contracts fall under the Civil Transactions Law (Federal Law No. 5 of 1985 as amended; check for later amending or replacement legislation before citing an article) and the Commercial Transactions Law (Federal Decree-Law No. 50 of 2022); onshore courts work in Arabic and require certified translations of every document; the DIFC Courts and the ADGM (Abu Dhabi Global Market) Courts are English-language common-law courts and parties can opt into DIFC Courts jurisdiction by contract even without a DIFC presence; arbitration is seated at DIAC (Dubai International Arbitration Centre), which absorbed the DIFC-LCIA under Dubai Decree No. 34 of 2021. Since 2022 a cheque bounced for insufficient funds is enforced mainly through the civil execution route (section 3.5), with criminal liability kept for bad faith, and that speed is why post-dated cheques still work as security. Media content is regulated under Federal Decree-Law No. 55 of 2023 on Media Regulation, enforced since December 2025 by the National Media Authority, which replaced the UAE Media Council (section 14.7).

### 4.12 When to involve a lawyer

Always: any MSA; any client paper; any contract above Rs 25 lakh or AED 150,000; cross-border contracts; celebrity talent; commercial music sync; anything with uncapped liability, exclusivity or a non-compete; a data processing agreement; payment in equity or revenue share; a dispute. Budget 1 to 2 percent of contract value for review on large contracts and a fixed annual retainer with a commercial lawyer in each country for the small questions, because the expensive mistakes are the ones nobody asked about.

## 5. Pitching

### 5.1 RFI and RFP responses

An RFI (request for information) asks who the house is: credentials, size, clients, process, finances. An RFP (request for proposal) asks for scope and price, and sometimes for creative. Qualify before answering either. Go only when: the decision maker is reachable before submission; the budget is stated or discoverable; the number of agencies invited is four or fewer; the timeline allows at least three weeks for creative; the IP terms do not claim pitch ideas; price carries less than 40 percent of the scoring weight (above that, the buyer is buying rate, and rate is what the house is worst at). Answer the questions in their order, in their numbering, so the procurement reader can score; put the house's own story on one page at the front, because the decision maker reads only that page.

Public-sector tenders. Government tourism boards, development authorities and Gulf semi-government entities buy premium work through formal tenders, and the security they demand ties up cash. India: the GeM portal (Government e-Marketplace) for most central purchases; empanelment with the Central Bureau of Communication (formed in December 2017 by merging the Directorate of Advertising and Visual Publicity with two other media units) for government advertising; bid security of 2 to 5 percent (General Financial Rules 2017, rule 170) and performance security of 3 to 10 percent (rule 171; verify the current band, which has been relaxed and restored since 2020), held for the contract term plus any warranty period. UAE: supplier registration with each entity and an ICV (In-Country Value) certificate, which federal and Abu Dhabi procurement score. Saudi Arabia: Etimad (3.6). Price the locked-up security into the fee at the house's cost of capital for the term.

### 5.2 Chemistry meetings

Sixty to ninety minutes, the founder and the CD in the room, three to five cases chosen for this brief and shown large, and a listening ratio of about 60 to 40 (the house asks more than it tells). The goal is to hear the real problem under the brief and to leave one point of view the client did not have before; the solution waits for the paid phase. Send a one-page recap within 24 hours.

### 5.3 Credentials decks

Twelve to twenty slides built to the agency and studio showcase entry of design-taste, references/worlds.md, on the dark client deck of design-taste, SKILL.md, section 7. Keep a master credentials file sectioned by category (hospitality, fashion, beauty, jewellery, real estate, F&B, events) with case pages kept current, so a bespoke deck assembles in two hours.

### 5.4 Pitch theatre and its cost

Pitch theatre is the staged element: a dressed room, printed books, mock-ups, a film, a built prototype. It wins when the idea is strong and loses nothing when the idea is weak. Cost it honestly. A typical creative pitch at the house:

| Role | Hours | India at senior rates (Rs) | Dubai at senior rates (AED) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strategist | 40 | 2,40,000 | 24,000 |
| Creative director | 40 | 4,80,000 | 44,000 |
| Art director | 60 | 3,30,000 | 30,000 |
| Designer (senior) | 80 | 4,00,000 | 40,000 |
| Copywriter | 40 | 2,20,000 | 20,000 |
| Motion designer | 30 | 1,65,000 | 15,000 |
| Producer | 20 | 70,000 | 7,000 |
| Account lead | 30 | 1,05,000 | 10,500 |
| Total time | 340 | about 20 lakh | about 190,000 |
| Hard costs (mock-ups, print, travel) | | 50,000 to 3,00,000 | 5,000 to 25,000 |

Rates used, mostly from the senior bands of section 3.2: strategist Rs 6,000 (AED 600), CD Rs 12,000 (AED 1,100), art director, copywriter and motion designer Rs 5,500 (AED 500), senior designer Rs 5,000 (AED 500), producer and account lead Rs 3,500 (AED 350) an hour.

At a 30 percent win rate, each win costs three to four pitches, or Rs 60 to 80 lakh of unbilled senior time in India. A win must therefore carry at least that much in gross margin over its first two years to break even, which at a 55 percent margin means about Rs 1.2 to 1.5 crore of fees. That is the arithmetic behind the house rule, which scales with the house. At the current stage (Rs 0 to 3 crore of fee income, per agency-ceo), go for a full creative pitch only when the account is worth at least 10 to 15 percent of annual fee income or opens a category the house has chosen, and run it lean under the hour cap of 5.9 or as a paid sprint (5.5), because a Rs 20 lakh pitch is more than a month of payroll at that size. The same 10 to 15 percent rule holds through early growth (Rs 3 to 10 crore); from the scale stage (Rs 10 crore and above), the floor is an account of about Rs 1 crore a year in India or AED 1 million in Dubai.

### 5.5 Paid pitches and the ethics of free pitching

Two positions. The first holds that free creative pitching is the cost of entry: the networks and procurement-led clients run it, and in India the "creative shootout" with four to eight agencies is still normal for FMCG, auto and telecom. The second holds that creative work in a pitch is spec work (work done on speculation, without pay) and corrodes the profession: AIGA's position against spec work, the NO!SPEC campaign, D&AD's guidance, and Blair Enns' The Win Without Pitching Manifesto (2010). The IPA and ISBA Pitch Positive Pledge (UK, 2022) sits between the two: it commits clients to pitch only when necessary and to say in writing why, to invite a proportionate number of agencies, to respect working hours, weekends and public holidays in the timeline, and to give direct feedback win or lose; agencies commit to manage the workload of the pitch team and to accept the decision. House position: chemistry and credentials are free; strategic thinking is free up to one meeting; creative concepts are produced only against a pitch fee (Rs 2 to 10 lakh in India, AED 15,000 to 75,000 in the Gulf, credited against fees on award) or inside a paid discovery sprint of two to three weeks that replaces the pitch. The reason: a client who will not pay for ideas in the pitch will not pay for them in the contract, and the sprint shows the client the house's way of working, which is the thing actually being bought.

### 5.6 The win rate maths

Expected value of a pitch equals the probability of winning multiplied by the first-year gross margin, minus the pitch cost. Score the probability on a fixed card before committing: existing relationship (0 to 3), portfolio fit (0 to 3), budget confirmed (0 to 2), competitors (2 for one or two agencies, 1 for three or four, 0 for more), incumbent in the room (0 if yes, 1 if no). Go at 7 of 11 or above with a positive expected value. Track four numbers quarterly: pitches entered, win rate, cost per pitch, cost per win. The target is a win rate above 40 percent on qualified pitches, reached by declining the unqualified ones; a house that wins one in six is pitching too much, and the sixth win pays for the five losses only if it is large.

### 5.7 How to decline

Within 48 hours, by phone and then by email. One honest reason (timeline, conflict, fit, terms), an alternative where there is one (a paid sprint, a referral to a house that fits better), and an open door. A good decline is remembered as confidence; a late one is remembered as discourtesy.

### 5.8 Pitch follow-up and reference calls

Follow up within 24 hours with a one-page recap and answers to open questions; ask for the decision date; check in once on that date and once two weeks later; then stop. For reference calls, brief the referee on what was asked and on the result, choose referees who will name a problem the house solved (a reference with no problem sounds coached), and rotate referees so no client is called more than twice a year.

### 5.9 The pitch to win versus the pitch to learn

A pitch to win is fully resourced and theatrical. A pitch to learn is entered to understand a category or client type the house wants (a Saudi giga-project, a global tech brand), run lean under a fixed hour cap with the learning written up afterwards for section 9.9. Both are legitimate. A pitch to fill idle time is neither: it trains the team that pitching is free.

### 5.10 New business engines

- Referrals from clients, architects, hospitality consultants, PR firms, photographers and real estate brokers: the largest source for premium independents, and the cheapest; the QBR (quarterly business review, section 8.7) is where to ask.
- Content: case films, the founder's channels (see the kaavish-personal-ig skill), the agency's social (section 12.3 and 17-short-form-social-and-platform-craft.md), writing that shows judgement.
- Awards (section 12.5): a filter for pitch invitations and a recruiting signal.
- Outbound: researched, specific, few; the cold-email and linkedin-dm skills carry the method; the India account map in the new-business/india-agency-account-map folder tells the house who holds what and when a review is likely.
- Events and juries: Design Yatra, Goafest, Dubai Lynx week, Cannes; judging is cheaper than entering and reads better.
- Pipeline discipline: a weekly new business hour; stages (lead, qualified, chemistry, proposal, pitch, won, lost) with conversion rates per stage; qualified pipeline held at three times the revenue target because a third converts.
- Budget calendar. Indian clients set budgets for the April to March year in February and March, and spend what is left in the January to March quarter before it lapses; Gulf clients set budgets in the last quarter of the calendar year. Time outbound and credentials to land before budgets are set: December and January for India, August and September for the Gulf (house planning).
- Loss debriefs. Within two weeks of a loss, ask for 30 minutes with fixed questions: what decided it; where the house ranked on work, people and price; what the winner did that the house did not. Log the answers beside the go or no-go card (5.6), so the card's weights improve.
- Search consultants. Pitch consultants run many network-scale reviews; R3 covers Asia-based marketers, and others operate in India and the Gulf (verify the current list). Register with the active ones and keep current the credentials file they ask for.

These durations assume two rounds per stage and feedback within five working days; each extra round adds a week and each late approval adds its delay plus a re-start cost.

### 6.2 Critical path and dependencies

The critical path is the chain of dependent tasks whose total duration sets the end date; any slip on it slips delivery one for one. The recurring dependencies in this business: strategy sign-off before concepts; copy before layout (layout around lorem ipsum is redone); photography before final site design, or agreed crop ratios and placeholders; fonts licensed before guidelines ship; trademark clearance before a name is revealed to anyone outside the room; legal and claims clearance before a film shoots; client assets (product, data, logos, access) before build; Arabic and Hindi translation before bilingual layout, because line counts and reading direction change the page (see 03-typography.md, section 15). Mark the client's dependencies in the plan in their own colour; the schedule is shared, so the delay is too.

### 6.3 Gates and approvals

Gates, in order: brief sign-off; strategy sign-off; concept selection; design development sign-off; pre-production (the PPM, pre-production meeting, where boards, cast, locations, wardrobe and schedule are approved); final artwork sign-off; launch readiness. Each gate has a named approver, a written approval (an email or a tool comment), and a date. Nothing proceeds past a gate without it, and the gate is where any change-order conversation happens, because the cost of change doubles at every gate after it.

### 6.4 Buffers

Add 20 percent to every internal estimate and one week per client approval cycle; hold the buffer inside the phase durations, without a line of its own. Indian clients promise five working days and deliver ten to fifteen; Gulf throughput halves in Ramadan (expected to begin about 8 February 2027; confirm the date each year) and around the Eids; India loses the Diwali and Holi weeks and the wedding season slows founder-led clients from November to February. Plan calendars around all of these at proposal time, because a deadline that lands in Ramadan was never real.

### 6.5 The sprint model for design

For product and site work, two-week sprints with design running one sprint ahead of engineering. Ceremonies: planning (what ships this sprint), a daily 15-minute stand-up, a review where the client sees working software or finished screens, and a retrospective. A definition of done (the checklist a ticket must pass to count as complete) includes design QA against the system (see 07-design-systems-and-tokens.md). Keep critique separate from sprint review: the review is for acceptance, the crit is for craft. Linear is the house tool for product teams (issues, cycles, projects, fast keyboard-driven triage); Figma branches (section 9.2) keep the library stable while a sprint explores.

### 6.7 Status reporting and the weekly client rhythm

Monday, 30 minutes, internal traffic meeting across every live job (traffic is the function that routes work through the studio and spots collisions). Monday, the burn report for every live job (13.10). Monday, one status email per account: done last week, this week, needed from you and by when, risks, with a RAG flag (red, amber, green) and a one-line reason for anything not green. Mid-week, a work-in-progress review with the client where the stage calls for one. Friday, the internal craft review (section 10.4). The reason for the fixed rhythm: clients who hear from the house every Monday stop calling on Tuesday.

### 6.8 Risk management

A risk register per project: risk, likelihood (1 to 3), impact (1 to 3), owner, mitigation, trigger. The ten that recur: client approval delay; scope creep; the founder client changing the idea after concept selection; key freelancer unavailability (the DP booked elsewhere); weather (Mumbai monsoon June to September, Dubai heat June to September); permits (Dubai public locations need a Dubai Film and TV Commission permit; Mumbai public locations need municipal and police permissions); payment delay; vendor failure; platform spec change mid-production (see 26-platform-and-format-specs-2026.md); legal or trademark clearance failure. Each has a mitigation in this file; the register's job is to make the owner say the risk out loud on Monday.

## 7. Production management

### 7.1 Sourcing vendors

Categories the house buys: photographers and DPs; directors; editors and colourists; 3D artists; printers and finishers; fabricators (exhibition, retail, signage); composers and sound designers; voice artists; casting; locations; stylists, hair and make-up; retouchers; translators (Hindi, Marathi, Arabic). Where they are found: the house database first (section 7.9), then the portfolios of people whose work the CD has already admired (Instagram and Behance for stills and design, Vimeo for film, ArtStation for 3D), then referrals from crew already trusted. Vetting, in order of weight: portfolio match for this brief's light and feel (design-taste, SKILL.md, section 2); references from the last three jobs; reliability on dates; kit and crew; rate; usage terms; insurance; tax registration (a GST-registered vendor in India gives the house input tax credit, which is 18 percent of the invoice; section 3.6).

### 7.2 Briefing vendors

The vendor brief carries: objective; the strategy in two lines (vendors do better work when they know why); references with the exact thing borrowed from each (design-taste, SKILL.md, section 4); deliverables with specs (see 26-platform-and-format-specs-2026.md); schedule; usage terms; budget band; approval path; who from the house is on set and who signs off. Send it in writing and walk through it on a call. A vendor briefed by WhatsApp voice note delivers a WhatsApp voice note's worth of precision.

### 7.3 Quotes

Three quotes, like for like: the same deliverables, days, crew size, kit, usage and post, on one comparison sheet, or the comparison is meaningless. A production estimate lists pre-production, crew, talent, locations and permits, art and props, wardrobe, hair and make-up, equipment, transport and catering, post (edit, grade, sound, VFX, visual effects), music, insurance, a contingency of 10 percent, and the house mark-up. Exterior quotes also hold a weather day (a day of crew and kit kept in reserve for bad weather) at about 50 percent of crew fees (house planning). The cheapest quote is checked for what it left out before it is believed. Budget structure: 14-video-preproduction-and-cinematography.md, section 5.

Mark-up disclosure is contested. Procurement teams, networks and audit clauses demand a disclosed mark-up on every vendor line, the transparency standard the ANA pushed after its 2016 K2 Intelligence study of US media buying. The agency-negotiator skill takes the other position: quote a bundled production fee (one production price that includes the house's margin, with vendor costs not itemised) at 20 to 30 percent over vendor cost in India and 15 to 25 percent in the UAE, and never show the vendor's invoice. The house uses both, by client type. Procurement-led, network, Gulf government and any client with an audit clause: a disclosed mark-up of 15 to 20 percent when the house carries cost and risk, or 10 percent handling (a fee for managing a cost the client pays directly) when the client pays the vendor. Founder and private clients: a bundled production fee at the negotiator's rates. In neither case does the house take an undisclosed rebate from a vendor, because a rebate found in an audit ends the account and a bundled fee does not.

### 7.4 Production insurance

Types: public liability (injury or damage to third parties on set); equipment cover (hired kit is the hirer's responsibility, and a single cinema lens can cost Rs 10 lakh); production or shoot insurance (negative and media, weather, cast non-appearance) for large shoots; employee compensation for crew (in India under the Code on Social Security 2020, in force since 21 November 2025, which absorbed the Employees' Compensation Act 1923); professional indemnity for the agency itself (section 14.10). Rule: no shoot without public liability and kit cover in place and named on the call sheet; check whether the client's or the production company's policy already covers the day before buying a second one.

### 7.5 Contracts and releases

Crew deal memos (role, days, rate, overtime, credit, IP); talent releases (usage term, territory, media, fee, exclusivity, right to alter, no obligation to use); property and location releases; parental consent for minors; composer and voice agreements with assignment or licence terms; a release written in a language the signatory reads (Hindi, Marathi or Arabic as needed), because consent that was not understood is not consent, and the names of the house's model release forms are kept in the vendor database.

### 7.6 On-set management

A call sheet the evening before (times, locations, contacts, weather, nearest hospital, the shot list, and the on-set conduct contact of section 11.10; anatomy in 14-video-preproduction-and-cinematography.md, section 3.6). The house's roles on set: the CD or AD approves frames against the boards; the producer guards the schedule and the money; one client representative with one voice, agreed in advance. The shot list is signed off before wrap. Shift rules: Indian film crews work a 12-hour shift with overtime after it; Dubai crews 10 to 12 hours; heat protocols in the Gulf summer; Ramadan hours for fasting crew. A wrap report the same day: what was shot, what was missed, what changed, what it cost.

### 7.7 Post schedules and approvals

Offline cut v1 within five to seven working days of wrap; two rounds of offline; picture lock (the point after which the edit's timing no longer changes); then online, grade, sound design, music and voice; then deliverables per spec. Clients approve picture lock before grade begins, because every change after grade is paid for twice. Approvals live in Frame.io with timestamped comments (section 9.5); the editorial workflow itself is in 16-editing-colour-grading-and-delivery.md, section 5, and the post schedule in 14-video-preproduction-and-cinematography.md, section 5.3.

### 7.8 Delivery and archiving

Deliver the package of section 9.4 with a delivery note. Archive: freeze the project folder; keep camera originals and RAW for two years on LTO (Linear Tape-Open, archival magnetic tape) or cold cloud storage (Backblaze B2, AWS Glacier or Google Archive class); keep final masters and source files indefinitely; record usage expiry dates in the delivery note and in the house calendar with a 60-day reminder. The reason for the two-year RAW rule: re-use requests arrive at 18 months, when the client's next campaign is planned.

### 7.10 Events and experiential production

Events are a house service line (Lollapalooza India is a house client, per agency-ceo), and they carry more licences, deposits and physical risk than any shoot. The paper and the plan:

- A run of show (the minute-by-minute script of the event, with cues and owners) and an event P&L (profit and loss account) kept live from the first estimate to the strike.
- The venue contract, with its deposit schedule written into the client's payment plan. Vendor deposits run at about 50 percent on booking (house planning), so the client's 50 to 70 percent advance (3.8) lands first.
- India: police permission; a fire NOC (no-objection certificate) from the fire department; PPL (Phonographic Performance Limited) and IPRS licences for music; a temporary liquor licence from the state excise department where alcohol is served (in Maharashtra, an occasional licence); FSSAI (Food Safety and Standards Authority of India) registration for caterers; a structural stability certificate for stages and rigging. Verify each per state and city.
- Dubai: an event permit through the Department of Economy and Tourism's permit system, with Dubai Police and Civil Defence approvals and an event liquor licence; Abu Dhabi through DCT Abu Dhabi, the Department of Culture and Tourism. Verify each per event.
- Insurance: event cancellation and abandonment; public liability at the venue's minimum; non-appearance cover for named talent.
- A crowd safety and medical plan with accessible routes, and a load-in and strike schedule agreed with the venue.
- Weather plans for the Gulf summer (May to September) and the Indian monsoon (June to September).
- A documentation shoot in the budget, because the case study (12.4) is made on the night or never.

Launch timing for experiential work is in 19-campaigns-and-creative-direction.md, sections 6.5 and 14.1; event branding and fabrication tolerances are in 20-print-packaging-ooh-and-physical-production.md, sections 8.5 and 8.6. The reason for the list: an event has one date, so every missing permit is a cancellation.

### 7.11 Moving crew and kit between India and the Gulf

- Crew. A UAE visit visa does not permit work. Shoots in Dubai run through a Dubai-licensed production service company that holds the Dubai Film and TV Commission permit and arranges crew permissions (verify the mechanism per shoot). Saudi shoots need business visas and a Saudi Film Commission permit (14-video-preproduction-and-cinematography.md, section 4.4).
- Kit. An ATA Carnet (an international customs document for temporary duty-free import of goods) is issued by FICCI (the Federation of Indian Chambers of Commerce and Industry) in India and by Dubai Chambers in the UAE; check each destination's acceptance for professional equipment on the World ATA Carnet Council list before relying on it. Otherwise pay a refundable customs deposit, or hire kit locally in Dubai, which is often cheaper than shipping it.
- Money. Crew travel, per diems and visas are the client's cost unless the SOW says otherwise (3.9), and payments to Indian freelancers for work abroad still carry TDS (3.6).

The reason: a crew stopped at immigration or a camera held at customs costs the shoot day, and the shoot day is the most expensive day in the budget.

## 8. Client management

### 8.1 Onboarding and immersion

Kick-off within five working days of signature. Immersion: visit the property, the store, the showroom or the site; read the last three years of the brand's work; interview five to eight stakeholders for 30 minutes each; hear the founder's story from the founder; collect assets and guidelines; audit the brand against the category (design-taste, references/worlds.md). Set up the shared workspace, the review tools and the onboarding document: contacts, approvers, cadence, tools, invoicing details, PO process, vendor registration on the client's system (which in India can take three weeks and gates every payment). The reason: most later friction is set in the first two weeks, when the house either learnt who decides or did not.

### 8.2 Stakeholder mapping, decision makers and approvers

Map every account: the decider (signs off; the founder or CMO), the approvers (sign off on stages), the influencers (the founder's spouse, the architect, the brand consultant, the agency the client used before), the gatekeepers (procurement, legal, the executive assistant), and the users (the marketing manager who lives with the work daily). Write the map, agree in the SOW who approves what, and insist on a decider check-in at concept selection even when the decider skips the other reviews, because a decider who sees the work for the first time at final artwork resets the project to concept.

### 8.3 The single point of feedback

The SOW names one person through whom all feedback is consolidated within five working days of a presentation; conflicting views are resolved on the client side before they reach the house; the house does not act on feedback from anyone else, including the decider's late-night message, until it comes through that person. The reason: unconsolidated feedback doubles rounds and the house is paid for two.

### 8.4 Rounds policy, difficult feedback and the taste education

Rounds are defined in section 3.4. Present every stage with a recommendation and the reasons for it; a presentation of options with no opinion asks the client to be the creative director. Handling feedback: listen to the end, restate it, separate the problem from the proposed fix ("make the logo bigger" is a fix; the problem is "I cannot see the brand", which has better fixes), and answer the problem. Taste education happens over months: references shown at every stage, the physics of expensive explained in plain words (design-taste, SKILL.md, section 2), the cover-the-logo test done together, and the category's tropes named and refused in the brief (design-taste, SKILL.md, section 4.3). Never argue taste in email; call, then confirm in writing. When the feedback is "I don't like it", ask what it would need to feel like and return to the single idea agreed at the brief; if the idea has changed, that is a change order and a conversation with the decider.

### 8.5 Scope and expectation setting, and saying no

Set expectations at the brief: what premium costs in rounds, weeks and money, and what the client must bring. Say no to unpriced scope, timelines that force cheap work, feedback outside the protocol, and work that would hurt the brand, because the house's name goes on it. The form of no: acknowledge the request, state the consequence (cost, time, quality), offer the alternative with a price. A no with a priced alternative is a yes the client can afford.

### 8.6 Client archetypes

- The founder client (most of the house's luxury clients): fast, intuitive, changes mind, buys taste and people, and messages at midnight. Needs the decider check-in, the single idea in one sentence they can repeat, a 20-minute weekly call, and a named senior on the account. Beware the influencer circle (section 8.2) and the WhatsApp approval that the marketing team never saw.
- The marketing manager client: needs to look good to a boss. Wants rationale, documentation, options and safety. Give them the deck that sells the work internally, the numbers, and a rehearsal before they present it upward.
- The procurement conversation: procurement measures rates, discounts and compliance. Move the conversation from rate to scope: three options (section 3.1), scope removed before price is cut, the rate card ranges the networks quote held in reserve, the scoring criteria asked for, and the decider kept informed, because procurement cannot buy taste and knows it.
- The local agency of a global maison (much of the house's luxury work: Dior, L'Oreal, Dyson, St. Regis, per agency-ceo). Origination sits with the head office and its global agency; the house adapts, activates and produces locally (social, PR, events, retail, local shoots). Rules: read the global usage matrix before placing any asset (3.7); price adaptation per asset and count head-office approval rounds as rounds; add one to three weeks of head-office brand approval to every launch (house planning); work inside the maison's own DAM and brand portal; shoot local content to the maison's global photography guidelines, with photographer and model choices signed off by the head office before booking. The reason: the head office judges the local market by its compliance before its creativity, and a usage breach on a global asset is the fastest way to lose the account.

### 8.7 Relationship rhythm

Weekly status (section 6.7). Monthly review for retainers: output, results, next month's plan. A QBR (quarterly business review) with the decider: results, learning, roadmap, relationship health, and the question "what would make us better for you". Annual planning in the last quarter of the client's fiscal year (April to March in India; calendar year in the UAE) with a one-page "state of the brand" and the next year's scope and fee. Reviews are also where referrals and case study permissions are asked for, at the moment of a success.

### 8.8 Churn signals

Slower approvals; a new marketing head; a request for "benchmarking" rates or a procurement review; reduced scope at renewal; missed QBRs; another agency's name in conversation; late payment where it was punctual; a request for all source files and passwords. Response: a direct conversation with the decider within a week, a re-pitch plan where the account is worth it, and a clean exit plan where it is not. Waiting is the only wrong move.

### 8.9 Upsell and cross-sell with integrity

Propose only what the brand needs and the house can deliver at the same standard; put the "what next" page in the QBR; never bundle a weak discipline to fill a retainer; where the house subcontracts (a specialist 3D studio, a media buyer), say so and price it under the mark-up rule of section 7.3. The reason: a premium house's cross-sell is believed exactly as long as it has never been padding.

### 8.10 References, case studies and the breakup

Ask for the case study, the reference and the testimonial in launch week, with the case format of section 12.4. The breakup comes when an account stays unprofitable after re-scoping, when conduct is abusive to staff, or when the client's taste and the house's cannot meet. Give notice per the contract, hand over cleanly (files, passwords, vendor contacts, a transition document), say nothing in public, and keep the door open; clients return and decision makers move companies.

### 8.11 Sensitivity review and backlash

Before launch, a sensitivity review (a read by someone outside the team) checks every piece for religion, caste, region, gender, national symbols and rulers' images, and, for the Gulf, the Ramadan and public-morals standards of section 14.7; the review goes into the approval trail with the reader's name and date. Every creator's back catalogue is read before booking (17-short-form-social-and-platform-craft.md, section 5.9). On launch, social listening runs for 72 hours with a holding statement (a short prepared response for the first hours of a controversy) drafted in advance. If a backlash starts, the decision tree has four branches: hold (monitor, no change), edit (fix the specific element), pause paid media, or withdraw. The client decides and the client speaks; house staff make no public comment on any channel, their own included. Indian precedents: Tanishq's "Ekatvam" (October 2020, withdrawn after threats; 19-campaigns-and-creative-direction.md, section 9.3), Fabindia's "Jashn-e-Riwaaz" (October 2021, withdrawn) and Dabur Fem's Karva Chauth film (October 2021, withdrawn after a Madhya Pradesh minister's warning). The reason for the record: the review trail is the house's defence when the client asks, after the fact, who approved the work.

### 8.12 Conflicts and exclusivity

No two direct competitors in the same category and city on brand work without both clients' written consent; where both consent, separate teams and keep files behind access controls (9.11). Exclusivity is granted only against a named list of competitors, for the engagement plus at most six months, and it is priced: a minimum retainer or a 10 to 25 percent premium (house planning). Conflicts are checked at the first enquiry, before the chemistry meeting, against the client list and the new-business account map. The reason: in luxury hospitality, jewellery and real estate a small house is asked for exclusivity constantly, and unpriced exclusivity gives away the next client in the category.

## 9. Creative operations

### 9.1 File structure and naming conventions

One structure for every project, so that anyone can find anything in a project they have never opened:

```
CLIENT_Project_2026/
  01_Admin            proposal, SOW, PO, invoices, approvals
  02_Brief_Strategy   brief, strategy, research
  03_References       reference boards, competitor audit
  04_Assets_In        client-supplied, dated subfolders
  05_Design           Figma links, working files by discipline
  06_Copy             copy decks, translations
  07_Production       shoot, post, print, vendor quotes
  08_Review           every export sent to the client, by round
  09_Final            approved masters and the delivery package
  10_Archive          frozen at close
```

Naming: CLIENT_Project_Deliverable_Variant_v03_20261014_KR.ext. Versions run v01 to v99 and the word "final" never appears in a filename; the 09_Final folder is the only "final". Dates in ISO 8601 order (YYYYMMDD) so files sort by time. No spaces or special characters; Devanagari and Arabic in filenames break some render pipelines and CDNs (content delivery networks), so transliterate. Review exports carry the round (R1, R2). The reason: naming is retrieval, and a file that cannot be found is a file that gets remade.

### 9.2 Version control for design

Figma branching and merging are available on the Organization and Enterprise plans (and the Education K-12 and Government plans), with no cap on the number of branches; creating a branch needs a Full seat, and merging needs edit access to the main file, with a review step for anyone without it (Figma help centre, read October 2026). Use branches for explorations on a published library and for keeping the client-visible file stable; name a version in version history at every gate ("R2 presented 2026-10-14"). Abstract, the git-style version control built for Sketch, is legacy and the house does not run Sketch. Code lives in Git on GitHub, trunk-based (everyone merges small changes into one main branch daily) with short-lived branches and pull request review; design tokens live in the repo (see 07-design-systems-and-tokens.md).

### 9.3 Asset management, DAM and brand portals

A DAM (digital asset management system) is the searchable, permissioned library of approved assets with metadata and usage terms. Tools: Bynder and Brandfolder (now part of Smartsheet) for enterprise libraries; Frontify for brand guidelines with an integrated library, the house default for client brand portals because guidelines, assets and downloadable formats sit in one place; Dropbox and Google Drive for working files. Drive structure: a Shared Drive per client, because a file in a person's My Drive leaves with that person, with the project structure of section 9.1 inside. A brand portal is the living guidelines site (Frontify, or a custom Next.js site for identities where the house wants control of the typography); for identities above about Rs 15 lakh or AED 75,000 the house delivers a portal and a PDF, because a PDF is out of date the day a new asset is made.

The risk: a format applied unedited produces the sameness design-taste lists as a cheap tell ("everything the same width and weight", SKILL.md, section 3), so every use still goes through the edit (SKILL.md, section 4.6).

### 9.7 QA gates and checklists

The checklists per deliverable are in 23-critique-taste-and-craft-judgement.md, section 5. The operational rule here: no deliverable leaves the building without its checklist signed by someone other than its maker. The items that recur across every checklist: spelling and grammar in every language; legal lines and disclaimers; logo clear space; colour profile; bleed; font embedding; link checks; alt text and captions (11-accessibility-and-inclusive-design.md); platform specs; usage expiry on the assets used; the AI label where section 14.6 requires it.

### 9.8 Post mortems and retros

Within two weeks of delivery, 45 minutes, the team and the account lead: what was planned, what happened, what was learnt, what changes (one to three changes with owners and dates). Blameless by rule. The numbers on the table: estimated versus actual hours by role (3.3), rounds planned versus actual, realisation and the write-off reason codes (13.10), gross margin, the client's satisfaction score. Every learning either changes a reference file in this corpus (section 9.9) or is discarded; a learning that lives in a meeting note is forgotten in a month.

### 9.9 Knowledge management

This skill corpus is the house's knowledge system: a written, versioned, cross-referenced reference that a new person or Claude reads before acting. Rules: every file has an owner and an as-of date; claims that can change carry their date; post mortems feed it; a quarterly review retires what is stale; the operational layer (clients, vendors, projects, rates) lives in Notion and the craft layer lives here. The reason to write it down: taste that lives only in the founder's head leaves when he is on a plane.

### 9.10 Time tracking, utilisation and capacity planning

Utilisation is billable hours divided by available hours. Available hours per person per year are about 1,800 to 1,900 after public holidays (10 to 15 in India depending on state; about 14 in the UAE) and leave (15 to 24 days). Targets: 65 to 75 percent for makers, 40 to 50 percent for CDs, 20 to 30 percent for the founder. Track in Harvest or Toggl, review weekly. Below 60 percent means too few projects or too much unbilled work; above 80 percent means no time for crits, references or rest, and the quality of the next job pays. Capacity planning: a rolling 12-week view of booked hours per person against capacity, booked to 80 percent so the unexpected fits; hire (section 13.8) when the view runs above 90 percent for six consecutive weeks with pipeline to support it.

### 9.11 Security, access and embargo

Gulf government entities, networks and luxury maisons send security questionnaires at vendor onboarding, and one leaked launch costs the account. Baseline: SSO (single sign-on, one identity login for every tool) with enforced two-factor authentication on Google Workspace, Figma, Slack and Notion; a password manager (1Password or Bitwarden) with a vault per client; managed laptops; access revoked within 24 hours of anyone leaving, freelancers included. Client-owned accounts: ad accounts, pixels, domains and social handles belong to the client, with the house granted partner access, and they are never held in the house's own Meta Business Manager (domains: 21-web-design-and-digital-craft.md, section 7.10). Embargoed launches: watermarked review links (check the review tool's plan for watermarking), named viewers, downloads off until release, and code names in file names. A one-page security statement stays ready for questionnaires that ask about ISO/IEC 27001 (the international information security management standard) or SOC 2 (the US service-organisation controls audit) controls, stating plainly which controls the house meets. The reason: a maison's launch date is its most guarded fact, and the house is judged on the leak it did not cause as much as on the work.

## 10. Quality system

### 10.1 The CD sign-off

Nothing reaches a client without the CD's sign-off, recorded in the review tool ("Approved by CD, date"). The CD reviews against the brief, the single idea, the taste tests (design-taste, SKILL.md, section 4.7) and the deliverable checklist (10.2). The sign-off means the CD owns the failure if it fails; that is the point of it.

### 10.2 Checklists per deliverable

Held in 23-critique-taste-and-craft-judgement.md, section 5, one per deliverable type (identity, deck, website, app screen, film, social post, print, OOH, campaign idea), each run by a second pair of eyes. Guidelines have no checklist there yet, so they go through the identity checklist plus a page-by-page read by the CD. The quality system here only enforces the gate: a deliverable without a signed checklist is not finished.

### 10.3 The standards document

Two to four pages of the house's non-negotiables per discipline, drawn from the corpus: type setting (03-typography.md), colour (04-colour.md), motion (12-motion-principles-and-ui-motion.md), copy (the mask360-copywriting skill), the house layer (design-taste, SKILL.md, section 7), file naming (9.1), the AI rules (4.9 and 14.6). Read on day one, signed, revised quarterly. The reason for keeping it short: a 40-page standards document is read once; a 3-page one is used.

### 10.4 Calibration sessions and the craft review cadence

Calibration: once a month, the team scores five pieces of work (three from the house, two from outside) as expensive, competent or cheap, independently and in writing, then compares. The purpose is agreement on the scale, so that "cheap" means the same thing in every mouth, and the disagreements are where the teaching happens. Craft review: every Friday, 60 minutes, work in progress, in the critique format of design-taste, SKILL.md, section 8 (verdict, what works, what does not and why, the fix with the reference). Quarterly: a portfolio review of everything shipped, with the question of which pieces the house would show.

### 10.5 Measuring quality

| Measure | Definition | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Rework rate | Hours spent after client approval divided by total hours | Under 10 percent |
| Rounds per stage | Average rounds actually used | 2 or fewer on 80 percent of stages |
| Out-of-scope revisions | Count of revision requests priced as change orders | Tracked per client; rising count is a churn signal |
| On-time delivery | Milestones met on the agreed date | 90 percent |
| Awards | Entries, shortlists, wins per year | Shortlist rate above 30 percent of entries |
| Referrals | New clients introduced by existing ones | Half of new business |
| Satisfaction | A 1 to 10 score at every QBR | 8 or above |
| Portfolio inclusion | Share of shipped projects the CD would show | Above 50 percent |

The reason for measuring: a quality system without numbers is a mood.

## 11. Talent

### 11.1 Hiring for taste

Portfolio review (method in 23-critique-taste-and-craft-judgement.md, section 11): five minutes per portfolio, the two-second test, cover the logo, look for the edit (what they left out) and for one project they can explain in depth. Interview: a 30-minute walk through one project including the rejected routes, which shows judgement in a way the final never does. A paid test of four to eight hours at freelance rate, on a brief the house has already answered, because a live client brief would make the test spec work by another name. A paid trial project of two to four weeks. Two references with one question: "would you hire them again, and for what". The reason for the trial: a portfolio shows the best 5 percent of someone's work; the trial shows the median, and the house ships the median.

### 11.6 Culture of craft

Premium work needs thinking time, so the house protects it: a no-meeting block every morning; a 45-hour week as the planning norm with overtime tracked and repaid in time off; no weekend pitch work without agreement and compensation; a studio ritual list (Monday traffic, Friday crit, monthly calibration, a quarterly field trip to a show, a hotel, a printer or a foundry; the reference wall; an annual studio book of the year's work). Burnout signals: missed crits, defensive feedback, late nights as a habit, falling utilisation with rising hours. The response is a conversation and a workload change in the same week. The reason: the taste the house sells lives in rested heads, and a tired senior approves cheap work.

### 11.7 Performance reviews for creatives

Twice a year, portfolio-based (what was made, before and after, what was removed), competency-based (the six of 11.2), and behavioural (crit contribution, reliability, client conduct). Input from the crit record and from two peers. The CD gives the taste verdict in the critique format. The output is a growth plan with named projects for the next six months and a compensation decision once a year (April in India, January in the UAE, to match budget cycles).

### 11.8 Letting go

For performance: a written 60 to 90 day plan with observable outputs, weekly check-ins, then exit with notice per contract (30 to 90 days is typical in India under the employment contract and the state Shops and Establishments Acts, with wages due on exit paid within two working days under the Code on Wages 2019; the UAE Labour Law, Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021, sets notice at 30 to 90 days and end-of-service gratuity). For redundancy: say it plainly, on the day, with the most generous terms the house can afford, portfolio help and a reference. Never by message. The reason beyond decency: the market is small, and the person leaving will be on a client's side of the table within two years.

## 13. Finance basics for creative leaders

### 13.1 Revenue per head

Revenue per head is annual fee revenue (excluding pass-through production costs) divided by total headcount. It falls out of the rate card and utilisation: a mid designer at Rs 3,000 an hour, 1,850 available hours, 70 percent utilisation yields about Rs 38.9 lakh a year; a senior at Rs 5,000 and 65 percent yields about Rs 60 lakh; a CD at Rs 10,000 and 45 percent yields about Rs 83 lakh; account, ops and finance staff bill nothing directly. Blended across a 15-person house (section 2.2) that gives about Rs 8 to 15 crore, or Rs 55 lakh to 1 crore per head: the ceiling at full card rates, before realisation (13.10), write-offs and the non-billable roles a real house carries. The working targets are the agency-ceo skill's, by stage:

| Stage (annual fee income) | People per Rs 1 crore of fee income | Revenue per head, India (about) | What moves it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seed, Rs 0 to 3 crore | 4 to 6 | Rs 15 to 25 lakh | Founder hours; retainers at the negotiator's floors |
| Early growth, Rs 3 to 10 crore | 4 to 5 | Rs 20 to 30 lakh | First department heads; projects priced off this file's cards |
| Scale, Rs 10 to 25 crore | 3 to 4 | Rs 25 to 40 lakh | Utilisation discipline; flagship projects; a growing Gulf share |
| Mature, Rs 25 crore and above | 2.5 to 3.5 | Rs 35 to 50 lakh | Premium rates held; more juniors per senior |

Dubai clients pay two to three times Indian rates for equivalent work (agency-ceo), so plan a Dubai office at AED 350,000 to 600,000 per head once it passes ten people (house planning). Below the band for the stage, rates, realisation or utilisation is wrong, and section 13.10 shows which.

### 13.2 Gross margin

Gross margin is fee revenue minus the direct cost of delivery (billable staff cost at loaded rates, freelancers, and pass-through costs billed at cost), divided by fee revenue. Targets: 50 to 60 percent on projects; about 50 percent on retainers, rising when the client under-uses the capacity it pays for and falling when the house over-services (13.6); 15 to 25 percent net after overhead. The agency-negotiator skill sets 40 percent gross as the floor on any job, and that floor holds here. Worked example: a Rs 20 lakh identity uses 320 staff hours at a loaded cost of Rs 2,000 (Rs 6.4 lakh) and Rs 1.5 lakh of freelance, so gross margin is Rs 12.1 lakh (60 percent); overhead allocated at 35 percent of revenue is Rs 7 lakh; net is Rs 5.1 lakh (25 percent). Add one extra round at 40 hours (Rs 0.8 lakh) and the net falls to Rs 4.3 lakh; add three, and net falls to Rs 2.7 lakh (14 percent); add six, and the project is at about break-even. That is why section 3.4 counts rounds.

### 13.3 Overhead and the loaded cost

Overhead is every cost that is not a billable person: rent, software, admin and finance staff, new business, insurance, the founder's non-billable time. Premium independents run 30 to 40 percent of revenue. Loaded cost per hour equals (annual salary multiplied by about 1.2 for statutory and benefits, plus the person's share of overhead) divided by billable hours. Example: a designer on Rs 12 lakh CTC costs Rs 14.4 lakh loaded, carries Rs 6 lakh of overhead, and bills 1,300 hours a year, so the loaded cost is about Rs 1,570 an hour; at a Rs 3,500 rate the hour earns 55 percent gross margin. Every rate on the card in section 3.2 was built this way, which is why it does not move for a client who asks nicely.

### 13.4 Pricing to margin

Price equals hours multiplied by loaded cost, divided by one minus the target margin, plus third-party costs with mark-up; then adjust upward to value (section 3.1) where the client's upside justifies it. Never price below loaded cost plus 40 percent except for a documented reason (a portfolio piece, a category the house wants to enter), capped at 10 percent of annual revenue, because every discounted job is paid for by the others.

### 13.5 Cash flow and the receivables problem in India

DSO (days sales outstanding) is receivables divided by daily revenue; Indian independents run 60 to 120 days against a contractual 30. Cash rules: three months of overhead in the bank at all times; advances and milestones as in section 3.5; invoice the day the milestone lands, on the client's portal; a weekly receivables call. The statutory lever: under section 15 of the MSMED Act 2006, a buyer must pay a registered micro or small enterprise within the agreed period, which cannot exceed 45 days (15 days where nothing is agreed); under section 16, delayed payment carries compound interest at three times the RBI bank rate; from assessment year 2024 to 2025, section 43B(h) of the Income-tax Act 1961 (renumbered in the 2025 Act; confirm the new reference) denies the buyer a tax deduction for sums owed to micro and small enterprises beyond that period, which made finance departments notice the rule. Udyam registration is the key to all of it; the revised thresholds announced in the Union Budget 2025 and in force from 1 April 2025 put a micro enterprise at investment up to Rs 2.5 crore and turnover up to Rs 10 crore and a small enterprise at Rs 25 crore and Rs 100 crore, so a 15-person house qualifies. Disputes go to the MSME Samadhaan portal and the state Facilitation Council. Receivables from large buyers can also be discounted on the TReDS (Trade Receivables Discounting System) platforms (RXIL, M1xchange, Invoicemart). In the UAE the levers are the advance, the LPO (local purchase order, without which nothing is paid), the post-dated cheque, and the DIFC Courts' small claims track for amounts up to AED 500,000 when the contract opts in.

### 13.6 Retainers as stability

Hold 40 to 60 percent of revenue in retainers. Design them as capacity in days per month, so the client buys the team's time and the house sets the output mix, with unused days expiring monthly or rolling one month, a quarterly re-scope, an annual price review tied to the rate card, and 60 to 90 days' notice. Show every retainer two ways: days per month, and an FTE (full-time equivalent) staffing plan, for example 0.2 CD, 0.5 AD, 1.0 designer and 0.3 account lead, priced at about 2.5 to 3.5 times direct salary cost (house planning multiplier), which is the language procurement, networks and luxury head offices price in. Reconcile monthly, hours used against plan by role: over-servicing above 110 percent for two consecutive months triggers a re-scope or a price change at the next quarterly review, and under-use below 80 percent for a quarter is raised by the house before the client raises it. Small social scopes use the retainer tiers of the agency-negotiator skill. A retainer paid monthly in advance is worth more than its margin in India, because it is the only revenue that arrives on a date.

### 13.7 The cost of a pitch

Pitch cost and the go or no-go card are sections 5.4 and 5.6; record actual hours per pitch in the time system so both stay honest.

### 13.8 When to hire

Hire when booked capacity runs above 90 percent for six consecutive weeks and three months of the role's loaded cost is covered by contracted work; otherwise freelance for three months first. Order of hires for a growing house: a producer or project manager before another designer (the bottleneck is coordination, section 2.2); a design engineer before a developer; a senior before a junior, because juniors need seniors to become anything.

### 13.9 Pricing AI-augmented work

Three cases. Where AI cuts the hours on variants and adaptations, pass part of the saving through: adaptation sets priced per unit at about half of their 2023 price, and the volume won on it. Where AI is the medium (generated film, synthetic imagery, voice), price the idea and the direction as creative fees, pass the tool costs through with mark-up (Higgsfield, Runway, Midjourney, Kling and similar credits), and disclose per sections 4.9 and 14.6. Where AI is in ideation and process, nothing changes in the price; it is the house's method. Value pricing stays for ideas, identities and films (section 1.6), and the client is shown the machine wherever the machine is doing machine work.

### 13.10 Realisation and job burn

Utilisation (9.10) says whether people are busy; realisation says whether the busy hours were paid for. Realisation is fee invoiced divided by hours logged multiplied by the rate-card rate: target 85 to 100 percent on fixed fees, and investigate below 80. The effective hourly rate is fee divided by hours logged. Yield per person is available hours multiplied by utilisation, realisation and rate: a mid designer at 1,850 hours, 70 percent, 95 percent and Rs 3,000 yields about Rs 36.9 lakh a year.

The burn report, every Monday for each live job (6.7): budget hours, hours burnt, percent complete (the producer's estimate) and forecast hours at completion. A job that has burnt 50 percent of its hours before concept approval gets a CD and account-lead review that day, which ends in either a change order (3.4) or a written write-off decision. Write-offs (hours the house will not bill) are logged with a reason code: extra round, internal rework, estimate wrong, unpriced client change. The post mortem (9.8) reads the codes, because a code that repeats is a process problem.

Worked example: a Rs 20 lakh identity budgeted at 320 hours at a blended Rs 5,000 an hour has a rate value of Rs 16 lakh, so planned realisation is 125 percent, which is value pricing at work. If the job takes 450 hours, the rate value is Rs 22.5 lakh, realisation falls to 89 percent and the effective hourly rate to about Rs 4,440; the Monday report shows the slide by the concept gate, while a change order is still possible.

### 13.11 Concentration and the shape of the P&L

AGI (agency gross income) is revenue after pass-through costs (media, production and creator fees billed at cost), and it is the denominator for every ratio in this section. Keep the largest client under 20 to 25 percent of AGI and the top three under 50 percent. Above 30 percent the client sets the terms, and its exit removes about a third of payroll cover. Protections for a large client: 90 days' notice in its contract, a reserve of three months of the payroll that client funds, and a new-business target that dilutes it within 12 months. The reason: one maison at 30 to 40 percent of fees is the most common way an agency of this size fails.

Firm shape as a share of AGI (house planning):

| Line | Share of AGI | Signal when outside the range |
|---|---|---|
| Delivery staff (billable salaries and freelancers) | 40 to 45 percent | Above: rates too low or over-servicing; below: under-staffed or leaning on the founder |
| Non-billable staff (operations, finance, new business, unbilled account time) | 12 to 15 percent | Above: coordination cost outrunning revenue (2.2) |
| Non-staff overhead (rent, software, insurance, travel, awards, legal) | 15 to 20 percent | Above: office or tooling ahead of revenue |
| Operating profit | 15 to 25 percent | Below 15: no reserve for a lost client |

Receivables ageing (unpaid invoices grouped by days outstanding) is reported monthly in four buckets: 0 to 30, 31 to 60, 61 to 90, and over 90 days. Anything over 90 days goes founder to founder, and anything owed by a buyer to a Udyam-registered house past 45 days goes to the MSMED route of section 13.5.

## 14. Legal and ethics

### 14.1 Trademark checks

Before any name or mark is presented: a knock-out search (a quick search for identical or near-identical marks) on the IP India public search (ipindiaonline.gov.in) under the Trade Marks Act 1999 in the relevant Nice classes (the international classification of goods and services used for trademark filing: 3 cosmetics, 14 jewellery, 25 clothing, 35 advertising and retail, 36 real estate, 41 entertainment, 43 hospitality); the UAE Ministry of Economy trademark register for the Gulf; the WIPO (World Intellectual Property Organization) Global Brand Database for international marks; domain and social handle availability (naming process and Indian and UAE naming law: 18-brand-strategy-and-identity-design.md, sections 2.2 to 2.6). The house's search is indicative and the proposal says so; a lawyer's clearance opinion precedes launch, and the Madrid Protocol (India since 2013, UAE since 2021) is the route to international filings. The reason: a name revealed and then withdrawn costs the client the launch and the house the account.

### 14.2 Model and property releases

As in section 7.5. Personality rights in India are judge-made: Titan Industries v Ramkumar Jewellers (Delhi High Court, 2012) on using a celebrity's image without consent, and Anil Kapoor v Simply Life India (Delhi High Court, 2023) protecting a performer's name, voice and likeness against AI misuse. In the UAE, photographing or publishing a person's image without consent is an offence under the cybercrime law (Federal Decree-Law No. 34 of 2021) and the Penal Code, with fines and imprisonment, so no street photography of identifiable people in Dubai without a release.

### 14.3 Music sync

Both a master licence (the recording, from the label) and a synchronisation licence (the composition, from the publisher) are needed for any commercial recording; library platforms (Artlist, Epidemic Sound, Musicbed) bundle both within their plan terms, and paid-media coverage must be confirmed per plan; Indian film music sits with labels (T-Series, Saregama, Sony Music India, Zee Music) and is priced per section 3.7; events and public playback in India need IPRS (composition) and PPL (recording) licences (7.10). Cue sheets go with every film.

### 14.4 Font licensing

Desktop, web, app, broadcast and logo licences are separate products at most foundries, and a wordmark drawn from a licensed font may need a logo licence; the client buys fonts in its own name; the house never ships font files outside the EULA. Detail in 03-typography.md, section 16.

### 14.5 Stock and image licensing

Getty, Shutterstock, Adobe Stock, Stocksy and Unsplash+ for commercially released imagery; editorial licences do not cover advertising; sensitive-use clauses exclude certain categories; model and property releases are checked per image. The house avoids stock and generic AI imagery for the reasons in design-taste, SKILL.md, section 2; where AI imagery is used, section 14.6 governs.

### 14.6 AI disclosure

ASCI's Guidelines for Responsible Labelling of Synthetically Generated Content in Advertising were released on 29 September 2026 after a May 2026 draft and public consultation, and take effect three months after publication, on 29 December 2026 (Khaitan and Co, October 2026). Synthetically generated content (SGC) is audio, visual or audio-visual content in an advertisement that is artificially or algorithmically created or materially altered to appear real. The guidelines sort advertising use of SGC into three classes: prohibited, mandatory labelling, and no labelling required. They sit beside the IT Rules 2021 as amended in February 2026, which require intermediaries (the platforms) to label all SGC; the ASCI rule binds the advertiser and applies where the content materially influences a consumer decision. Labelling is mandatory where SGC materially influences a consumer decision; the guideline's own examples include synthetic influencers and ambassadors, a real person's replicated likeness or voice even with consent, fabricated events or settings, a product that does not yet exist ("a 3D model of an unbuilt housing complex", which is every real estate launch the house works on), product-relevant sound design, and paid AI recommendations, which must read "Sponsored by [Brand]". No label is needed for minor enhancements (colour correction, blemish removal, copy refinement), decorative backgrounds and ambient sound, obviously fantastical effects, text and administrative uses, and accessibility aids. Suggested labels are "Audio/Video created using AI" and "Audio/Video enhanced using AI". A label never cures prohibited content: fabricated endorsements, exaggerated results, non-existent locations, unauthorised copyrighted work, deepfakes or likeness without consent breach the code regardless. The house's own practice is in 22-ai-era-craft-and-generative-tools.md, sections 5 (tells), 7.4 (likeness and voice), 7.6 (disclosure) and 8.4 (the client ethics note); that file's 7.6 table predates the final ASCI guideline and the National Media Authority, and this section governs where the two differ. For EU-facing work, the EU AI Act's transparency obligations for synthetic content (Article 50) apply from 2 August 2026 under the Act's original timetable (check whether the EU's late 2025 digital omnibus proposals have moved any of it); for the Gulf, the National Media Authority's content rules (14.7) and each platform's own synthetic-media labels apply.

### 14.7 The ASCI code and UAE advertising standards

The ASCI (Advertising Standards Council of India) code has four chapters: truthful and honest representation, non-offensive to public decency, no harmful products or situations, and fair competition; Chapter III was last updated on 29 April 2024 and Chapter I carries the chairman's signature of 10 July 2025 (ascionline.in, read October 2026). Its guidelines include influencer advertising in digital media (May 2021: disclosure labels such as #ad or #collab, prominent, in the language of the post, for any material connection), harmful gender stereotypes (June 2022), and the synthetic content guideline above. ASCI is self-regulatory, and the statutory teeth sit in the Consumer Protection Act 2019 and the guidelines of the CCPA (Central Consumer Protection Authority), the Guidelines for Prevention of Misleading Advertisements 2022 (penalties up to Rs 10 lakh for a first violation and Rs 50 lakh after). Real estate adds RERA: every advertisement for a registered project carries the RERA registration number and unregistered projects may not be advertised (Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act 2016; MahaRERA in Maharashtra), and in Dubai property advertising needs a permit number from the Dubai Land Department's Trakheesi system shown on the ad. In the UAE, Federal Decree-Law No. 55 of 2023 on Media Regulation governs content, enforced by the National Media Authority, which replaced the UAE Media Council in December 2025: respect for religion, the state and national identity, public morals and the Arabic language in public advertising; no alcohol, tobacco or gambling advertising; an advertiser permit, mandatory from 31 January 2026 for anyone in the UAE posting paid or unpaid promotional content, free for three years to citizens and residents who registered by that date and AED 1,000 a year from year four, with a three-month visitor permit at AED 500 through a licensed agency and fines up to AED 10,000 for posting without one (Pinsent Masons, read October 2026). The house checks that every creator in a Gulf campaign holds a current permit before the brief goes out. Ramadan, National Day and modesty norms are craft matters in 19-campaigns-and-creative-direction.md, section 9.4; here the rule is that the house checks every Gulf execution against the National Media Authority's standards before the client does.

### 14.11 Bribery, gifts and commissions

India. The Prevention of Corruption Act 1988, as amended in 2018, makes a commercial organisation liable when anyone acting for it gives an undue advantage to a public servant, with adequate procedures as the only defence (section 9); that reaches public-sector undertakings, government tourism boards and development authorities, all of which buy premium work. Private kickbacks fall outside that Act, and they still breach the client's code of conduct and end the relationship when found. The UK Bribery Act 2010 (section 7, the failure-to-prevent offence) reaches work for UK-linked clients. The UAE Crimes and Penalties Law (Federal Decree-Law No. 31 of 2021) criminalises bribery in the private sector as well as the public (verify the articles). House rules: festival gifts to clients only under a value cap (for example Rs 5,000 or AED 250, house planning) and logged; no commission or referral fee to any client employee, in cash or kind; a vendor kickback to house staff is grounds for dismissal, and the vendor leaves the database (7.9). The reason: Diwali and Eid gifting is a real custom in both markets, and a written cap lets the house observe it without a question being asked.

### 14.12 Promotions and contests

India: lotteries are regulated by the states under the Lotteries (Regulation) Act 1998, and a pure-chance draw that needs a purchase or an entry fee can amount to an unauthorised lottery, so the house avoids entry fees, picks winners on a judged skill criterion, and publishes terms and conditions with the post. Prize winnings above Rs 10,000 per transaction attract TDS at 30 percent under section 194B (threshold set per transaction by the Finance Act 2025; verify the reference under the 2025 Act), which the brand deducts before the prize is handed over, non-cash prizes included. UAE: promotions and raffles in Dubai need a permit from the Department of Economy and Tourism, and in Abu Dhabi from its Department of Economic Development (verify each). Instagram's promotion guidelines require the brand to state that the promotion is not sponsored, endorsed or administered by Instagram. Entry data is personal data under the DPDP Act (14.8). The reason: retainer accounts run giveaways often, and a giveaway run as an illegal lottery is the brand's liability with the house's name on the post.

### 15.4 Usage rights pricing factors

Start from a base usage fee for one year, one country, organic digital and social, no exclusivity, the asset as one of a set: for a photographer, 50 to 100 percent of the day fee; for talent, 50 to 100 percent of the shoot fee (section 3.7). Add the uplifts below as percentages of that base. They are house planning figures: no published India or Gulf standard for photography or talent usage exists, so each is confirmed in negotiation.

| Factor | Base | Uplift on the base usage fee (about) | Why it costs more |
|---|---|---|---|
| Term | 1 year | 2 years +50 to 75; 3 years +100; 5 years +150 to 200; perpetuity +200 to 300 | Each year removes a renewal the artist would otherwise sell |
| Territory | One country, India or UAE | GCC +30 to 50; India plus GCC +50 to 80; worldwide +100 to 200 | Wider reach, more conflict with the artist's other clients |
| Media | Organic digital and social | Paid digital +20 to 50; print and OOH +30 to 50; TV and cinema +100; all media +150 to 200 | Paid and broadcast reach multiply exposure |
| Exclusivity | None | Category exclusivity for the term +25 to 100 | The talent or photographer cannot work for a competitor |
| Prominence | One of a set | Hero key visual or face of the campaign +50 to 100 | The asset carries the campaign |
| Quantity | One image or one spot | Sets priced per image with a volume reduction agreed up front | Volume is cheaper per unit to administer |
| Creator paid use (base: the creator's fee for one organic post) | One organic post | Brand organic reuse +10 to 25 per three months; paid amplification or whitelisting +20 to 50 per 30 days; raw footage priced separately | Paid reach spends the creator's credibility on the brand's media (3.7) |
| AI training and synthetic reuse | Excluded | Licensed separately, if at all; likeness and voice need written consent | Sections 4.9 and 14.6 |

Worked example: a model's shoot fee is Rs 50,000 and the base usage is set at 50 percent, Rs 25,000. The client wants two years (+60), India plus GCC (+60), paid digital (+40) and OOH (+40): +200 percent, so usage is Rs 75,000, or 150 percent of the shoot fee, and the delivery note records the expiry date.

## Terms introduced

- ACD (associate creative director): day-to-day creative lead of a team of two to six and the first sign-off before the CD.
- AD (art director): owner of the visual idea: composition, image, type and layout.
- Adex: advertising expenditure, the total spent on advertising media in a market in a year.
- Advertiser permit: the UAE permit, mandatory since 31 January 2026, for anyone in the UAE posting paid or unpaid promotional content.
- AGI (agency gross income): revenue after pass-through costs, the denominator for the house's financial ratios.
- AMEC: the International Association for Measurement and Evaluation of Communication, author of the Barcelona Principles.
- ANA: Association of National Advertisers, the US client body whose surveys track in-housing.
- ASCI (Advertising Standards Council of India): India's self-regulatory advertising body and its code.
- Assignment: a written transfer of copyright ownership from the creator to another party.
- ATA Carnet: an international customs document for temporary duty-free import of goods such as camera kit.
- AVE (advertising value equivalent): valuing press coverage at the price of the same space bought as advertising, a method the Barcelona Principles reject.
- Barcelona Principles: AMEC's principles for measuring communication by outcomes.
- Blended rate: one hourly rate averaged across a team's roles, used for retainers and sanity checks.
- Brand portal: the living online guidelines site holding a brand's rules and downloadable assets.
- Bundled production fee: one production price that includes the house's margin, with vendor costs not itemised.
- Burn report: the Monday report for each live job of budget hours, hours burnt, percent complete and forecast hours at completion.
- Buyout: a lump-sum purchase of broad or unlimited usage of an asset.
- Calibration session: a monthly exercise in which the team scores work independently, then compares, to align the quality scale.
- Call sheet: the document issued the evening before a shoot with times, locations, contacts, weather, nearest hospital, shot list and conduct contact.
- CCO (chief creative officer): owner of the creative standard and reputation of the whole house.
- CCPA (Central Consumer Protection Authority): India's statutory regulator of misleading advertisements under the Consumer Protection Act 2019.
- CD (creative director): owner of an account group's work from brief to craft sign-off.
- CDN (content delivery network): servers that cache and deliver a site's files close to each visitor.
- CGI (computer-generated imagery): images and film made in 3D software.
- Change order: a written, priced addition or alteration to scope, signed before the work starts.
- Chemistry meeting: a 60 to 90 minute meeting that tests the fit between client and house before any paid work.
- CMO (chief marketing officer): the client's senior marketing decision maker.
- CMS (content management system): the software through which a site's content is edited and published.
- Contract for service: an engagement of an independent contractor; under Indian law the freelancer keeps copyright unless it is assigned.
- Contract of service: employment; the employer owns work made in its course unless agreed otherwise.
- Critical path: the chain of dependent tasks whose total duration sets the end date.
- CRM (customer relationship management): the client's system of customer records, used in campaigns.
- CTC (cost to company): the Indian annual pay figure including employer provident fund and gratuity accrual.
- Cue sheet: the record of every music cue in a film with timings, composers and publishers, used for rights reporting.
- D2C (direct to consumer): brands that sell through their own channels.
- DAM (digital asset management): a searchable, permissioned library of approved assets with metadata and usage terms.
- De minimis rule: the UAE cap on a Qualifying Free Zone Person's non-qualifying revenue at 5 percent of revenue or AED 5 million, whichever is lower.
- Decoupling: separating production from origination and awarding it to a production specialist at lower rates.
- Deemed acceptance: a clause that treats silence for a set period after delivery as acceptance.
- Definition of done: the checklist a ticket must pass to count as complete.
- Design engineer: a designer who also builds the designed thing in production code.
- Design ops and creative ops: the function that owns tools, files, naming, DAM, review tooling, utilisation reporting and process.
- DIFC (Dubai International Financial Centre) and ADGM (Abu Dhabi Global Market): financial free zones with their own English-language common-law courts.
- DP (director of photography): the person responsible for light and camera on a shoot.
- DPA (data processing agreement): the contract that sets how a processor handles a controller's personal data.
- DSO (days sales outstanding): receivables divided by daily revenue; how long the house waits to be paid.
- DTAA (double taxation avoidance agreement): a treaty that prevents the same income being taxed twice across two countries.
- ECD (executive creative director): owner of creative output across a group of accounts or an office.
- Effective hourly rate: a job's fee divided by the hours logged on it.
- Emiratisation: the UAE federal quota for UAE nationals in private-sector jobs, run by MOHRE through the Nafis programme.
- Equalisation levy: India's 6 percent levy on online advertising bought from non-residents, abolished from 1 April 2025.
- Etimad: Saudi Arabia's government procurement platform.
- EULA (end user licence agreement): a foundry's or software maker's licence terms for using its product.
- Expected value: probability of winning multiplied by first-year gross margin, minus pitch cost.
- Export of services: a supply to a client outside the country that meets the statutory conditions and is zero-rated for GST or VAT.
- FIRC or FIRA: the bank's certificate or advice proving receipt of foreign currency against an invoice.
- FMCG (fast-moving consumer goods): packaged goods sold quickly at low cost, the networks' core retainer category.
- Force majeure: events outside either party's control that suspend contract obligations.
- FTE (full-time equivalent): one person's full working capacity, the unit of a retainer staffing plan.
- Gate: an approval point with a named approver, a written approval and a date.
- GCC (Gulf Cooperation Council): Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain and Oman.
- GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation): the EU's data protection law, which follows EU data subjects.
- GeM (Government e-Marketplace): India's online portal for central government purchasing.
- Global usage matrix: a head office's record of the term, territory and media licensed for each element of a global campaign.
- Gross margin: fee revenue minus the direct cost of delivery, divided by fee revenue.
- GST (goods and services tax): India's indirect tax, 18 percent on agency services.
- Handling: a fee, usually about 10 percent, for managing a third-party cost the client pays directly.
- Holding statement: a short prepared response for the first hours of a controversy.
- HUF (Hindu undivided family): a family unit taxed as a separate person under Indian income tax.
- IA (information architecture): the structure and labelling of a site's content.
- ICV (In-Country Value): the UAE certificate scoring a supplier's local spend, used in federal and Abu Dhabi procurement.
- IGST (integrated GST): GST on inter-state and cross-border supplies, including imported services under reverse charge.
- In-housing: a client building its own creative team to replace or reduce agency spend.
- Input tax credit: GST paid on purchases that is offset against GST charged.
- INS accreditation: the Indian Newspaper Society's accreditation that gives an agency credit with member publishers.
- IPRS and PPL: India's collecting societies for musical compositions (Indian Performing Right Society) and sound recordings (Phonographic Performance Limited).
- IRN (invoice reference number): the identifier India's GST e-invoice system assigns to each invoice.
- ISO/IEC 27001 and SOC 2: the international information security management standard and the US service-organisation controls audit.
- Kill fee: the amount payable if the client cancels after work begins.
- Knock-out search: a quick search for identical or near-identical marks before a name is presented.
- KYC (know your customer): identity checks required in payments and financial products.
- Labour codes: India's four consolidated labour laws on wages, industrial relations, social security and workplace safety, in force since 21 November 2025.
- Licence: permission to use a work while the creator keeps ownership.
- Line producer: the person who runs the on-the-ground execution of a shoot: calls, permits, locations, transport, catering.
- Loaded cost: the hourly cost of a person including statutory costs, benefits and a share of overhead.
- LPO (local purchase order): the UAE purchase order without which an invoice is not paid.
- LTO (Linear Tape-Open): archival magnetic tape used for long-term storage of camera originals.
- LUT (letter of undertaking): the annual GST filing that lets an Indian business export services without paying GST.
- Master licence: permission from the owner of a sound recording to use that recording.
- Mawthooq: Saudi Arabia's licence for influencers who advertise.
- MENA (Middle East and North Africa): the region the Dubai network offices serve.
- Moral rights: the author's rights of paternity and integrity, which survive assignment.
- MSA (master services agreement): the umbrella contract that sets legal terms once so each SOW can be short.
- MSMED Act (Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development Act 2006): the Indian law capping a buyer's payment period to registered micro and small enterprises at 45 days.
- NDA (non-disclosure agreement): a contract that binds the parties to confidentiality.
- Nice classes: the international classification of goods and services used for trademark filing.
- Nitaqat: Saudi Arabia's Saudisation bands, which set quotas for Saudi nationals in private-sector jobs.
- NOC (no-objection certificate): an authority's written clearance, such as a fire NOC for an event.
- OOH (out of home): advertising in public space: billboards, transit, street furniture and digital screens.
- Overhead: every cost that is not a billable person: rent, software, admin, new business, insurance.
- P&L (profit and loss account): the record of revenue, costs and profit for a job, an event or the firm.
- Paid discovery sprint: a two to three week paid engagement that replaces a speculative creative pitch.
- PAN (permanent account number): the Indian tax identifier against which TDS is credited.
- Phantom equity: a cash bonus that tracks the value of shares without issuing them.
- Picture lock: the point after which an edit's timing no longer changes.
- PINT AE: the structured e-invoice format of the UAE eInvoicing Programme.
- Pitch fee: a fee paid for pitch creative, credited against fees on award.
- Pitch theatre: the staged elements of a pitch: dressed room, printed books, mock-ups, film, prototype.
- Pitch to learn: a lean, hour-capped pitch entered to understand a category, with the learning written up.
- Pitch to win: a fully resourced, theatrical pitch entered to win the account.
- PO (purchase order): the client's written commitment to pay for defined work, issued before work starts.
- POSH Act: India's Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act 2013, which requires an Internal Committee in offices of 10 or more.
- PPM (pre-production meeting): the meeting at which boards, cast, locations, wardrobe and schedule are approved before a shoot.
- PR (public relations): earned media, press office and reputation work.
- Professional indemnity: insurance against claims of error or omission in professional work.
- Public liability: insurance against injury or damage to third parties.
- QA (quality assurance): checking a deliverable against its specification before release.
- QBR (quarterly business review): a quarterly meeting with the decider on results, learning, roadmap and relationship health.

- RACI: a matrix naming who is responsible, accountable, consulted and informed for each deliverable.
- RAG: a red, amber or green status flag in a report.
- Rate card: the published hourly or daily price per role and seniority.
- Realisation: fee invoiced divided by hours logged multiplied by the rate-card rate.
- Receivables ageing: unpaid invoices grouped by days outstanding.
- Retainer: a fixed monthly fee for an agreed scope or capacity.
- Revenue per head: annual fee revenue, excluding pass-through costs, divided by total headcount.
- Rework rate: hours spent after client approval divided by total hours.
- RFI (request for information): a buyer's request for credentials, size, clients, process and finances.
- RFP (request for proposal): a buyer's request for scope and price, sometimes with creative.
- RHQ (regional headquarters) programme: the Saudi rule, in force since 1 January 2024, tying government contracts to an in-Kingdom regional headquarters.
- Risk register: a per-project list of risks with likelihood, impact, owner, mitigation and trigger.
- Round: one consolidated set of client feedback on one presented stage, answered with one revised presentation.
- Run of show: the minute-by-minute script of an event, with cues and owners.
- Rush surcharge: a premium of 25 to 50 percent for compressing a timeline below the house minimum.
- SAC (services accounting code): the GST classification code for services; agency services sit under heading 9983.
- Scope creep: the accumulation of small unpriced additions to agreed scope.
- Sensitivity review: a pre-launch read by someone outside the team for religious, caste, regional, gender and national sensitivities.
- SGC (synthetically generated content): advertising content artificially or algorithmically created or materially altered to appear real.
- SKU (stock keeping unit): one distinct product variant in a range.
- Small Business Relief: the UAE election that treats a resident business with revenue of AED 3 million or less as having no taxable income.
- SOW (statement of work): the contract-grade description of one engagement under an MSA.
- Spark Ads: TikTok paid ads run from an organic post, a creator's included.
- Spec work: creative work done on speculation, without payment, usually in a pitch.
- SSO (single sign-on): one identity login for every tool, the base of access control.
- Stakeholder map: the written record of decider, approvers, influencers, gatekeepers and users on an account.
- Sync licence (synchronisation licence): permission from the publisher to set a musical composition to picture.
- TDS (tax deducted at source): Indian income tax withheld by the payer and credited to the payee's PAN.
- Three-option pricing: presenting a lean, a recommended and a premium option to anchor the fee.
- Time-and-materials: billing the hours actually worked at rate card.
- Traffic: the function that routes work through the studio and spots collisions between jobs.
- TReDS (Trade Receivables Discounting System): RBI-regulated platforms on which MSME invoices to large buyers are discounted.
- Trunk-based development: a version control practice in which everyone merges small changes into one main branch daily.
- Udyam registration: India's online registration of micro, small and medium enterprises, the key to MSMED Act protection.
- UGC (user-generated content): material about or for a brand made by its customers or the public.
- Usage right: permission to use a created asset for a defined term, territory and set of media.
- Utilisation: billable hours divided by available hours.
- UX (user experience) and UI (user interface): the flow and the screens of a digital product.
- Value-based pricing: setting the fee from the value of the outcome to the client, agreed before scope is detailed.
- VAT (value added tax): 5 percent in the UAE and 15 percent in Saudi Arabia.
- VFX (visual effects): digital work added to filmed footage in post-production.
- Weather day: a day of crew and kit held in reserve on an exterior shoot, quoted at about 50 percent of crew fees.
- Whitelisting: the brand running paid ads through a creator's handle.
- Win rate: pitches won divided by pitches entered, tracked on qualified pitches.
- Work authorisation: a signed one-page permission to start phase one, capped at the first milestone, before the full contract clears.
- WPS (Wage Protection System): the UAE system through which private-sector salaries must be paid.
- Wrap report: the same-day record of what was shot, missed, changed and spent.
- Write-off: hours on a job that the house will not bill, logged with a reason code.
- Yield per person: available hours multiplied by utilisation, realisation and rate.
