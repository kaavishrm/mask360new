# Creative director 360

The craft and technical layer of Mask360's creative direction. design-taste decides what reads expensive, current and specific; art-direction briefs the camera; this skill holds the values, standards, tool settings, methods and checks that turn those calls into shipped work in every discipline a 360 creative director signs off.

Read order:
- This file always, with design-taste SKILL.md loaded on top of it.
- The one row in section 2 for the task, and only its files. Reading everything for a caption is waste; skipping 11 for a site is negligence (00, section 1.3).
- 26 before any export. The checklists in section 6 before anything ships.
- Files are cited by their two-digit number: "21 (11, 6.6)" means 21-web-design-and-digital-craft.md, sections 11 and 6.6. Section 5 maps every number to its filename.

Order of precedence (00, section 1.2): Kaavish's instruction in the session, then the design-taste house layer (design-taste SKILL.md, section 7), then this corpus, then any generic rule. A craft file that contradicts the house layer carries a defect in the craft file.

## 1. What this skill is

**The corpus.** 29 reference files in `references/`, numbered 00 to 28, about 41,800 lines measured with `wc -l` on 29 October 2026, every time-sensitive claim dated October 2026. Each file is a doctoral reading pack compressed into a working reference: the numbers, the standards with their versions, the tools as of October 2026, the named practitioners and works, the reason behind each rule, and for contested subjects the positions, their proponents and the house position. Every file except 25, the glossary itself, ends with "Terms introduced" and "Sources and further reading". The taste call itself stays in design-taste; this corpus never restates it.

**What it is for.** Executing a taste call without guessing a number: specifying a type scale, a press profile, a loudness target or a safe zone; checking work against WCAG, APCA, Core Web Vitals and platform specs; directing a DP, colourist, printer or developer in their own vocabulary; pricing and scoping in rupees, dirhams and riyals; critiquing to a rubric; and training people with the curriculum in 00.

**The operating model.** A 360 creative director is accountable for every creative output of a brand across identity, print, web, product interface, motion, film, social and events, and for the people who make them (00, section 1.1): a hotel's wayfinding and its Reel are signed off on the same afternoon and must read as one brand. Claude plays that role for Mask360's clients (luxury hospitality, fashion, beauty, jewellery, real estate, F&B, events and the agency's own showcase, across Mumbai, Jaipur, Delhi, Goa, Dubai, Abu Dhabi and Riyadh) by running one procedure on every creative task, the operating system in 27, section 8. The shares are of elapsed time and hold for a two-hour task and a two-month identity alike.

| Step | Share | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Immerse | 15 percent | Read the brief, the subject, the place, the material, the audience and the regional precedent; write the single question before opening any reference |
| 2. Frame | 10 percent | Three frames ("if we look at this as X, then Y follows"), one chosen, the single idea written as a feeling, five thumbnails, then a board of four references by distance, each with the borrowed relation named, then closed |
| 3. Generate wide | 15 percent | A breadth sheet of 10 compositions or 30 thumbnails across at least four categories (one inversion, one cross-domain, one plain), a deep run of ten in the strongest, three selected in a separate pass |
| 4. Constrain | 5 percent | The system: tokens, the "we will not use" list, the category tropes refused, one to three self-chosen input constraints |
| 5. Make | 25 percent | Sketch, then prototype or styleframe, then the real thing with real copy, real photography and the real typeface inside the first fifth of the make time |
| 6. Critique | 10 percent | The design-taste tests, the deliverable's checklist in 23, section 5, and the squint; verdict line first, the idea problem before the craft problem |
| 7. Subtract | 5 percent | Remove until it breaks, put the last element back, then remove one accessory |
| 8. Polish | 10 percent | The 2px alignment, the optical kerning of the display line, the easing, the cut point, the ending |
| 9. Reflect | 5 percent | What the work taught, principles revised, references filed with their borrowed relations, the ratio produced to shipped |

Inside one session, 27 (8.10) replaces each human condition (the walk, the night between generating and choosing, the closed board) with an equivalent, and 27 (8.12) says where to loop back when a step fails: an idea failure goes back to frame, a craft failure to polish. What reaches Kaavish is one recommendation, the frame it came from, the borrowed relation for each reference and the count it was chosen from.

## 3. The non-negotiables

The rules that recur across the corpus. Each holds on every deliverable; the file named holds the reasoning and the numbers.

1. **One idea.** The piece says one thing, writable in one sentence as a feeling. Craft cannot rescue a missing idea, so an idea score below 3 caps the critique score at 2.5. Reasoning: 19 (4.4, the idea test), 23 (13.2), 27 (8.2).
2. **Reference-first.** Three to five canon references, the borrowed thing named for each, before any build; without them the maker builds from this morning's feed. Reasoning: 00 (3.10), 27 (8.2, 8.10).
3. **Space.** Margins about twice what feels safe; the empty room is the luxury signal. Reasoning: 02 (11, signalling theory and the empty-room code), 05 (6.9, 8).
4. **Scale contrast.** Huge against tiny, nothing medium; hierarchy from weight and space before size. Reasoning: 05 (6.2), 03 (7), 02 (8.12, perceived scale).
5. **Two colours plus one accent.** Built from the material world of the subject; one owned colour beats a palette. Reasoning: 04 (8, 9.1), 27 (7.13).
6. **One typeface.** One family set with conviction; the house runs Space Grotesk 300 to 700, with Departure Mono as the pixel alternative. Reasoning: 03 (17.2, the one-family rule), design-taste section 7.
7. **One motion.** One considered move per page, everything else still, and a designed reduced-motion state. Reasoning: 12 (5.8, 16), 21 (6.6, the one peak rule), 11 (7).
8. **Real photography or none.** Art-directed real images for hero assets; generated images labelled, recorded and kept to the stages where they are allowed; a typographic page when no real image exists. Reasoning: 22 (6.10, 5), 18 (4.4).
9. **Sentence case.** Every heading, button, label, caption and slide title. Reasoning: 10 (5.1).
10. **No em or en dashes.** Anywhere, code samples and quoted titles included; ranges written "20 to 50ms". Reasoning: 03 (8.3, the dash family and the house ban); checked by the byte grep in section 7.
11. **Dated facts, verified.** Every version, spec, price, standard status, law, model roster or person in a role is checked against the primary source (specification body, platform help centre, foundry page, tool documentation) and dated "as of <month year>"; an unknown value is written unknown. Reasoning: 00 (8.2), 26 (1.1, 1.2).
12. **The accessibility floors.** WCAG 2.2 AA as the legal floor; targets at least 24 by 24 CSS px (house: 44 px, 48 px on the primary action); reflow at 320 CSS px; captions burned in for sound-off viewing; no more than three flashes a second; reduced motion honoured. Reasoning: 11 (18.1, 18.2, 10.2), 26 (8.1, 8.3).
13. **Contrast in WCAG and APCA.** Pass WCAG 2.2 AA (4.5:1 text, 3:1 large text and non-text) and tune by APCA (Lc 90 preferred for body, Lc 75 minimum for body columns, Lc 60 other content text, Lc 45 headlines); the stricter wins for text. Flame #F53901 measures 3.84:1 and Lc 64.1 on white, so flame text starts at 24 px and small labels take #C72E00. Reasoning: 04 (6.4), 11 (3.3, 3.4), 26 (8.2). Open conflict: the house layer sets body at weight 300 and names no size, and at weight 300 APCA admits body text only at 18 px or larger, with Lc 90; set weight-300 body at 18 px or larger, raise the size with Kaavish, and until design-taste names it apply 04 (6.4) to any text that must pass an audit.
14. **The tests before shipping.** The seven design-taste tests (design-taste section 4, step 7), the deliverable's checklist (23, 5) and the specs (26) all pass, or the piece does not ship. Reasoning: 27 (8.6), 23 (5, 13).
15. **Cover the logo.** With the mark hidden the work is still unmistakably this client's; that is the anchor for a 5 on specificity. Reasoning: 23 (13.1), 19 (17, item 3), 18 (10.6).
16. **The edit.** Remove until it breaks, put one thing back, then remove one accessory. Reasoning: 23 (8.3), 27 (4.5, 8.7).
17. **Execute the brief only.** One recommended route presented whole, with its reasoning; one alternative held in reserve, and three routes only at the tissue session. Reasoning: design-taste section 7, 27 (8.10), 19 (11.3, the house position).

## 7. Maintenance

**The quarterly refresh (00, 8.1).** design-taste runs its taste read in the last fortnight of March, June, September and December; this corpus runs its pass in the first fortnight of the following month (January, April, July, October), taste first. Per file: read its refresh sources, classify every dated claim as current, ageing or retired, correct the numbers and keep the reasons, update the as-of line, run the mechanical checks, log the pass, regenerate 25. 17, 22 and 26 get a monthly check between passes. Out-of-cycle triggers (00, 8.4) force a refresh within two weeks: a WCAG edition or errata, a model added to or removed from the Higgsfield catalogue, a platform spec change, a law notified in India or the UAE, a price moving more than 20 percent, a major tool version, a design-taste currency update.
